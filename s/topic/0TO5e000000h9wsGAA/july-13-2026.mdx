### **Resolved Bugs**

1. **Smarter HVAC and More Emergency Detection Improvements**

We've made several improvements to our Intake Settings emergency decision making.

**What was happening:** Air conditioning requests could occasionally match appliance rules that were intended to exclude HVAC equipment. Additionally, if a key piece of information specified in an intake rule—like the indoor temperature—was missing, Intake could incorrectly assume the issue wasn't an emergency instead of asking the resident.

<Check>
  **What's fixed:**

  - Air conditioners, furnaces, heaters, heat pumps, and thermostats are now correctly recognized as HVAC equipment.
  - Intake now asks residents for missing information whenever it could change the emergency outcome instead of making assumptions.
  - We've upgraded the underlying rule matching engine for improved accuracy across all intake scenarios.
</Check>

As always, your **custom Intake Settings** take priority if you've configured a different timeframe. **To know more about custom Intake Settings, visit:**

### **Key Ops Process Changes**

**Updated Emergency Guidance for Fire Hazards & Power Shutoffs**

We've expanded our emergency diagnosis guidance to better handle electrical hazards while continuing to respect each property's configured Intake Settings.

**What's changing:**

- Burning smells, sparking, smoke, or visible electrical damage now qualify as potential property damage/emergency hazard situations.
- Teams should always follow the property's Intake Settings when determining whether to dispatch immediately or escalate.
- A hazard is only considered resolved once the affected breaker or disconnect has been confirmed off.
- If electrical hazard signs occurred only in the past and are no longer present—with the breaker confirmed off—the issue should be handled according to the property's normal Intake Settings.

<Check>
  **What this means for you:**

  Electrical hazards are handled more consistently while ensuring your customized emergency policies continue to drive dispatch decisions.

  **Why this matters:**

  It improves resident safety, creates more consistent emergency handling, and reduces unnecessary emergency dispatches when a hazard has already been resolved.
</Check>
