Audience: Supervisors & Ops Trainer

# **What This Does**

Every 2 weeks, the system checks each associate's pace in a queue — actions completed per hour actually spent in that queue — against their own recent history. Not against other people, just against how they normally perform.

If someone's pace drops meaningfully and stays down, they're flagged for a possible task refresher and their supervisor gets a Slack DM.

| **Level** | **When it fires** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Recommendation | Pace has been low for 2 periods in a row (a soft heads-up) |
| Escalation | Low for 3\+ periods in a row, or their overall output is down too (a more serious signal) |

Guardrails built in: PTO weeks are skipped, and there's a cooldown so nobody gets pinged repeatedly for the same dip. Two separate rules protect anyone who's still learning: associates under 90 days at the company are excluded outright, and — independently — nobody gets flagged in a queue until they have 3 periods of real history in that exact queue (about 6 weeks of actually working it). So someone who's been here 2 years but just moved to Troubleshooter last week won't be flagged there until they've had a fair shot at learning it, even though they'd pass the 90-day company-tenure gate on day one.

# **Where the Data Comes From**

- Volumes and hours: your existing weekly productivity sheet — no change to that process.
- Hours in queue: derived from the platform's status logs (this is what makes the pace comparison accurate instead of just using total hours worked).
- Tenure: pulled automatically from the platform's account-creation date. This replaced the old Zoltar column, which had several #N/A values that were dropping real people from consideration — no manual upkeep needed going forward.

# **What You Get**

1. A Slack DM to the associate's supervisor at the moment a flag happens.
2. A row in the [<u>Refresher Log tab </u>](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1bC4NWuqf_cRZu0AsbsO4Osfn8QmfWaUDb5Edf1383J4/edit?gid=1047737466#gid=1047737466)of the productivity sheet, for every flag, permanently.
3. A [<u>Looker dashboard</u>](https://datastudio.google.com/u/0/reporting/a528e2c3-b73f-4c8d-ae8c-2eb4358aad3b/page/Q1m4F) for browsing, filtering, and reviewing flags over time.

All three read the exact same underlying data, so the dashboard, the log, and the DMs always agree.

## **Reading the Table**

Below the counters is the full list of flagged associates for the current filter, most recent first. The built-in note at the bottom of the dashboard explains each column, reproduced here:

<Frame>
  ![Task Refresher Signal SOP Google Docs (4)](/images/Task_Refresher_Signal_SOP---Google-Docs-\(4\).png "Task Refresher Signal SOP Google Docs (4)")

| **Column** | **Meaning** |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Current Pace** | How much this person is producing per hour in that queue right now. |
| **Normal Pace** | Their own usual pace, based on their last ~2 months in that queue — not compared to anyone else. |
| **Percentage of Normal** | Current pace as a share of their usual. Under 50% means a real drop. |
| **Recommendation** | An early, soft heads-up — their pace dipped for two periods in a row. |
| **Escalation** | More serious — the dip has held for 3\+ periods, or their overall output is down too. |

# **When You Get a Flag**

A Slack DM means the signal fired — it's not a verdict. Work through this before assuming a training gap:

1. Talk to the associate first. Ask if anything changed — schedule, workload mix, personal circumstances — before assuming it's a skill gap.
2. Check Current Pace vs. Normal Pace for that queue on the dashboard. A Recommendation is a soft signal (2 periods down); an Escalation is stronger (3\+ periods, or overall output down too). Weight your response accordingly.
3. Decide: if the signal is true\*\*, schedule the refresher.\*\* If context explains it, **no action needed** — it drops off on its own once pace recovers.
4. If a flag looks wrong, report it in #data-bug-report slack channel. Especially right now — the Emails-queue thresholds are still being refined, so a flag that doesn't make sense is worth flagging back rather than acting on.

# **Cadence**

The check runs every 2 weeks. 1 period = 2 weeks. Realistically, expect about one flag every couple of weeks, often none at all — this is designed to catch a real, sustained dip, not routine week-to-week noise.
