## 🧠 What This Process Is Trying to Solve

<Note>
*We want to prevent delays, unresolved issues, and confusion when Operations Associates or Troubleshooters reach the limits of their job scope. The goal is to ensure that customer requests are escalated promptly to the correct internal teams or specialists, so that issues are resolved efficiently and customers receive high-quality service—saving time, reducing frustration, and protecting Latchel’s reputation.*
</Note>

<Note>
### **⬅️**What is happening prior to this process?

Prior to Transferring a Work Order, the associate has followed Latchel standard procedures to advance the work order, but can no longer advance the Work Order or perform the actions requested by the customer.
</Note>

## ☎️Handling Requests for Supervisor/Manager Calls

<Note>
### When to Escalate

When a customer ***repeatedly ***expresses dissatisfaction, it often indicates that they feel their concerns are not being adequately addressed. This dissatisfaction could stem from ongoing issues, perceived lack of attention to their needs, or inadequate solutions provided in previous interactions. Associates are typically trained to handle a wide range of common issues, but there are situations ***where the complexity of a problem exceeds their decision-making authority***. In such cases, escalation is necessary to bring fresh eyes to the problem—someone with more authority or expertise who can provide a different approach or solution. The escalation process serves as a way to demonstrate to the customer that their issue is being taken seriously and that the company is committed to resolving it to their satisfaction.
</Note>

### Escalation Procedure

1. Identify your customer and follow the dissatisfied customer de-escalation steps before escalating the request.
  1. **For all customers, associates must follow Steps 1–4** outlined in https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/2706440268:
    1. Follow customer experience quality standards and customer tenets.
    2. Apolophrase the customer’s complaint.
    3. Provide a brief explanation of how the process or product is supposed to work.
    4. Ask the customer where we did not meet their expectations.
  2. **If the associate cannot assist further, or the customer still insists on speaking with a supervisor after Steps 1–4**
    1. Escalate to Operations Support Specialists through the appropriate Support channel per the relevant SOP.
    2. Acknowledge the customer:
***Say something like:**** "Thank you for sharing your concerns with me. I understand that you would like to speak with a supervisor/manager, and I want to assure you that I'm here to help. I'll escalate your request and make sure that someone with the appropriate authority will follow up and call you back. We appreciate your patience and are committed to resolving this issue for you."*
      1. Notify the Operations Support Specialist team by escalating the issue to the appropriate channel. Refer to https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/edit-v2/776044545#Escalating-to-Operations-Support-Specialists.
      2. **The associate's role ends here.** The Operations Support Specialist takes over and owns the interaction from this point forward, including any further escalation if needed.
  3. **For Operations Support Specialists:**
    1. **If the concern is resolved during the call, document the outcome and any actions taken.**
    2. **If the concern cannot be resolved during the call and the customer still insists on speaking with someone higher, the Operations Support Specialist will:**
      1. ** For Resident/Service Provider:**
        1. Escalate to a manager.
      2. **For Property Manager/Owner:**
        1. Submit a [Product Support ticket](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Escalate-to-Product-Support) so Customer Success/Product Support can review and follow up within one business day. This is handled by Support — not the associate.
  4. **The support’s role ends here.**

**⚠️ Weekend Supervisor Calls**

If a customer requests a supervisor/manager call on a weekend and no Operations Supervisor is available:

2. **Say something like:** *"I understand you'd like to speak with a supervisor. Unfortunately, there is no available escalation point at this time. I will make sure someone follows up with you by Monday. We appreciate your patience."*
  1. The Operations Support Specialist must create a task assigned to Ops Support task group for the supervisor call follow-up on Monday and monitor it until it is completed.
  2. The Operations Support Specialist must **post the request in the Operations Support channel and tag both supervisors** so they are aware of the pending Monday follow-up.
