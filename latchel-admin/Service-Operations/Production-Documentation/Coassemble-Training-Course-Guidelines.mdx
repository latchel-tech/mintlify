The purpose of this guide is to establish consistent quality standards for creating and updating training courses in Coassemble. Our objective is to streamline the course production process and ensure that our training content remains current, engaging, and effective. By following these guidelines, we can maintain a robust and standardized training program that fulfills the learning needs of our users and aligns with the strategic goals of our organization.

<Tip>
**👁️ Training Creation Vision**

We are moving towards offering shorter, single-topic training courses instead of extensive, multi-topic ones. Our aim is to provide a course for each SOP, or group of SOPs when relevant, to clearly outline the overall process and give a 'bigger picture' understanding of the SOP through the training course with interactive activities that provide learners the opportunity to exercise what they learn.
</Tip>

## Training Content Quality Standards

Following this checklist when you're creating or revising a Coassemble Training course will ensure that our training content remains current, engaging, and effective.

### Course Objectives

Every course should have a clear set of objectives defined upfront. These should align with the overall goals of the course and the skills we want the learners to acquire.

Defining course objectives helps the learner get an overview of what will be seen during the training course while helping the trainer stay on track with the goals of the course. Follow these steps to create a clear "Course Overview":

- **Review the Course Material:** Examine all the content to be included in the course. From this, determine the key takeaways for the learners — these form your objectives. Aim to outline how the course will benefit the learner or influence their understanding.
- **Write a Course Overview:** Utilizing your defined objectives, compose a succinct paragraph summarizing the course. This should offer a high-level glimpse into the course content.
- **Insert Overview in the Course:** Navigate to the "Course Overview" page and place the drafted paragraph in the "Course Overview" section, located at the bottom left.

### Content Quality

The courses should contain factually correct, up-to-date, and comprehensive material. Expert peer review of course content can help maintain high standards.

When providing training, it is essential to ensure our information is substantiated, with SOPs referenced and linked every time their content is explained.

<Warning>
Every time you link something in Coassemble, make it clear by changing the hyperlink text color to blue.
</Warning>

Creating new or revising existing training courses should involve a peer review from knowledgeable associates, coaches, or others well-versed in the course's topic. This process guarantees course comprehensiveness and ensures alignment with our quality standards.

If you're uncertain about any process or topic you're covering in a course, don't hesitate to schedule a meeting with experienced colleagues for clarity.

To maintain the relevance and accuracy of our training courses, they will undergo review every 3 months. This policy ensures the courses remain continuously updated and accurate.

### Course Length

Establish a time frame for course completion depending on the complexity of the topic. Too long might seem overwhelming and too short might give the impression it lacks depth.

Determining the length of a course can be challenging due to varying complexity levels. As a general rule, aim to limit the average course length to **20 minutes**. For longer, more complex courses, it's important to break down the information into manageable parts. Ideally, include an engaging or relaxing activity every 20 minutes to prevent learner overload and to maintain attention levels.

To estimate the total course length, sum up the runtime of all videos, estimate the reading time for all written content using a reading time calculator, and predict the duration of assessments and interactive activities based on their complexity.

Once you've calculated your estimated course length, update it in the course overview page. This will provide learners an expected completion time and will help them adapt to it.

### Practical Application

Courses should facilitate the application of knowledge, with clear use cases or real-world examples or practices when possible.

Ensure learners not only grasp the course material but also understand its application in their daily work. The course closing should highlight the real-world relevance of the learned concepts. For instance, a course introducing a new tool should conclude with a demo of its usage. Likewise, a course covering a broad subject can incorporate practical examples or engaging exercises pertinent to their daily tasks. Establishing such connections accelerates the retention of new information and links it to the learners' work context.

### Assessments

Inclusion of tests, quizzes, or projects to evaluate the comprehension of the learners. They also provide a tangible goal for learners.

All courses should include an assessment at the end. We have a template for creating assessments. Follow the steps below to create an assessment for your training course.

1. Create multiple response questions from the information in your course, making sure the questions are relevant to the course objectives and the topics viewed during training. Add as many questions as your course requires. Ideally aim to keep it between 5–10 questions but don't shy away from adding more if needed.

2. Go to the **"Training Assessment"** Typeform.

3. Go straight to question **#3** and click on **"Edit choices"**.

<Frame>
  ![Typeform - Edit choices on question 3](/images/TC-13.png)
</Frame>

4. In the pop-up screen, insert a new line and write the name of your course. *(This only applies to new courses, or updated courses that did not previously have an assessment — do not duplicate the course name here.)* Click **"Save choices"**.

<Frame>
  ![Typeform - Save choices pop-up](/images/TC-14.png)
</Frame>

5. Click on the **"+"** button at the top left. From here, create a new **Question Group**. This will create a group of questions for your course assessment that will help separate them from other courses.

<Frame>
  ![Typeform - Create new Question Group](/images/TC-15.png)
</Frame>

6. In the question group title page, leave your course name followed by **"-- Let's put your knowledge to the test!"**

<Frame>
  ![Typeform - Question Group title format](/images/TC-16.png)
</Frame>

7. Click on the **"+"** sign and select the **"Multiple Choice"** option. Repeat this process as many times as needed until you add all the questions you created in step 1.

<Frame>
  ![Typeform - Add Multiple Choice questions](/images/TC-17.png)
</Frame>

8. Make all questions required by checking the **"Required"** section at the middle right.

<Frame>
  ![Typeform - Make questions required](/images/TC-18.png)
</Frame>

9. Go to the **"Logic"** section at the top right.

<Frame>
  ![Typeform - Logic section](/images/TC-19.png)
</Frame>

10. From here, click on **"Branching & Calculation"**.

<Frame>
  ![Typeform - Branching & Calculation](/images/TC-20.png)
</Frame>

11. Go to question **#3** and click on **"+ Add rule"**.

<Frame>
  ![Typeform - Add rule on question 3](/images/TC-21.png)
</Frame>

12. Select the following Logic: *If Question 3 is "Your Training Course", Go to: Your Training Course Question Group.* This will make learners only see the questions in your question group when they select your training course.

<Frame>
  ![Typeform - Logic rule configuration](/images/TC-22.gif)
</Frame>

13. The next step is to add value to your questions. Take the amount of questions you have and divide it between 100. This will be the value you'll be assigning to each question.

    *Example: If you have 5 questions, you'll divide 100/5=20. So each question will be equivalent to 20 points.*

    In Typeform, decimals in scoring aren't accepted. If you end up with a decimal when dividing your total score by the number of questions, round down the result. Then, allocate this rounded score to all but one question.

    For example, if you have 6 questions, you might think to give each question a score of 16.67 (100 divided by 6). But since decimals aren't accepted, round down to 16. Multiply 16 by 5 (one less than your total number of questions) to get 80, then allocate the leftover (20 points in this case) to your remaining question. So, 5 of your questions will be worth 16 points each, and 1 question will be worth 20 points, giving you a total of 100 points.

    Once you've defined the value of each of your questions, go back to **"Branching & Calculations"**. From here, find your group of questions and start giving each question a value. To do this, follow this logic for all your questions: **If:** Your Question, **Is:** The correct response, **Add:** Value, **To:** Score.

</Frame>

## Revisiting Existing Training

To maintain the relevance and accuracy of our training courses, they will undergo review every 3 months. This policy ensures the courses remain continuously updated and accurate.

### Step 1: Enroll in the Training Course

The first step is to open Coassemble and click on the course you want to review.

1. Click the share button at the top left.
2. Copy the **"Enroll with a link"** option.
3. Paste the link in a new tab, and that's it! You're now enrolled.

### Step 2: Take the Course

Once you're enrolled in the course, begin taking it as a learner and take notes on any outdated content or content that does not meet our training quality standards.

If the course you're taking is full of outdated information, consider building a new course for the topic being covered.

### Step 3: Compare and Align with SOPs

As you're going through the course, ensure you're linking the information presented to our SOPs. Keep track of the SOPs referenced in the course as we will include these in our "Resources" page.

### Step 4: Create or Update the Training Document

The course document is an informal document that shows the skeleton of the course and any important notes taken by the trainer. It doesn't necessarily need to have all the information in the course, but should cover all the topics viewed in it and should serve as a place to document any updates to the course.

- Check whether a document for the training course is available in the Coassemble Course Tracker.
- If yes, read through and spot information that is outdated or not aligned, while also documenting any updates you make to the training course.
- If there's no existing document, create a new one using the information from the course you've completed and the notes you've made. Link it in the corresponding course space in the Coassemble Course Tracker.
- If the existing document is mostly obsolete, consider re-writing it entirely.
- If only some parts are outdated, then update those sections accordingly.

### Step 5: Ensure Quality

As you're updating the training course content, ensure you're following our **"Training Content Quality Standards"** to make sure we provide quality training courses.

### Step 6: Create or Review Assessment

Check if the course has an assessment. If it does, ensure the assessment aligns with the course content.

If there's no assessment or if the existing one doesn't align with the updated information, create a new one using the steps in the **"Training Content Quality Standards"** SOP.

### Step 7: Link to SOPs

After the course content and assessment are up-to-date, ensure all relevant SOPs are linked in the **"Resources"** module at the end of the document.

### Step 8: Final Review

Do a final pass on the training course content, assessment, and SOP links to ensure everything aligns and is up to standards before publishing the updated course.
