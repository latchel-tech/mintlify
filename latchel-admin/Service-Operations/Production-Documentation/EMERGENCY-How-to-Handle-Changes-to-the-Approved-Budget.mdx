## 🧠 What This Process Is Trying to Solve

<Note>
We want to prevent unauthorized spending and miscommunication when a service provider requests additional funds beyond the original approved budget during an emergency work order. The goal is to ensure all budget changes are promptly reviewed, properly documented, and approved by the property manager or owner before any extra work is authorized—protecting customers from unexpected costs and helping Latchel maintain financial accountability and trust during urgent situations.
</Note>

<Note>
🎓**Training Content**

The contents of this page have been included in the **Budget Communication and Approval Process **Coassemble course.
</Note>

<Note>
### When do we provide estimates?

We will provide an estimate for property managers, but we cannot track, collect, or enforce for Preferred service providers.  If we receive the estimate, we'll pass it on to the Property Manager for approval. For LCN -- we do **NOT **do free estimates at all. We will not send a Service Provider to a job to collect an estimate. We will, however, send a Service Provider to a site to perform a diagnostic and provide an estimate. This will incur a service charge. We do not handle collecting multiple estimates and bids. That is project management that is outside the scope of Latchel's services.
</Note>

# Content

<Note>
### 🗂️Information

Each Work Order automatically has an "Approved Budget" and "Hard Budget" amount. Both these amounts are set by the Property Manager.

---

**⚠️ Automatic Budget Adjustment After Invoice Upload (System Update — June 2026)**  
Once an invoice is uploaded to a work order — whether by the Service Provider via their portal or by a Latchel associate — the system will **automatically reduce both the Approved Budget and the Hard Budget** by subtracting the invoice amount. This happens at the time of upload; the invoice does **not** need to be marked as paid first.

**What this means:**

- Any new estimate submitted after an invoice has been uploaded will be compared against the **remaining** budget, not the original amount.
- This prevents the system from auto-approving new estimates based on the full original budget when part of the work has already been invoiced.

**⚠️ Reminder:** When uploading an invoice, always ensure the file is properly **tagged as an invoice** in the system (not as an estimate or other document type) so the automatic budget adjustment triggers correctly.

**Example:** If the Approved Budget is $260 and an invoice for $260 is uploaded, the Approved Budget will automatically update to $0. If a Service Provider then submits a new estimate, it will be compared against the $0 remaining budget and will require PM approval.

---

**Emergency timing and automatic budget adjustments**

- For some emergency work orders, internal automations/action sets may **adjust the Approved Budget** based on the **scheduled appointment time**.
- If an emergency work order’s appointment is scheduled **more than 24 hours out**, the system may lower the soft emergency budget to a **non‑emergency soft limit**, even though the severity remains Emergency.
- When reviewing a Service Provider’s budget increase request, always:
  - Verify the **current appointment date and time**.
  - Keep in mind that a lower soft limit on an Emergency WO may reflect that the appointment is no longer within the emergency (same‑day / 24‑hour) window.
  - If the Service Provider is confused about the amount, follow the expectations in [**How to: Set expectations with Service Providers when dispatching**](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/2600337430) and then continue the budget approval process as outlined in this SOP.

**NOTE:** If an Owner or Property Manager requests that they want to approve **any amount before work is done** on a specific work order, **or do not want anything over $[X] approved without checking with them first** (especially when the Hard Budget Limit is higher), please refer to the **Frequently Asked Questions** section of this SOP.

When the Service Provider contacts Latchel to request approval for a higher monetary amount than the "Approved Budget", the Troubleshooting agent will review the amount for "Hard Budget". If the new cost exceeds the "Hard Budget" amount, the agent needs to work on handling an Over Hard Budget Work Order approval. This requires verbal or written approval from a responsible party within the Property Management company.

- Approval for "Over Hard Budgets" for Emergency Work Orders is most often given verbally or in written form after receiving confirmation from the Property Manager.
- Denial for "Over-Approved Budgets" for Emergency Work Orders is also given verbally or in written form by the Property Manager.
</Note>

<Warning>
Always consider all expenses/invoices reported by the Service Provider(s), such as initial service visits, past invoices, etc. The approval request should always consider all costs incurred throughout the life cycle of the WO.
</Warning>

<Warning>
Whenever a Service Provider sends multiple estimates for the same Work Order (for example: one estimate to *repair* an existing equipment and another to *replace* the same equipment), we will seek the Property Manager’s approval before proceeding, even if the estimates fall under the Hard Limit. Latchel will not decide on which estimate to choose; we will allow the Property Manager to make that decision. In such cases, follow the same procedure as when an estimate exceeds the hard limit.
</Warning>

<Warning>
Before calling escalation contacts for Property Damaging or Extreme habitability issues, please ensure you verify the current status of the issue to account for any changes in the situation.
</Warning>

## 📋 Step-by-Step Process

### **How to Handle Service Provider Visit Fees, Trip Charges, Diagnostic Fees**

<Note>
### What is a Visit Fee/Trip Charge/Diagnostic Fee?

- Visit Fee/Trip Charge - (n.) Minimum fee when the Service Provider is dispatched, even if they do not perform any work.
- Diagnostic Fee - (n.) Fee the Service Provider charges to assess the issue. They may charge this fee even though they can't complete the work.
</Note>

1. Service providers may charge visit fees, trip charges, and diagnostic fees.
  1. Each service provider and trade treats these fees differently. Property Managers are responsible for paying any visit fees, trip charges, or diagnostic fees.
  2. If a visit fee is outrageous, then PMs have a dispute process that they can go through. Please refer tohttps://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/795967495.
2. The PM only pays what the Service Provider charges.
  1. If the Service Provider doesn't charge a visit fee,  the PM pays no visit fee. 
  2. The Property Manager pays visit fees via the normal invoice feature in our system
  3. The Property Manager only pays for things that the Service Provider invoices through our platform or via Preferred Service Provider relationships outside of our system
3. If the Service Provider charges a visit fee but doesn't complete the work, review **Part 2: Service Provider Payment**

## 🎯 When to Break This SOP

You can break the SOP in the following scenarios:

- **If the Property Manager or Owner has already provided explicit approval for the new budget outside of the standard process** (such as via email, call, or note on the work order), you may update the approved budget and document the exception accordingly.
- **If the service provider requests immediate payment for an initial visit while the budget change is still pending,** escalate to the Payments team or follow the payment escalation process, rather than waiting for standard approval.
- **If you are unsure how to proceed or encounter an unusual situation not covered by the SOP,** escalate to an Operations Support Specialist for guidance rather than proceeding independently.
