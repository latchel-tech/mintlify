## 🧠 What This Process Is Trying to Solve

<Note>
  We want to prevent confusion, miscommunication, and resident frustration during complex or sensitive troubleshooting calls by providing associates with practical tips and suggested language for handling tricky situations. The goal is to help troubleshooters navigate challenging conversations—such as when residents are anxious, issues are unclear, or expectations need to be managed—so they can gather accurate information, show empathy, and set clear next steps.
</Note>

<Accordion title="General tips">
  When Troubleshooting, we call because a resident is experiencing a maintenance problem that is disrupting their normal daily routine at home, _therefore it's critical to be considerate in how we gather information and communicate._ We should aim for interactions characterized by agreement, mutual understanding, and empathy. In other words, **_establish rapport._**

  - Be prepared. Follow the steps mentioned in [How to: Properly Diagnose Emergency Situations](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Properly-Diagnose-Emergency-Situations) before calling. Call-back time is important, but being prepared will provide the best customer experience.
  - **Listen actively and calibrate with the resident.** Paraphrase what the resident is saying and agree with their statements. Calibrate with their attitude. Remember that anxiety levels may be high. If they are, adopt a serious but calm tone while showing empathy. Avoid fillers like “hmm” or “huh” as they’ll diminish their perception of your confidence.

  <Check>
    Example: _“I see, so the situation is…” or “Let me recap the information to make sure I’ve got it right.”_
  </Check>

  - **_Be mindful of the context._** You should always use [Job Aid: Troubleshooter Question Bank](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Job-Aid-Troubleshooter-Question-Bank), but be mindful of the specific situation and _only choose those questions that are pertinent. _You can also adapt the questions or add some of your own.
  - **_Tactfully mention if there is a potential risk for additional issues._** Water leaks or furnace problems are examples of maintenance issues that may require more than one work order to mitigate their consequences. Confirm that you have received the full information about the initial report with the resident, and after that, mention any other work order that may be necessary for you to create.

  <Check>
    Example:<br />_“Thank you for confirming the information with me. Now, I’d like to help you mitigate all the outcomes of this issue and that may require additional work orders. For that, I need to ask you just a few more questions. Would that be ok?”_
  </Check>

  - **_Be clear, keep your word and close the loop_**. Keep your word if you tell the resident that you will do something after the call. In case things don’t go as you expected, _close the loop with the resident with a follow-up SMS to inform them of the next steps._
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="AC: Discussing temperature thresholds during hot weather">
  1. Most importantly, **_show empathy_**. Apologize for the situation by saying something like:

  <Check>
    "_I'm really sorry you're experiencing this AC issue. It definitely sounds uncomfortable, and we'd like to get it solved for you as soon as possible._"
  </Check>

  2. Make sure to **_check the temperature widget (which shows up to 3 days of forecast data) and refer to the conditions with the customer_**. This shows the customer that you're familiar with their situation and helps confirm whether the temperature threshold will be met within the applicable timeframe.

  <Check>
    Ex: "_I see that today's forecasted high is 82 degrees, and over the next 3 days it could reach up to 88 degrees."_
  </Check>

  **📝 Note:** If the PM's Intake Settings specify a shorter timeframe (e.g., "in the next 24 hours"), only reference the forecast within that timeframe. If the PM's rule references **inside temperature**, ask the resident to confirm the current indoor temperature instead.

  <Check>
    Ex: "_I see that today's forecasted high is 82 degrees, and tomorrow it could reach 84 degrees."_
  </Check>

  3. **_Request photos!_** You can ask the resident for a photo of the thermostat as well as the AC unit (if it is not central air).
  4. **_Determine if the AC issue is an emergency_**.
     a. **‼️IMPORTANT**: Always refer to the guidelines on [Handling Work Order Severity via Harmonia Intake Settings](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Service-Ops-Handling-Work-Order-Severity-via-Harmonia-Harmonia-X-Intake-Settings#service-ops-handling-work-order-severity-via-harmonia-and-harmonia-x-intake-settings)

  <Warning>
    **⚠️ MANDATORY STEP FOR ALL AC ISSUES: Physical Cooling Limitation Check**

    This step is now required for **every** AC issue during extreme heat — not only when the PM's Intake Settings explicitly reference indoor temperature. Do not skip this check.

    If the PM has designated the issue an emergency based on outdoor temperature, and the resident reports the AC is not reaching the thermostat setting, you must **ask for the current indoor temperature** and compare it to the outdoor temperature.

    An AC system can typically only cool to about **20°F below the outdoor temperature**. If the indoor temperature is within ~20°F of the outdoor temperature, do not assume the AC is malfunctioning or automatically dispatch a vendor. Example: if it's 100°F outside and 80°F inside, the system may be operating within its expected physical limitations.

    Before making a dispatch decision, confirm whether there is evidence of an actual equipment failure:

    - Is the AC running?
    - Is it blowing cool air?
    - Is the system maintaining a reasonable differential from outdoor temperature?
    - Are there other signs of malfunction (leaking, unusual noises, not turning on, etc.)?

    **Decision:**

    - No evidence of failure + temperature within expected physical limits → **DE-ESCALATE / DO NOT DISPATCH**. Explain (with scripting) that extreme outdoor temperatures can limit how low an AC system can physically cool the home.
    - Evidence of equipment failure or an abnormal temperature differential → **DISPATCH**.

    **🔑 Key bias:** Before dispatching an AC vendor during extreme heat, determine whether there is evidence of an actual equipment failure versus the system simply being unable to achieve the requested temperature due to the outdoor conditions.

    **📝 Note:** This is a general operational guideline that applies independent of the product workflow. Product will eventually add indoor-temperature collection and automated guidance to the intake flow so Harmonia can identify these scenarios automatically — but Operations should begin applying this logic now rather than waiting for the product enhancement.
  </Warning>

  **Follow the 3-Day Default Temperature Protocol**

  - Always err on the side of caution. If the situation within the next 3 days qualifies as an emergency based on Harmonia's weather data, treat it as such, even if it may resolve or become non-emergency later. If the PM's Intake Settings specify a different timeframe (e.g., "in the next 24 hours"), the Intake Settings override this default.
  - TS members must **assess the current situation**, not the potential future condition.
    - Example: If the current temperature meets the emergency threshold, classify as Emergency even if it is predicted to drop tomorrow.
  - Even if dispatch is deferred, always follow the **current severity**, not the next day’s forecast. On the following day, a troubleshooter may re-evaluate and de-escalate if appropriate.

  **⚠️ Inside Temperature Rules**

  Some PM Intake Settings reference **inside (indoor) temperature** rather than outside temperature.

  Examples:

  - _"If the heater is broken and the inside temperature is below 60°F, escalate to emergency"_
  - _"If the AC is broken and the inside temperature is above 85°F, escalate to emergency"_

  For inside temperature rules:

  - Harmonia **cannot** determine inside temperature from weather data — it must be confirmed with the resident during intake.
  - If Harmonia did not ask the resident about indoor temperature during intake, the troubleshooter **must ask** during the TS call.
  - Ask the resident to provide a **photo of the thermostat or indoor thermometer** showing the current reading when possible.
    - **Do not offer this proactively**: If they cannot provide a photo, document the verbal report and note that it was not photo-verified.
  - Document the resident-reported indoor temperature in the WO notes.
  - Apply the PM's rule using the resident-reported temperature.

  **Latchel’s default temperature settings**:

  **High temperatures: 85**°**F for infants/elderly/disabled, 95**°F standard

  **Low temperatures: 55**°**F for infants/elderly/disabled, 45**°F standard

  5. **_Even if you need to de-escalate based on PM’s Intake Settings, do not tell the customer their issue is not an emergency_** -- this just creates an emotional response (and usually a poor rating!). Even if you know that the issue will be de-escalated to Urgent or Normal based on the PM's temperature thresholds and intake settings, it's not worth trying to explain to a customer what all of them mean and the associated timeframes. Just set expectations by letting the customer know:

  <Check>
    Premium: "_We'll work on getting this scheduled as quickly as possible and you'll receive a text with the appointment window as soon as it's confirmed."_
  </Check>

  <Check>
    Non-Premium: "_This work order will be forwarded to the appropriate team and they will be reaching out to you soon on next steps."_
  </Check>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Roof leaks during a storm">
  1. Most importantly, **_show empathy_**. Leaks in the roof or ceiling may cause residents to feel vulnerable because their homes and belongings are at risk. Use some of the following examples to empathize with the resident:

  <Note>
    - _“I really appreciate you taking the time to report this issue.”_
    - _“Thanks for providing us with this information about the leak. It definitely sounds uncomfortable, and we'd like to get it solved for you as soon as possible.”_
  </Note>

  2. **_Start by confirming and paraphrasing the information the resident has provided in the work order, then troubleshoot_**. Then proceed to ask (and take note of!) the Troubleshooting Questions listed in the “Roof leak during a storm” section of [Job Aid: Troubleshooter Question Bank](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Job-Aid-Troubleshooter-Question-Bank), adapting them to the situation as needed.

  <Note>
    Example for paraphrasing:<br />_”I’d like to confirm I received the information you gave us correctly…”_<br />_”The information I have about this leak is…”_

    Example to make resident aware you’ll start asking questions:<br />_”Thanks again for providing us with this information about the leak. In order to assist you in the best way possible, I’d like to ask you for more information, including pictures or videos, to share with the appropriate team and Service Provider. Do you have a few minutes for that?”_
  </Note>

  Set clear expectations with the resident. Steps 8 & 9 of the “Roof leak during a storm” section of the [Job Aid: Troubleshooter Question Bank](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Job-Aid-Troubleshooter-Question-Bank) will help you to determine the next steps to follow. Advise the resident that we’ll try to find a Service Provider as soon as possible, but they may be delayed due to the weather conditions. Focus on mitigation in the meantime. **_Do not tell the resident that we might be able to tarp the roof until you’ve secured an appointment with a Service Provider._**

  <Note>
    “I agree with you on the severity of this leak. However, I’d like to set realistic expectations that most Service Providers will not be able to perform a roof inspection until the weather has cleared since the current conditions can be quite dangerous.”

    - If the issue is still an Emergency based on PM settings: _“That said, I will start contacting Service Providers now, and you’ll receive a text message once we’re able to confirm an appointment window. It may need to be rescheduled based on weather conditions, but we’ll do our best to get someone out as soon as it’s safe.”_
    - If the issue will be de-escalated based on PM settings: _“That said, I will share all of the information you provided with the appropriate team and they will be in touch with you regarding next steps.”_
  </Note>

  When contacting a Service Provider, keep it conversational. Keep in mind that most Service Providers will only be comfortable going out once the weather improves, but we can ask about the possibility of tarping. Agree with them and keep a conversational, less formal, tone to earn their trust. Provide the SP with the information you’ve obtained from the resident. Answer the SP’s questions, and do your best to secure an appointment within the next 48 hours, following Step 9 of  [Job Aid: Troubleshooter Question Bank](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Job-Aid-Troubleshooter-Question-Bank).

  <Note>
    _”I need someone to help with a roof leak in (name of city). I am not super familiar with the weather in that area. What’s your recommendation?”_

    _“Are you able to go out and make an evaluation of whether you can tarp the roof to prevent further property damage? If you’re comfortable with that, you could send us an estimate for the repair to complete once the weather improves.”_

    _“Do you think you can go within 24 hours? 48 hours? We understand that the appointment may need to be rescheduled depending on how the weather evolves, but we’d like to get something on the calendar ASAP.”_
  </Note>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Basement or Below-Grade Water Intrusion During Active Rain">
  When a resident reports water entering the basement or another below-grade area during active rain — whether through wall cracks, floor seams, foundation joints, window wells, or other exterior groundwater intrusion — this is typically a foundation, drainage, or waterproofing issue. The source of the water may not be possible to stop until the weather clears.

  **Key Decision: Does this meet Property Damage / Extreme Habitability criteria?**

  Do not treat every basement or below-grade rain leak as an automatic EEC escalation. First, assess the current severity using the guidance in [How to: Identify Property Damage and Extreme Habitability Situations](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Identify-Property-Damage-and-Extreme-Habitability-Situations).

  For water leaks, including foundation leaks, treat it as Property Damaging if the leak is intense, filling more than a bucket per day and is not leaking into a drain, or if the resident cannot contain or mitigate the leak. If there is standing water near electrical systems, unsafe living conditions, or another immediate habitability/safety concern, follow the Extreme Habitability guidance in the same job aid.

  **Important:** Water extraction may be ineffective while rain is still active if water is continuing to enter from a weather-driven foundation, drainage, or below-grade exterior source. Do not dispatch water extraction solely because water is present if the resident can contain it and the situation does not meet PD/EH criteria.

  **Steps:**

  1. **Show empathy and confirm the situation with the resident.**

  <Note>
    _“I'm sorry you're dealing with this. I can see how frustrating it must be to have water coming into the basement, especially while it is still raining.”_
  </Note>

  2. **Confirm the source and severity of the leak. Ask probing questions to verify:** If the source is confirmed as weather-driven foundation, drainage, or below-grade exterior intrusion, not plumbing, this is a structural, drainage, or waterproofing issue driven by weather.
     1. Is the water coming through visible cracks in the basement wall, floor, or floor seams?
     2. Is water entering through a window well, foundation joint, or another below-grade exterior opening?
     3. Is there any plumbing nearby that could be the actual source?
     4. Has this happened before during heavy rain?
     5. How much water is coming in? Is it a small trickle, pooling water, or several inches of standing water?
     6. Is the water draining anywhere, such as into a floor drain or sump pump?
     7. Can the resident contain or mitigate it with towels, buckets, or by moving belongings away?
     8. Is the water near electrical outlets, panels, appliances, HVAC equipment, or other safety concerns?

  If the source is confirmed as weather-driven foundation, drainage, or below-grade exterior intrusion, not plumbing, this is a structural, drainage, or waterproofing issue driven by weather.

  3. **Determine the next step based on PD/EH criteria.**
     1. **If the resident can contain the water and it does not meet PD/EH criteria:** Do not contact EECs by default. Set expectations that water extraction or permanent repair may need to wait until the rain stops, document the details, and follow the standard non-emergency/PM settings process for remediation or foundation/drainage assessment after the weather clears.
     2. **If the water is intense, filling more than a bucket per day, not draining, or the resident cannot contain/mitigate it:** Treat it as Property Damaging and follow the standard PD escalation process.
     3. **If there is an immediate safety or habitability concern:** Follow the Extreme Habitability guidance and escalate accordingly.
  4. **Advise the resident on mitigation steps in the meantime:**

  <Note>
    _“While we wait for the rain to clear, here are a few things that can help minimize damage:”_

    - Move belongings and furniture away from the affected area.
    - Place towels or buckets to collect water if it is safe to do so.
    - If there is a floor drain nearby, try to direct water toward it.
    - Do not use electrical appliances near standing water.
    - If water is near electrical outlets, panels, or appliances, advise the resident to avoid the area and follow the appropriate emergency guidance.
  </Note>

  5. **Set expectations with the resident:**

  <Note>
    _“I want to be upfront with you — because the water appears to be coming in from outside due to the rain, sending someone for water extraction right now may not fully resolve the issue if water continues entering. I’m going to document the severity and whether the water can be contained, then we’ll follow the appropriate next steps based on the current risk and your property manager’s settings. If anything changes or the situation gets worse — for example, the water starts rising, you can no longer contain it, it reaches electrical outlets or appliances, or you notice any new safety concerns — please reach back out to us right away so we can reassess and take the appropriate next steps.”_
  </Note>

  6. **Document in the WO notes:**
     1. Source confirmed as weather-driven foundation, drainage, or below-grade exterior intrusion, if applicable.
     2. Active rain at time of call.
     3. Estimated amount of water entering and whether it is draining.
     4. Whether the resident can contain or mitigate the water.
     5. Whether the issue meets PD/EH criteria and what escalation path was followed.
     6. Any safety concerns, such as water near electrical systems or major appliances.
     7. Mitigation steps communicated to resident.
     8. Follow-up plan once weather clears.

  **📝 Note:** Once rain stops, follow standard water leak escalation guidelines. If water remains and remediation is needed, create the appropriate remediation WO. If the source appears to be foundation, drainage, or waterproofing-related, create or recommend a separate WO for assessment/repair based on the applicable process.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Calling to verify duplicate WOs">
  There are two main reasons residents submit a duplicate Work Order: 1) If they do not receive a fast response, or 2) if the issue has worsened. In both situations, you’ll need to verify if it is a duplicate work order before taking action.

  You should follow [(⛑EMERGENCY) How to: Handle Duplicate Work Orders](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/EMERGENCY-How-to-Handle-Duplicate-Work-Orders#emergency-how-to-handle-duplicate-work-orders). Here are some tips on how to discuss duplicates with residents, who may be angry or anxious while their issue is resolved.

  Provide an overview of the current status of the active WO. This may answer some questions from the resident or raise new ones. Make sure you gather enough information if there have been changes and **_provide a concise answer to any questions based on the information you have._**

  <Note>
    Examples:<br />_”We received a new request regarding your (issue) issue and I’d like to follow up with you on it. Do you have a couple minutes to go over it?”_

    _”I understand. It’s been (period of time) since you first contacted us to report this. At the moment, we are (current stage of the process) and that is the reason why you haven’t received any further information or updates yet.”_
  </Note>

  Set the right expectations. Once the resident is aware of the process and the status of the request, you may share the steps that will follow. Be clear that certain steps of the process depend on the appropriate team that will handle their case.

  <Note>
    Example:<br />_”The next step is… (add information according to the status). I’d like to thank you in advance for your patience while we wait for the appropriate team’s guidance.”_
  </Note>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Diagnostic Flow and Severity Questions">
  **_Start by paraphrasing the resident’s answers to the diagnostic flow._** When residents submit a new work order, they answer questions intended to deescalate the issue and that may be similar to those in [Job Aid: Troubleshooter Question Bank](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Job-Aid-Troubleshooter-Question-Bank). To avoid sounding redundant and having negative reactions from the residents, paraphrase their answers in a conversational tone before proceeding to ask more questions.<br />

  Reword previous questions as conversational sentences to help you paraphrase. Start around the second question and begin your sentences with we to establish rapport for two main reasons. First, we identify as the maintenance emergency line, and different people may interact with the resident while we process the work order. Also, using the word we sounds more collaborative with the resident. If the questions in the workflow include you (Did you...), use it to start your sentences and questions to shift the focus of the conversation to the resident.

  <Note>
    Examples<br />_”Thank you for taking my call. We received your report about (issue), and we’d like to confirm the information we received.”_

    Answers in the workflow:<br />_Question: What type of issue are you experiencing?_<br />_Answer: Other Leak_<br />_Question: Are you able to stop the leak from the source?_<br />_Answer: No_<br />_Question: Are you able to place a bucket or container under the leak to minimize property damage?_<br />_Answer: Yes_<br />_Question: If unattended, will this bucket or container overflow?_<br />_Answer: Yes_

    Paraphrasing:  _“So, you mentioned that you have a leak issue that you aren’t able to stop, but you can place a container under it. However, if you leave it unattended for some time, it will overflow.”_
  </Note>

  **_Take some time to explain the workflow or the intake settings_**. If the resident asks you something like _“Why isn’t this considered an emergency?”_ or _“Why is this considered an emergency if the issue is not a big deal?”_ or even _“Why are you calling me if I already entered information and pictures?”_ Mention that the initial questions gather as much information as possible about the issue, and each Property Manager adapts their settings for different issues. We call residents to troubleshoot based on the settings their particular Property Manager has configured.

  <Note>
    Examples:<br />_”I apologize for any repetition. We are calling you based on the information you provided when your work order was created. Your responses, combined with your Property Manager’s settings, instruct our team when to call a resident to attempt further troubleshooting. We just wanted to make sure everything is alright and that there isn’t any potential for property damage.”_

    _“Rest assured that with the information we’ve discussed, the appropriate team has what they need to get back to you on next steps.”_
  </Note>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="‼️ Fire Hazard / Power Shutoff — Troubleshooting Script & Tips">
  **When to use this:** A resident reports a major electrical system (HVAC, furnace, electric water heater, or other hardwired appliance) as "off" and you need to determine whether de-escalation is appropriate or Fire Hazard classification applies.

  > ⛔ **Before proceeding:** Screen for immediate danger. If the resident reports active flames, active smoke, active sparking, or anyone in immediate danger → stop this workflow and follow the 911-Severity process immediately.

  **Step 1 — Empathize and set the stage**

  > _"Thank you for reporting this. I want to make sure we handle this safely and correctly. I'm going to ask you a few questions about the system and what you've noticed — this will help us determine the right next steps."_

  **Step 2 — Clarify how the system was turned off**

  > _"You mentioned the (system) is off. Can you tell me how it was turned off — was it at the thermostat, a dial or switch on the unit, or did someone switch off the breaker/disconnect for that system?"_

  - If thermostat/dial/unit switch → power is still supplied. Note this.
  - If breaker/disconnect → power is fully disconnected. Confirm: _"Just to confirm, the breaker labeled for that system is in the OFF position?"_

  **Step 3 — Screen for malfunction indicators**

  > ⚠️ **Important:** Complete this indicator screening BEFORE asking the resident about breaker/disconnect status or asking them to operate any panel. Do not jump to breaker questions until you know whether active signs are present.

  > _"Have you noticed any of the following — currently or at any point before the system was turned off: a burning or electrical smell, smoke, sparks, popping sounds, burn marks, melted plastic, or discoloration near the unit?"_

  If yes, clarify timing:

  > _"Are you noticing that right now, or was that something that happened earlier before the system was shut off?"_

  **Step 4 — If signs are currently present → this qualifies as a Fire Hazard / Property Damaging situation regardless of whether the system control is off.**

  Follow the PM's intake settings to determine severity. If the PM's settings classify the issue as non-emergency but PD/EH criteria are met, escalate to the PM's Escalation Contacts per the standard PD/EH process to inform them and get permission to dispatch.

  > ⚠️ **Safety boundary:** Document power status as unconfirmed and proceed per PM's intake settings — if PD/EH criteria are met and PM classifies as non-emergency, escalate to PM's Escalation Contacts per standard PD/EH process.

  **Step 5 — If signs are past-only AND breaker is now confirmed off → past-only exception applies**

  > _"It sounds like the issue you noticed earlier has stopped, and the power is now fully disconnected at the breaker. Based on that, I'm going to proceed with your property manager's settings for this type of issue."_

  Follow PM's intake settings. Document past indicators and current confirmed shutoff.

  **Step 6 — If no signs are reported AND breaker is NOT confirmed off**

  This is the **only** scenario where you ask the resident to shut off the breaker:

  > _"As a precaution, I'd like you to go to your electrical panel and switch off the breaker for that system. This will fully disconnect power."_
  >
  > ⚠️ **Safety boundary:** Never ask a resident to open a panel or equipment cover, or to approach heat, water, smoke, sparks, or exposed wiring. If the resident expresses concern about safely accessing the breaker, do not push — document power as unconfirmed and continue the underlying assessment using PM's settings.

  - If resident **confirms shutoff** → continue normal intake. Follow PM's intake settings. No PD/EH.
  - If resident **cannot or will not shut off** → potential PD/EH. Power is unconfirmed on a reported electrical issue. Follow PM's intake settings to determine severity; if PM classifies as non-emergency, escalate to PM's Escalation Contacts to inform them of the unconfirmed power status and get direction."

  **Step 7 — Document in the work order**

  - How the system was powered down (thermostat/dial vs. breaker/disconnect)
  - Whether malfunction signs are currently present, past-only, or not reported
  - The resulting classification and reasoning
  - Any safety concerns or resident inability to access the breaker

  > 🔗 **Cross-reference:** For the classification table and decision rules, see the Fire Hazard / Power Shutoff section in [How to: Identify Property Damage and Extreme Habitability Situations](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Identify-Property-Damage-and-Extreme-Habitability-Situations). If the equipment is actively misbehaving and cannot be controlled, use Step 1.5 Safety Assessment & Immediate Mitigation instead.

  ### Common Mistakes to Avoid

  | Mistake | Why it's wrong | Correct approach |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Treating "system off at thermostat" as safe / no fire risk | Thermostat off ≠ power off. Internal components still have power and can short-circuit, spark, or overheat. | Always clarify shutoff method. Only breaker/disconnect = power fully disconnected. |
  | Skipping indicator screening and jumping to breaker questions | You need to know if active signs exist before deciding next steps or asking the resident to approach the panel. | Complete Step 3 before Step 4. |
  | Treating past-only indicators as meeting PD/EH criteria when breaker is confirmed off | If signs are no longer present and the **breaker/disconnect for the affected circuit is confirmed off**, the active hazard is resolved. | Follow PM's intake settings. Document past indicators and confirmed shutoff status. No PD/EH escalation needed since the hazard is no longer active. |
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Leak Detection">
  **What is leak detection?**

  Leak detection is a sophisticated method for finding the source of a leak within plumbing _fixtures and piping. This is particularly important when you have_ no idea where the leak is actually coming from or where it is. It's pretty common to be needed when the water bill is just excessively high. Less common is when you see a leak but aren't sure where the water is coming from (e.g. since there isn't any plumbing nearby).

  If you're handling troubleshooting or dispatching for an emergency, chances are Leak Detection isn't the right skill. You should be thinking that when you are choosing this skill (Leak Detection) -- you **aren't** sending someone to fix a leak. You're sending someone to find a leak, and to find it using super expensive and fancy equipment. Refer to the following [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yrn9dXEOVGY) for an overview of the leak detection process.

  Leak detection would not be the correct skill in the following scenarios:

  - If the leak is suspected to be coming from the roof,  send a roofer instead.
    - Skill: Roofing > Roof Leak Inspection
  - If the leak is suspected to be coming from an AC unit, send an HVAC tech instead.
    - Skill: HVAC > General HVAC
  - If the leak is coming from a plumbing fixture, send a regular plumber instead.
    - Skill: Plumbing > Repair Internal Leaking
  - If the leak is visibly coming from a broken pipe, send a regular plumber instead.
    - Skill: Plumbing > Repair Broken Pipe
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Operational Guidelines for Water Leak Escalation">
  When addressing water leaks, it’s essential to act quickly to minimize damage and address the situation appropriately. Follow these guidelines to ensure a swift and coordinated response:

  <Warning>
    **⚠️ No shut-off valve? (Rain, groundwater, flooding, or exterior intrusion)**

    If the water is entering due to a natural event or exterior source — such as active rain, groundwater, flooding, foundation seepage, window wells, or another below-grade/exterior intrusion — there may be no water supply valve to shut off. Do not apply the shut-off-valve steps below as-is. Instead, refer to the **“Basement or Below-Grade Water Intrusion During Active Rain”** section of this page for guidance on assessing PD/EH criteria and determining whether remediation should be dispatched immediately or deferred until the source stops/weather clears.
  </Warning>

  - **If the water is already shut off:**
    - **Action**: Immediately dispatch the remediation team to mitigate any water damage.
    - Generate a new work order for remediation and assign a remediation service provider to handle the issue.
      - [How to: Create a New Work Order from "Incognito Mode" (All Submissions)](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Create-a-New-Work-Order-from-Incognito-Mode-All-Submissions#how-to-create-a-new-work-order-from-incognito-mode-all-submissions)
      - [(EMERGENCY) How to: Assign a Preferred + Latchel Network Service Provider](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/EMERGENCY-How-to-Assign-a-Preferred-Latchel-Network-Service-Provider)
    - Ensure that all necessary details (e.g., location of the leak, extent of damage, urgency) are accurately entered.
    - **Simultaneously**: Start the leak repair process to address the root cause of the issue and prevent further damage.
  - **If the water is not off:**
    - **Action**: Instruct the resident to turn off the water supply as the first step.
      - “**_Hi (Resident Name)! To prevent further water damage, please turn off the water supply immediately. If you’re unsure how to do this, look for a shut-off valve near the fixture or the main water supply for your home. Let us know once this step is completed so we can assist further._**”
    - **Then**: Send out the remediation team to prevent water damage, while also beginning the leak repair process in parallel.
    - By following these steps, the situation can be handled more efficiently, reducing potential damage to the property
</Accordion>
