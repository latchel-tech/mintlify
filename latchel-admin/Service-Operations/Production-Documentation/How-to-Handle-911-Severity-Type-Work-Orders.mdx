## 🧠 What This Process Is Trying to Solve

<Note>
  We want to ensure that true emergencies—those requiring a 911 response—are identified and handled quickly, safely, and according to protocol. The goal of this SOP is to provide clear, step-by-step instructions for associates when a resident reports a life-threatening or property-threatening emergency. This process prevents delays, miscommunication, and improper handling of critical incidents—reducing risk to residents, property, and Latchel.
</Note>

<Warning>
  If you are handling a call that does not have a work order created yet, please make sure it is created before hanging up with the customer, in case they haven't called 911
</Warning>

# Content

<Warning>
  For 911-severity situations, always notify the Property Manager:

  - If the issue indicates an **active or ongoing emergency**, \*\*call \*\*the Property Manager regardless of whether it is part of the current work order or related to a different work order.
    - Manually trigger the **Who Ya Gonna Call** feature from the Action Menu of the work order to identify the correct escalation contacts and their contact order. Refer to [https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Job-Aid-Critical-Pre-dispatch-Warranty-Special-Notes-Emergency-Escalation-Contacts](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Job-Aid-Critical-Pre-dispatch-Warranty-Special-Notes-Emergency-Escalation-Contacts) for more details.
  - For **contained or mitigated situations** \*\*handled by the 911 team, \*\*a phone call is **not required**; timely notification via \*\*SMS **is sufficient to inform the Property Manager of the actions taken and the recommended next steps. See [https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Handle-911-Severity-Type-Work-Orders\*\*1.c.1](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Handle-911-Severity-Type-Work-Orders**1.c.1).**

  Timely notification in all active cases remains **critical to ensure safety and proper escalation**.
</Warning>

## 📋 Step-by-Step Process

### \*\*Step 1 - When the Resident Should Call 911 \*\*

1. When a call is received from a resident for one of the following requests:

- 🔥Fire 
- 🆘Carbon Monoxide Leak 
- ⛽Gas Leak -\* (if the gas company has already visited and confirmed the property is safe, no need to call 911)\*
- 👮‍♂️Crime Related Issue  
  - Burglary related 
  - Blood 
- Hazmat - Hazmat refers to hazardous _chemical or airborne substances_ such as gas leaks, carbon monoxide, CO2, or toxic chemical spills. **Mold and biological growth are NOT classified as hazmat** under this SOP and should be handled through standard mold remediation channels.
- ⚡Electrical pole falling/leaning on building or home 
- Tree falling onto building
- Major flooding

### **When do I need to call the Property Manager**

It is critical to identify any indicators of a 911 situation when dealing with customers. In the following scenarios, contacting the Property Manager is essential. Refer to [https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/edit-v2/2537652248#Severe-Damage-Escalation](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/edit-v2/2537652248#Severe-Damage-Escalation) for additional details:

- **Ongoing 911 Situations:**<br />Any issues listed under **_Step 1_** of this document that are identified as active 911 situations.
- **Severe Damage Escalation:**<br />Situations where severe damage to the property has been identified or escalated.
- **Recent 911 Situations:**<br />Any mention of a recent 911 situation, whether it impacts the property associated with the work order you’re handling or any other property managed by the Property Manager.
- **Unclear or Potentially Emergency Scenarios:**<br />If there’s any uncertainty about whether an issue could qualify as a 911 situation or poses a significant risk, err on the side of caution and notify the Property Manager.

## **[❓](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Handle-Emergency-Work-Orders)Frequently Asked Questions**

<Accordion title="When is a vehicle or home break-in/burglary considered an active 911 emergency, and is requesting footage a maintenance request?">
  - \*\*Question 1: \*\*If a resident reports that their vehicle or home was broken into and items were stolen, should this be treated as an active 911 emergency?
    - \*\*Answer: No \*\*— if the incident has already occurred and there is no ongoing threat, it should be classified as a **Contained (Non-Active Incident)**.
      - This applies when:
        - First responders mitigated the issue.
        - The break-in is not in progress
        - The suspect is no longer on site
        - No one is injured
        - It shifts to documentation and follow-up (police report, footage preservation, etc.)
      - **When it _would_ be considered active:**
        - The break-in is occurring now.
        - The suspect remains on the property.
        - An active threat or confrontation occurred.
        - Someone is injured or in danger.
  - \*\*Question 2: \*\*Is requesting security/CCTV footage related to an incident considered a maintenance request?
    - \*\*Answer: No \*\*— requesting security or CCTV footage is **not considered a maintenance request**. Let the resident know we do not handle those requests.
      - **Maintenance requests** are typically for **repairs, inspections, or upkeep** of the property.
      - **CCTV/security footage requests** are **information or documentation requests**. They don’t involve fixing or servicing the property itself.
      - These requests are often handled as **security or administrative tasks** rather than maintenance.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What do I do if someone (resident, fire department, police, or other emergency services) reaches out about a 911 or PD/EH situation, but the PM/property management company is disabled or inactive (no subscription)?">
  Resident safety always comes first — even without an active subscription.

  1. **Determine the severity of the situation:**
  2. **If the issue is PD/EH only (not 911):** Route the customer to their Property Manager.
  3. **If the issue is a 911 emergency:** Route the customer to call 911 immediately.
  4. **Customer Script:** 🗨️ **For PD/EH issues (not 911), say something like:** _"I understand you're dealing with an urgent situation. Since your property management company is currently inactive in our system, we're unable to create a work order at this time. I'd recommend reaching out to your Property Manager directly for assistance. In the meantime, we will send a notification to your Property Manager to make them aware of the situation."_ 🗨️ **For 911 emergencies, say something like:** _"Your safety is our top priority. Please call 911 immediately for emergency assistance. Since your property management company is currently inactive in our system, we're unable to create a work order, but we will send a notification to your Property Manager to make them aware of the emergency."_
  5. **Send a courtesy email to the PM** using the **Failed Escalation Contacts** email under the Emergency escalation contacts tab.
  6. Refer to [https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Job-Aid-Critical-Pre-dispatch-Warranty-Special-Notes-Emergency-Escalation-Contacts](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Job-Aid-Critical-Pre-dispatch-Warranty-Special-Notes-Emergency-Escalation-Contacts)
  7. **Document the interaction** (call notes, email sent) so there's a record of what was reported and what action was taken.

  **Email Template**

  **Subject:** Urgent: [911 Emergency / PD/EH Issue] Reported at [Property Address] — Action Required

  **Body:**

  Hi [Property Management Company]! This is [Associate Name] from Latchel. We received [a call from the fire department / a report from the resident / a call from emergency services / a report regarding a property damage or environmental hazard issue] regarding [brief description of the emergency — e.g., an active fire / a gas leak / a police-related incident / property damage / environmental hazard] at [Property Address].

  [If 911: We advised [the resident / emergency services] to contact 911, and [emergency services are on-site / have been dispatched / 911 has already responded].]

  [If PD/EH only: We advised the resident to contact you directly regarding this property damage/environmental hazard issue.]

  Please note that your account is currently inactive in our system, so we are unable to process a work order for this property. However, we wanted to ensure you are aware of the situation so you can take any necessary steps to follow up.

  If you have any questions or would like to reactivate your account with Latchel, please feel free to reach out to us.

  Best regards,<br />[Associate Name]<br />Latchel Support Team

  📝 **NOTE:** No subscription = no work order can be created, but resident safety and PM awareness still take priority. For 911 emergencies, do NOT delay routing the resident or assisting emergency services while attempting to reach a disabled PM. For PD/EH issues, ensure the customer is directed to their PM and that a notification is sent.
</Accordion>
