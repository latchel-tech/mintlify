## 🧠 What This Process Is Trying to Solve

<Note>
We aim to ensure accurate identification and prioritization of property damage and extreme habitability issues. The goal is to provide clear guidelines for triage and escalation, preventing unnecessary delays and ensuring resident safety and satisfaction.

For our customers, this process ensures that emergencies are handled swiftly and appropriately, minimizing inconvenience and potential harm. For Latchel, it enhances service reliability and customer trust by ensuring that critical issues are prioritized and managed effectively.

Please refer to [Job Aid: How to Properly Diagnose Emergency Situations](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Properly-Diagnose-Emergency-Situations) to understand how defining property damage and extreme habitability fits into the overall emergency diagnosis process.
</Note>

<Danger>
Life threatening emergencies such as fire, gas leak, CO2 leak, crime related issues (attempted break-ins(burglaries) or blood), hazmat, electrical pole falling/leaning on building or home, Tree falling onto building and major flooding need to be referred to 911. Refer to https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Handle-911-Severity-Type-Work-Orders for detailed information.
</Danger>

<Warning>
**🔔 Reminder: Active Threat Protocols**

In the event of an **Active Threat** (e.g., gunman), please follow our **outbound communication protocols** as defined in the SOP here: https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Job-Aid-PM-Outbound-Communication-for-Emergency-WOs.

**Key Points:**

- **Inform the officer** that tenant data **CANNOT **be shared.
- **Notify the officer** that we will contact the **Property Manager's escalation team** to inform them of the situation. Request the callback details to provide to the Property Manager.
- **Call the escalation contacts **to notify them of the situation.
- Advise the **Property Manager **to contact the police directly if tenant data is needed.

Please ensure this protocol is followed to ensure proper handling of sensitive situations.
</Warning>

## **🏚️**Defining Property Damage and Extreme Habitability

- **Property Damage** is defined as any potential issue that is likely to cause continued physical damage to an otherwise non-damaged item or portion of the home, i.e. if the issue is left unresolved, the damage to the property will increase over time. With that, the longer it takes to resolve the issue, the bigger the damage will become and thus more expensive to fix.
- **Extreme Habitability** is defined as an issue that represents the loss of a major service or a potential safety issue (see table below for specifics).

### ‼️ Fire Hazard / Power Shutoff — Quick-Reference Classification

**🔥 Key Definitions — Fire/Electrical Severity Tiers**

- **911-Severity (Life-Safety):** Active flames, active smoke, active sparking, or anyone in immediate danger *right now*. → Immediately follow the 911-Severity process. Do not continue with the classification table below.
- **Fire Hazard / PD (Property-Damaging):** Signs of electrical malfunction are present (burning smell, evidence of past sparking, visible damage) but there is *no active, immediate life-threatening situation*. → Use the classification table below to determine next steps per PM's intake settings.

When a resident reports a major electrical system (HVAC, furnace, electric water heater, or other hardwired appliance) as "off," use the following table to determine classification. Remember: "system off" at a thermostat, dial, or unit control does NOT mean power is disconnected.

If malfunction signs were reported only in the past and are not currently present, AND the resident now confirms the breaker/disconnect is off, this does not meet PD/EH criteria — follow PM's intake settings for the underlying issue.

| Resident Response | Classification |
| --- | --- |
| Breaker/disconnect confirmed off, no signs of malfunction currently present | Follow PM's intake settings for the underlying issue |
| Breaker/disconnect confirmed off, but signs of malfunction **currently present or ongoing** | Fire Hazard / PD criteria met — follow PM's intake settings. If PM classifies as non-emergency, escalate to PM's Escalation Contacts per standard PD/EH process. |
| Breaker/disconnect NOT confirmed off, signs of malfunction currently present | Fire Hazard / PD criteria met — follow PM's intake settings. If PM classifies as non-emergency, escalate to PM's Escalation Contacts per standard PD/EH process. |
| Breaker/disconnect NOT confirmed off, no signs of malfunction reported | Ask resident to shut off breaker/disconnect as a precaution. **If unable/unwilling** - Potential PD/EH — power is unconfirmed on a reported electrical issue. Follow PM's intake settings; if PM classifies as non-emergency, escalate to PM's Escalation Contacts to inform them of the unconfirmed power status and get direction. If resident **confirms shutoff - **Continue normal intake. Follow PM's intake settings. No PD/EH. ; document power as unconfirmed. |
| Signs of malfunction **reported only in the past**, not currently present, AND breaker/disconnect now confirmed off | Follow PM's settings — past-only exception applies; document past indicators and current confirmed shutoff |

> ⚠️ **Safety boundary:** Never ask a resident to open a panel or equipment cover, or to approach heat, water, smoke, sparks, or exposed wiring. If the resident expresses concern about safely accessing the breaker, do not push — document power as unconfirmed and classify accordingly.

> 🔗 **Cross-reference:** For full troubleshooting scripting and step-by-step guidance on this workflow, see the Fire Hazard / Power Shutoff section in  https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Job-Aid-Troubleshooter-Tips-for-Tricky-Situations. If the equipment is actively misbehaving and cannot be controlled, use Step 1.5 Safety Assessment & Immediate Mitigation instead.

## ❓Frequently Asked Questions

<Accordion title="Does a wild animal trapped inside a wall qualify as a PD/EH issue?">
No — a wild/rabid animal trapped inside walls, ceilings, or inaccessible areas of the property does not qualify as an extreme habitability issue. For these types of issues, always follow the PM's intake settings. If the PM has no matching rule, defer to their fallback settings.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Resident reports an alarm from an inaccessible utility/mechanical room — no gas smell, no smoke, no symptoms. What do I do?">
**A:** **Step 1 — 911 Gate Check:**  
Does the resident report ANY of the following?

- Gas smell
- Hissing sound
- Physical symptoms (dizziness, nausea, headache)
- Smoke or fire

**→ YES to any** = 911-Severity. Instruct resident to evacuate immediately and call 911. Follow the [911 process](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Handle-911-Severity-Type-Work-Orders).

**→ NO to all** = Continue to Step 2.

**Step 2 — Can the resident access or mitigate?**

**→ YES — Resident CAN access the area:**  
Ask the resident to identify the source of the alarm if safe to do so (e.g., check for a tripped breaker, locate the alarming device). If the resident identifies active indicators (gas smell, smoke, sparking) at any point, return to Step 1. Otherwise, follow PM's intake settings for the underlying issue.

**→ NO — Area is locked/inaccessible to the resident:**  
They cannot verify or shut anything off. Follow PM's intake settings. If PM classifies as non-emergency, escalate to PM's Escalation Contacts. Inform them:

- Alarm is active
- Area is inaccessible to resident
- Gas/hazard presence cannot be confirmed or ruled out

Get direction from PM/escalation contact before proceeding.

**Document:** Note that the resident reported alarm only, no active indicators, and whether area was accessible or inaccessible. Include any mitigation steps the resident took.
</Accordion>

## 🎯 When to Break This SOP

<Note>
*You should break or make exceptions to this SOP if the situation escalates to a life-threatening emergency (such as fire, gas leak, carbon monoxide leak, crime, hazmat, electrical pole falling on the building, tree falling onto the building, or major flooding). In these cases, immediately follow the 911-Severity Work Order process instead of the standard escalation steps. The goal is to ensure resident safety and rapid emergency response, even if it means bypassing normal protocols.*
</Note>
