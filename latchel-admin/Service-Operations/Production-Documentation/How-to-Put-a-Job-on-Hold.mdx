## 🧠 What This Process Is Trying to Solve

We want to ensure jobs only move forward when all necessary correspondence and coordination with Property Managers, Service Providers, owners, or residents is complete. The goal is to keep everyone informed and aligned, so work orders progress smoothly and efficiently, resulting in better outcomes for customers and Latchel.

## 📋 Step-by-Step Process

<Note>
**Reminder for RentRedi and NOMAD Work Orders**

Before moving a work order forward (which will notify the owner), always include the following message to text us back:

"Please text us at 206-717-8374 to advise how you wish to proceed."
</Note>

## ⏸️ Putting a Job on Hold

Depending on the unique circumstances of a Work Order, the order may need to be placed on hold. The agent may be led to place the order on hold by the system workflow, or the agent may need to manually click the action button and choose the option for "Put this Job on Hold"

The agent will also enter a summary of what will happen next or what the next steps will be.

**Waiting for PM** - Work orders should remain in their current status unless there is missing or unclear information that only the Property Manager (PM) can provide in order to move forward. In most cases, this is rare. For guidance on how to handle work orders that are already awaiting PM Action, refer to [How to: Handle situations for WOs awaiting PM action](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/2741829633)

<Note>
📝NOTE: It should also be used when the property owner (with the exception of a RentRedi owner) requests to put the WO on hold. In this case, the PM should be informed of the owner's request and the PM should handle communication with the owner directly.

For example: An owner requesting to put the job on hold in order to contact a warranty company, etc.
</Note>

**Approved Scenarios for Moving a Work Order to "Waiting for PM"**

- The Property Manager has specifically requested that the work order be moved to "Waiting for PM" or forwarded to them or advised that they will be handling the work order on their end.
  - This also applies to work orders where critical notes or other WO notes include instructions from the PM to forward the request to them.
  - For cases where the work order is no longer in TS status but the PM has provided instructions to forward it, associates/troubleshooters must follow the process outlined in FAQ 1 of the [How to: Handle Emergency Work Orders](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/759332893) SOP.
- The work order is not managed by Latchel due to the PM's subscription coverage (e.g., PM is on 24/7 or after-hours emergency coverage and the issue is non-emergency).
- The work order is non-emergency and has warranty information which should be forwarded to the Property Manager.
- We are unable to source a service provider.
- The work order involves enabling a resident or property (e.g., resident or property got disabled mid-process).
- The work order was created by the Property Manager (e.g., for an inspection, unit turn, or make-ready), and the PM is actively managing it in the background.
- The unit is vacant and requires access instructions from the Property Manager.
- If the issue is property damaging or poses extreme habitability concerns (PD/EH) and all escalation contacts are unresponsive after the required attempts.
  - Refer to applicable scenarios where we can escalate back to PM in this SOP: [Job Aid: PM Outbound Communication for Emergency WOs](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/2537652248).
- The service provider submits an estimate directly to the PM but does not provide (or refuses to provide) the amount. Or if the service provider claims they have been approved, but cannot provide the estimate amount or specify who approved.

1. Set the Work Order to "Waiting for PM".
   - If the option is not available to you, escalate a request to the Operations Support Specialist team via Slack channel #ops_assistance_hub.
2. In the section "Describe why you moved the job to this status", explain why the job is on hold and what approval, direction, or clarification we are seeking from the property manager.

<Warning>
⚠️IMPORTANT: The information typed in this field will be sent DIRECTLY to the Property Manager. Provide descriptive responses; the goal is to allow Property Managers to receive better information on what actions they need to take.
</Warning>

3. Assign to the Property Manager user group.
4. Set the follow-up task due immediately.
5. Describe "What action or information is required from the Property Manager".

<Warning>
⚠️IMPORTANT: The information typed in this field will be sent DIRECTLY to the Property Manager. Be descriptive and clear with actionable steps from the Property Manager.
</Warning>

<Note>
📝NOTE: We do not call property managers to follow up (unless it is indicated on the critical notes).
</Note>

If the information needed is NOT critical to progressing the work order—or if it's something we can ask for via a task without halting progress—then the work order should not be moved to "Waiting for PM."

Instead, leave the WO in an appropriate state where no external workflows are triggered (vendor dispatch, auto-notifications, scheduling, etc.) and create a task to the PM.

If the scenario is not part of the approved 'Waiting for PM' list, leave the WO in the appropriate state below (based on the situation you're handling) and create a task to the PM.

**Pending**

Use when:
- The work order is in Confirming Completion or Awaiting Work Date and you receive an update from the resident or service provider that requires PM input.
- The resident reports the issue as unresolved via their dashboard, and the work order moves to Pending automatically. If the service provider is unresponsive, escalate to PM.
- You are following up with a resident or service provider about work order updates and they remain unresponsive.
- There is a conflict with service provider assignment and leaving the work order as-is risks automation errors, complaints, or disputes.

**Pending - Waiting for Materials**

Use when:
- The service provider reports they are waiting for materials but is unresponsive to follow-up attempts.

**Rescheduling with Tenant or Scheduling with Service Provider/Resident**

Use when:
- Both the resident and service provider must provide availability but are unresponsive.

**Needs Owner/PM Approval**

Use when:
- Awaiting estimate approval and new updates (e.g., revised amount or updated quote) require PM review.

Create/Assign the task directed to the PM with a task Title as "Action Required: PM Instructions or Information Needed"

Ensure that the task is set as follows: "Complete task on any job state change"

## 🎯 When to Break This SOP

You should only break the SOP for How to: Put a Job on Hold in rare, exceptional cases where strictly following the SOP would cause harm, violate compliance, or prevent urgent escalation (such as life safety, legal, or severe customer impact situations). In these cases:

- Document clearly why the SOP was not followed.
- Notify the Operations Support Specialist team immediately.
- Resume following the SOP as soon as the exception is resolved.

Examples of when it may be appropriate to break the SOP (not limited to):

- There is an immediate risk to health, safety, or property and waiting for standard approvals would cause harm. If the work order involves property damage, extreme habitability issues, or any emergency (such as fire, gas leak, major flooding, electrical hazard, or life-threatening situations), you must bypass the standard "on hold" process. Instead, immediately follow emergency escalation procedures and contact the appropriate emergency contacts. Only if all escalation contacts are unresponsive should you move the work order to "Waiting for PM"—and this should be clearly documented.
- System outages or technical issues prevent you from following the documented process.
- You receive direct instructions from senior leadership to deviate from the SOP.
