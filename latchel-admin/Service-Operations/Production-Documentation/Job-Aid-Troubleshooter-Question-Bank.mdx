## 🧠 What This Process Is Trying to Solve

<Note>
We want to prevent missed details, inconsistent troubleshooting, and misjudged emergencies by equipping troubleshooters with a bank of targeted questions for the most common urgent maintenance issues. The goal is to help associates quickly assess intake settings, gather the right information, and determine if an issue is property-damaging or requires escalation.
</Note>

<Note>
**🎓Training Content**

The contents of this page have been included in the **Troubleshooting refresher **Coassemble course.
</Note>

### Non-Emergency / Borderline Emergency — Resident Preference Check (Only for 5:00 PM–9:00 AM deferral window)

⚠️ This step applies ONLY to non-Property Damaging (PD) and non-Extreme Habitability (EH) emergencies, and only for calls received between 5:00 PM and 9:00 AM.

After completing troubleshooting with the resident, and before escalating to pre-dispatch contacts or proceeding with dispatch, use the following question to capture resident preference:

<Note>
"Would you be okay with us scheduling a service provider for tomorrow morning instead of tonight?"
</Note>

Use this question for non-PD/EH emergencies after completing troubleshooting, when the severity is mild enough that waiting is a legitimate option. This captures resident preference before any escalation to pre-dispatch contacts.

- If resident agrees to wait → Select "Work order does not need immediate dispatch" and document the resident's agreement in notes (e.g., "Resident confirmed the issue can wait until [time]. Dispatch deferred per resident preference.").
- If resident needs service tonight → Proceed with standard emergency dispatch flow.

Do NOT ask this question for PD or EH emergencies — dispatch immediately per standard flow.

### Troubleshooting Common Maintenance Problems

<Accordion title="Water leak">
<Warning>
**Attention: **Documenting water leaks is crucial for future mitigation or warranty claims. Always offer the customer to initiate a video call when there is a chance so you can guide them through the troubleshooting steps and document the issue at the same time. See: [(TECH 101) How to: Use Video Call for Troubleshooting](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/1058242752)

**Recommended blurb:**

*“We’d like to assist you in the best way possible with your emergency and avoid any damage to the property and your belongings. If you agree, we can hop on a quick video call so you can show me the issue and I’ll do my best to guide you through a few steps and questions to get this resolved. We don’t have to be on camera, you can just show me the issue and I’ll walk you through it.”*
</Warning>

### Step 1: Ask probing questions

1. Is this a common issue or is this the first time it has happened?
2. Can you describe specifically what is happening and how you noticed the issue?
3. Is there any specific time or event that seems to trigger this issue, or is it occurring randomly?
4. Have you noticed any recent changes or renovations in the property that might be related to the leak?

### Step 2: Identify where the leak is coming from

1. Are you able to see where the water is leaking from? 
2. Do you know what the source of the leak is?
3.  Are there any specific circumstances or weather conditions when the leak appears more pronounced?
4. Can you check if the leak is isolated to a particular area or if it's spreading to other parts of the property?

### Step 3: Identify the severity of the leak

1. From 1 to 10 how bad would you say it's leaking? 
2. Is the water leak causing any property damage?
3. Could you send me a video of the leak and any damage it is causing?
4. Would you like to switch to a video call so we can look at the leak together?
5. Are there any unusual smells associated with the water leak?
6. Have you noticed any discoloration or changes in the wall or ceiling near the leak?

### Step 4: Locate an individual shut-off valve

1. Are you able to see any individual shut-off valves near the leaking area? 
2. Can you shut off the water to this area without shutting off the water to the whole house?
3. Are there any peculiar sounds or vibrations in the plumbing system when the leak occurs?
4. Can you check if the water pressure in other areas of the house is affected when attempting to shut off the individual valves?
5. Are there any appliances or fixtures in close proximity to the leak that might be relevant to the issue?

<Note>
Each sink or toilet should have one or two individual shut-offs at the wall to supply water. You can turn these clockwise to shut them off in the event of an overflow. 
</Note>

</Frame>

### Step 5: Identify if the leak can be contained

1. Can you contain the leak in a bucket in the meantime so that somebody can go out and assess it? 
2. If so, would the bucket be filled up within the first 24 hours? 
3.  Is the leak intermittent, or does it persist consistently?

### Step 6: If you were able to shut off the individual valve or this is a minor leak, review the property's intake settings and determine if this should be de-escalated. If this is still an emergency go to the next step.

### Step 7: Locate the main shutoff valve if the leak cannot be contained.

1. Can you shut off the main water supply to the house while we send a plumber out? 
2. Can you locate the main water valve to the house? It's usually close to your water meter or in the garage/ basement.
3. Have you experienced any recent plumbing work or changes in the water system?

<Note>
For any active leaks coming from the plumbing, **immediately **shut off the water supply - usually located in the basement or outside of the property. 
</Note>

The main shutoff valve in the house probably has one of two designs:

- **Gate valve:** Gate valves are very reliable and last for years, but they become difficult to turn after not being turned for years.
- **Ball valve:** Houses with plastic or copper main water pipes leading into the house may have a full-flow ball valve. This valve is open when the handle is aligned with the pipe. To close it, turn the handle clockwise 1/4 turn so that it’s at a right angle to the pipe.

### Step 8: Immediately soak up any standing water to prevent mold.

1. Are you able to mop the water that leaked? 
2. Could you use towels or something similar to soak up the remaining water?  
 

<Warning>
**Attention**: If there is standing water and it cannot be soaked or mopped up by the tenant we need to create a separate work order for water mitigation. 
</Warning>

### Step 9: Request pictures or videos (If a video call was made, pictures might not be necessary)

1. Ask the customer to send photos or videos of the issue via SMS to document the issue and upload them in the files tab for the work order. See: [Job-Aid: Blurbs or Templates to Communicate with Customers](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/2028470664/Job-Aid+Blurbs+or+Templates+to+Communicate+with+Customers#To---Residents-(R))

**Request photos/videos of:**

- The source of the water leak. 
- Any standing water or anything that might have been affected by the leak. 
- A video showing how fast the water is leaking.

### Step 10: Dispatch a service provider ASAP.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Clear Sewage Backup">
### Step 1: Ask probing questions

1. Is this a common issue or is this the first time it has happened?
2. Can you describe specifically what is happening and how you noticed the issue?

### Step 2: Check all sinks, toilets, and wastewater pipes to locate any blockages and determine if the issue is isolated to one drain or toilet, or if it’s impacting all the drains and toilets on the property.

1. Can you please check your drains and toilets and try to identify if the issue is happening in multiple places or just on one drain?

### Step 3: If only one drain is affected, review the property's intake settings and determine if this should be de-escalated. If this is still an emergency go to the next step.

### Step 4: Investigate if the property is on city sewer or a septic tank.

1. Can you tell me if the property is running on city water or a septic tank? 
2. If the tenant is unsure, check the water bill and search for a sewer wastewater fee. If they have this on the bill, they are on city water. If they don't have this or it comes out at $0, they are on a septic tank. If they cannot locate the water bill, have the tenant look outside in the street and if they see manholes that say sanitary on them, this means they are most likely on city sewer.

### Step 5: Request pictures (Only if it’s a visible sewage backup)

1. Ask the customer to send photos or videos of the issue via SMS to document the issue and upload them in the files tab for the work order. **Request photos/videos of:**

- The source of the backup. 
- Any standing water/sewage or anything that might have been affected by the backup.

### Step 6: Ask the tenant to refrain from using any water at all and dispatch a service provider ASAP.

<Accordion title="Water heater not working">
### Step 1: Ask probing questions

1. Is this a common issue or is this the first time it has happened?
2. Can you describe specifically what is happening and how you noticed the issue?
3. Is the lack of hot water affecting the entire building or just your unit? (Only applicable if this is a building)
4. Have you checked if your neighbors are experiencing the same issue? (Only applicable if this is a building)
  1. For **duplicate issues affecting building-wide incidents**, refer to: https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/SOP-Building-Wide-Emergency-Work-Orders

🔗 **Cross-reference — Fire Hazard / Power Shutoff Assessment:**  
If the resident reports the electric water heater is simply "off" (not actively misbehaving) and you're considering de-escalation, use the Fire Hazard / Power Shutoff workflow in https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Job-Aid-Troubleshooter-Tips-for-Tricky-Situations. A water heater turned off at the dial or thermostat still has power supplied — 'system off' does not mean power is disconnected at the breaker. If there are signs of electrical malfunction (burning smell, sparking, smoke, visible damage), this meets Fire Hazard / PD criteria — follow PM's intake settings and escalate to PM's Escalation Contacts per standard PD/EH process if the WO is non-ER.

### Step 2: Make adjustments and check the pilot light

<Warning>
**ATTENTION**: Relighting the pilot light can be dangerous and the optimal way to do it is to check the owner's manual to find specific instructions. We can also go through the following general instructions if the customer agrees. Only attempt to relight it if the unit has a push-button ignitor AND the customer is comfortable with it. **DO NOT ATTEMPT TO RELIGHT IF THERE IS A GAS SMELL**. Have the customer contact the gas company or fire department.
</Warning>

1. If it is not heating, check the breaker to ensure it hasn’t been tripped. 
2. Also, check the pilot light to ensure it hasn’t gone out. If the pilot has gone out you can re-ignite it:
  1. ⚠️ **Open Flame Prohibition:** Before proceeding, confirm with the resident: "Does your water heater have a push-button ignitor?" If it requires a match or long lighter to ignite, **STOP — **do not proceed with relighting. **Proceed with the next applicable troubleshooting steps.** Only continue with the steps below if the resident confirms a push-button ignitor.
    1. **If the resident says YES:**  
“Thank you. Since your water heater has a push-button ignitor, we can continue with the safe relighting steps.”
**If the resident says NO:**  
“Thank you for confirming. Since it does not have a push-button ignitor, please do not try to relight it with a match or long lighter. We’ll arrange for the appropriate assistance.”
  2. First, turn off the switch to the gas before igniting the flame. This is usually a knob labeled “ON/OFF/PILOT”
  3. Turn the knob to the PILOT position and push down to release the gas. 
  4. At the same time, push the ignite button and you should hear a whoosh sound. 
  5. Check that the flame is lit and turn the knob back to the ON position. 
  6. If the unit does NOT have a push-button ignitor, do NOT instruct the resident to use an open flame. **Proceed with the next applicable troubleshooting steps.**.
  7. If the pilot does not light within a few seconds, turn the gas off and try again. 

<Warning>
**Warning: DO NOT** leave the gas running for a long amount of time. Residents could be at risk of a gas explosion. 
</Warning>

If the water is not hot enough, you can adjust the temperature on the water heater, though water heaters should not typically exceed 140 degrees Fahrenheit

1. Ask the customer to send photos or videos of the issue via SMS to document the issue and upload them in the files tab for the work order. 2. Have you noticed any issues while using the hot water on the property?

### Step 2: Identify the severity of the leak

1. From 1 to 10 how bad would you say it's leaking?
2. Is the water leak causing any property damage?
3. Could you send me a video of the leak and any damage it is causing?
4. Would you like to switch to a video call so we can look at the leak together?

### Step 3: Identify what type of water heater is on the property

1. Can you identify if this is an electrical or gas water heater?
  1. Gas: A blue flame pilot light, 0.5-inch black pipe or a 0.25-inch copper pipe connecting to the bottom of your heater, or a PVC pipe of around 3 to 4 inches in diameter are all things to look for to identify a gas water heater
  2. Electrical: Look for a black or grey electrical cable connecting to your heater or an electric center break labeled water heater in the breaker box to identify an electrical water heater.

### Step 4: If this is a gas water heater shut off the gas. If electric, shut off the electricity to the heater.

1. Gas: Twist the dial on the top of the thermostat from the “on” to the “off” position. This will turn off the gas at the water heater. If you ever smell gas – exit the home and contact the gas company immediately.
2. Electric: Find your breaker box and flip the breaker for your water heater or if your water heater has a disconnect switch close to the water heater, simply turn off the switch to turn off power to the water heater.

⚠️ **Fire Hazard check****:** If the resident reports any signs of electrical malfunction (burning smell, sparking, smoke, visible damage) in addition to the leak — this may be a Fire Hazard / PD situation regardless of whether the resident says the unit is "off." Refer to the Fire Hazard / Power Shutoff classification in https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Identify-Property-Damage-and-Extreme-Habitability-Situations and ensure power is confirmed disconnected at the breaker, not just at the unit's dial/thermostat.

### Step 5: Shut off the water supply to the water heater

1. Find the cold side water pipe connected to the water heater and follow the water pipe back to the source or the wall – you should see a water shut-off valve.
2. Gate Valve: If you have an old-style turn faucet handle – Twist the handle clockwise until it stops. This should turn off the water.
3. Ball Valve: If you have a newer lever-style ball valve pull or push the handle a quarter turn – this should turn off the water.

### Step 6: Drain the water heater (Only if the resident is comfortable!)

1. Drain the heater by connecting a garden hose to the bottom drain valve on the water heater. Run the other end of the hose to a lower location where hot or rusty water will not cause damage (hot water can damage grass and plants). Open the hot side faucet in the sink closest to the water heater – this will release the vacuum seal and let the water drain out of the water heater. Allow 10-30 minutes for the water heater to drain.
2. If the tenant does not want to drain the heater you can have them leave a hot water faucet open for a few minutes to drain as much as possible.

1. Are you able to mop the water that leaked?
2. Could you use towels or something similar to soak up the remaining water?

<Accordion title="Clogged Toilet">
### Step 1: Ask probing questions

1. Is this a common issue or is this the first time it has happened?
2. Can you describe specifically what is happening and how you noticed the issue?
3. Does the water drain slowly or is it completely blocked?
4. Check if there have been any issues with water pressure in the house recently, as this might affect toilet flushing.

### Step 2: Ask if there are any other toilets on the property

1. Are there any other toilets on the property or is this the only one?
2. Check if there have been any issues with water pressure in the house recently, as this might affect toilet flushing.

<Warning>
**Attention: **Even if there are other toilets on the property, we should still have the tenant inspect the toilets for any possible leaks. If there are leaks, proceed to the “*Leaking toilet*” section. 
</Warning>

### Step 3: Ask for accidentally dropped items in the toilet bowl

1. Would you happen to know if something could have been accidentally dropped in the toilet?

### Step 4: Turn off the water at the valve

1. Before using the plunger, turn off the water to stop the toilet from overflowing. The valve is usually located under the toilet tank.

### Step 5: Try plunging and/or snaking the toilet

1. Have you tried plunging or snaking the toilet to see if it goes through?
2. Can you try to plunge and/or snake the toilet several times and let me know if it seems to go down?

**Proper plunging and diagnosing instructions:**

<Warning>
**Attention: **

- **Advise the tenant to avoid using harsh chemicals or objects that could exacerbate the clog or damage the plumbing system further. Emphasize safety precautions such as wearing gloves and protective eyewear when handling cleaning agents or tools.**
</Warning>

1. If the toilet bowl is full, put on gloves and use something to bail out the water into a bucket until the bowl is only half full. 
2. If the bowl is empty, add water to fill the bowl to halfway mark. 
3. With the plunger completely underwater, fit the plunger completely over the bowl drain. 
4. Press and pull the plunger rapidly up and down 15-20 times. 
5. If water drains out of the bowl, add more water and repeat. 
6. If the water seems to be draining, try flushing the toilet. 

If the toilet is not clogged but won’t flush, open the lid of the back of the toilet to make sure the flusher is still connected to the water drainer in the toilet tank. 

If the toilet is constantly running, it is likely also due to the chain being disconnected. 

<Frame>
  ![](/images/SVC-807.png)
</Frame>

### Step 6: Alternatives (Try one only)

1. **Hot water & soap**: Pour around 1/4 of a cup of dish soap into the toilet and let it sit for 10 minutes. Then heat some water or use tap water, but don’t let the water boil. Pour the hot water into the toilet and give it some time to sit. Then flush the toilets at least 2 times to make sure it’s clear. 
2. **Backing soda & vinegar**: Pour one cup of baking soda and one cup of vinegar down the toilet drain. After about thirty minutes, follow up with some hot water and see if it drains.

<Warning>
**Attention: **When following one of these steps, do not hang up and offer to call back to see if it worked. Instead, continue to the next step and ask the customer to contact us right back via phone or SMS to cancel the dispatch. 
</Warning>

### Step 7: Request pictures 

1. Ask the customer to send photos or videos of the issue via SMS to document the issue and upload them in the files tab for the work order. See: [Job-Aid: Blurbs or Templates to Communicate with Customers](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/2028470664/Job-Aid+Blurbs+or+Templates+to+Communicate+with+Customers#To---Residents-(R))

**Request photos/videos of:**

- **Request photos/videos of:**
  - Any visible sign of the clog in the toilet.
  - Any potential property damage with possible overflows

### Step 3: Plunge/snake the drain 

First, verify if the Resident has been sent a Drain Pack by reviewing the Resident’s Critical Note. 

<Frame>
  ![](/images/SVC-814.png)
</Frame>

The Drain Pack is a Resident Benefit offered by Latchel intended to resolve clogged drains. For more information on how to use the Drain Pack, refer to [Resident-Benefit-Drain-Pack-Instructions.](https://help.latchel.com/s/article/Resident-Benefit-Drain-Pack-Instructions)

1. Ask the Resident if they have the Drain Pack that was sent previously.

3. Stand by on the call until the resident finishes trying to unclog the drain with the Drain Pack.

Proceed to the steps below if the Resident wasn’t successful or if they don’t have a Drain Pack.

**Sinks: **

1. Fill the sink up to half with hot water and plunge it using a sink plunger. If this is a double sink, cover the drain in the second basin with a wet cloth to help create pressure within the pipe. 
2. Use a plumbing snake to try and clear the drain.
3. If plunging/snaking did not work, clean out the P-trap (the curved portion of the pipe under the sink). Place a bucket under the pipe and loosen it from both ends. Then remove the P-trap and clean as much as you can. Once this is done reinstall it and run your sink. 

**Other drains:**

1. Remove the internal stopper if there is any. Look to remove hair or gunk from the stopper. 
2. Use a plumbing snake to try and clear the drain.
3. Use a plunger to plunge the drain.

**Clogged sink drain:**

1. Try all of the below
2. If the customer is comfortable, you can have them detach the piping underneath the sink and pull out the portion that’s clogged. There will be plenty of gunk to clean out from both the piping and the drain pulled up from out of the sink. 
3. Place a large bucket below the trap. This will catch any water or debris that falls from the pipe once you remove the trap.
4. Use slip joint pliers to loosen the slip nuts on the trap and unscrew the nuts by hand. Slide the nuts away from the connections and carefully slip off the trap.
5. Use a small wire brush to scrap out any debris from the trap, emptying it into the bucket below. Also, clean the trap out by scrubbing with the same wire brush.
6. Carefully rinse the trap with hot water. You may want to use another sink since you just removed a portion of the drain pipe for the sink you are currently working on.
7. **After Cleaning the Trap – Reattachment and Follow-Up: Inspect the Slip Nut Washers:**  
Before reattaching the trap, check each slip nut washer for wear, cracking, or brittleness. If any are damaged, replace them to ensure a tight seal.
  1. **Align the Trap Properly:**  
Make sure the trap is oriented the same way it was before removal. The curved portion should match the previous positioning to allow proper water flow and prevent leaks.
  2. **Hand-Tighten the Slip Nuts:**  
Slide the slip nuts back toward the pipe connections and hand-tighten them. Do not overtighten, as this can crack the fittings or strip the threads.
  3. **Test for Leaks:**
    - Slowly run water into the sink.
    - Watch the trap closely while water flows through it.
    - If you see any drips or moisture forming around the connections, try tightening the slip nuts slightly more with your hand (or very gently with pliers if needed).
  4. **Dry Towel Check:**
    - Wipe down the trap area with a dry towel before testing.
    - Once water has run through the pipe, use the towel again to check if any moisture has appeared, which might indicate a slow leak.

### **Step 2: Inspect the Door Tracks and Rollers**

1. Do the tracks appear to be properly aligned?
2. Is there any visible damage, such as dents or broken components, on the garage door or its mechanisms?
3. Are the rollers moving smoothly without any noticeable resistance or sticking?

### **Step 3: Check the Sensors**

1. Ask the tenant to locate the sensors near the bottom of the garage door, about 6 inches above the ground.
2. **Check the sensor lights**:
  1. The **sending sensor** usually has an **amber/yellow light** that indicates it’s sending a signal.
  2. The **receiving sensor** should show a **green light**, meaning it’s receiving the signal and the sensors are aligned.
  3. If the lights are **off or blinking**, the sensors may be misaligned or blocked.
3. **Ask the tenant** to ensure the sensors are facing each other and gently realign them if necessary until the lights are steady.

### **Step 4: Power Troubleshooting – GFCI Reset**

> ⚠️ **Important:** Proceed with this step **only if the resident agrees** to troubleshoot. **Do not insist** if the outlet is difficult to access or the resident is uncomfortable.

If the resident mentions that the garage door opener is **completely unresponsive** (no lights, no sound, no movement), explain that the issue may be due to a **tripped GFCI outlet**, which can interrupt power to the opener.

1. Instruct the resident to **locate the GFCI outlet in the garage**. It typically has **“Test” and “Reset” buttons** and may control power to nearby outlets, including the opener.
2. Let them know the GFCI outlet might be **partially hidden** — often found **behind a refrigerator, freezer, or workbench**.
3. Once located, guide the resident to:
  - **Press the "Reset" button** on the GFCI outlet.
  - Then check if the garage door opener now has power or functions again.
4. If the opener works after the reset, no further action is needed.
5. If the resident **cannot locate the outlet** or the reset **does not restore power**, continue with the next steps

### **Step 5: Check the Garage Door Springs**

1. Are the springs intact and not broken?
2. Do the springs appear to be under tension, as they should be?

### **Step 6: Inspect the Cables and Hardware**

1. Are the cables intact and not frayed or broken?
2. Is all the hardware (hinges, brackets, etc.) securely fastened?
3. Do any of the components appear to be loose or worn out?

### **Step 7: Emergency Release and Safety**

1. Ask the resident to locate any red string or cord that might be an emergency release to manually operate the door.
2. If there is any visible damage, advise the resident not to attempt to close the door and to avoid using the emergency release.
3. Is the garage door issue preventing your home from being properly secured?
4. Is the garage or home currently accessible/unsecured?

1. If the issue persists and cannot be resolved through these steps, contact a professional garage door repair service.
2. Inform the property manager about the issue if they are responsible for maintenance.

<Accordion title="Snow & Freeze-Related Incidents">
<Warning>
**Attention: **Snow accumulation during winter storms can cause structural damage, accessibility issues, and frozen pipes. Always offer the customer to initiate a video call when there is a chance so you can guide them through the assessment and document the issue at the same time.

**Recommended Hub:** *"I understand you're dealing with snow accumulation at your property. I want to help assess the situation and get you the right assistance as quickly as possible. If you agree, we can hop on a quick video call so you can show me what's happening and I'll guide you through some questions to determine the best response. We don't have to be on camera, you can just show me the issue and I'll walk you through it."*
</Warning>

### **Step 1: Ask probing questions**

1. Is there any immediate danger right now (roof sagging, cracking sounds, someone trapped, emergency exits blocked)?
2. What's your main concern (roof snow load, blocked access, water coming in, equipment buried, frozen pipes)?
3. Approximately how much snow has accumulated (estimated in inches or described as light, moderate, or heavy)?

If applicable, follow existing troubleshooting workflows:

- **No heat reported:** Refer to *No Heat* Troubleshooting Steps
- **Burst pipe reported:** Refer to *Water Leak* Troubleshooting Steps

### **Step 2: Identify the severity and urgency**

1. On a scale of 1 to 10, how severe would you say the situation is right now (e.g., snow accumulation, water intrusion, access issues)?
2. Is anyone currently unable to safely evacuate, enter, or access essential services?
3. Could you send a photo or video of the affected area?
4. Would you like to switch to a video call so we can assess this together in real time?

### **Step 3: Identify the specific issue type**

Based on the resident’s responses, ask the appropriate questions below to clarify the exact issue:

**For Roof Concerns:**

1. Can you describe your roof type (flat, sloped, or unsure)?
2. Do you see or hear anything concerning (e.g., sagging ceilings, doors or windows that will not open, cracking sounds, or water stains)?

**For Access Issues:**

1. What is currently blocked (no exit at all, main door/driveway, or partial/inconvenient access)?
2. Do you need to leave urgently (e.g., medical appointment, essential travel), or is this routine access?

**For Water Intrusion:**

1. Where is the water coming from (ceiling or walls, windows or doors, or basement)?
2. Is the water actively leaking at this time?

**For Frozen Pipes:**

1. Do you have any water at all (no water, slow trickle, or only certain faucets)?
2. Do you see water damage or hear dripping anywhere?

### **Step 4: Provide Immediate Guidance Based on Issue Type**

Provide the appropriate safety guidance below, based on the identified issue. Emphasize resident safety and advise only actions that can be performed safely.

**For Roof Concerns:**

1. Stay out of rooms directly beneath areas with the heaviest snow accumulation.
2. Do not attempt to climb onto or clear snow from the roof yourself.

**For Blocked Access:**

1. If another safe exit is available, use that exit temporarily.
2. Avoid piling snow against the house or exterior walls when clearing.

**For Icy Walkways or Steps:**

1. Apply salt, sand, or kitty litter to improve traction.
2. Avoid using hot water, as it will refreeze and worsen icing conditions.
3. Clear snow before it becomes compacted and turns to ice.

**For Light Snow on Walkways:**

1. Use a shovel or broom to clear snow as soon as possible.
2. Push snow away from the house and doorways.
3. Create a clear path at least three (3) feet wide when possible.

**For Water Intrusion:**

1. Place buckets or containers under any active leaks.
2. If water is near electrical outlets or fixtures, shut off power to the affected area if it can be done safely.

**For Frozen Pipes (No Visible Water Damage):**

1. Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls to allow warm air circulation.
2. To help prevent freezing, run both hot and cold water at a slow drip at one upstairs faucet and one downstairs faucet.
3. If safely accessible, use a hair dryer on the LOW setting to gently warm the pipe.
4. Maintain indoor heat at a minimum of 55°F.
5. Never use open flames, propane heaters, or high-heat devices.

**For Frozen Pipes (Visible Water Damage / Burst Pipe):**

1. Shut off the main water supply immediately.
2. If water nears electrical outlets or fixtures, safely shut off electricity to those areas
3. Place buckets, towels, or containers to help control water spread.

### **Step 5: Request photos/videos (if a video call was not made)**

Ask the customer to send photos or videos of the issue via SMS to document and upload them in the files tab for the work order.

**Request photos/videos of:**

- Overall view showing snow depth and affected areas
- Roof conditions (if safely visible from ground)
- Any blocked access points (doors, walkways, driveways)
- Any structural concerns or damage
- Buried equipment or utilities
- For frozen pipes: the frozen pipe location and any water damage
- Reference objects for scale

### **Step 6: Determine priority level based on intake settings**

Review the property's intake settings and severity assessment to determine the appropriate response timeframe.

**Emergency/ PD/EH:**

- Roof sagging, cracking sounds, or signs of structural stress
- All exits are blocked 
- Burst pipe with active flooding or water damage
- No heat during freezing temperatures
- Active structural damage

**Standard indicators:**

- Routine snow clearing
- One safe exit is fully accessible
- Preventive roof clearing 
- Moderate snow accumulation

<Warning>
** Attention:** Due to snow blockages and hazardous road conditions, service providers may experience delays. Their safety is our priority. Please be patient as they work to reach your property safely. Response times may be longer than usual during active winter weather events.
</Warning>

### **Step 7: Document the issue**

In the work order document:

- Primary issue type (roof, access, water intrusion, equipment, frozen pipes)
- Safety concerns identified (yes/no, with brief details if applicable)
- Priority level assigned
- Estimated snow depth or accumulation level
- Photos and/or videos received (yes/no)
- Interior temperature (for frozen pipe scenarios)
- Road and property access conditions (if reported)
- Special circumstances impacting urgency (e.g., resident vulnerability, elderly, medical needs)

### **Step 8: Dispatch service provider based on priority level**

- Proceed with dispatch according to the** property manager's intake settings **and the priority level determined in **Step 6.**
- When dispatching, note any road or access concerns for the service provider's safety.
</Accordion>
