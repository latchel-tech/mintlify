## 🧠 What This Process Is Trying to Solve

<Note>
  We want to prevent confusion, missed information, and poor customer experiences during outbound calls to residents, vendors, or property managers. The goal is to ensure every outbound call follows a clear, step-by-step flow—covering proper identification, clear communication of purpose, active listening, thorough note-taking, and setting expectations for next steps—so that every customer feels heard, informed, and supported.
</Note>

<Note>
  **🎓Training Content**

  The contents of this page have been included in the \*\*Call Center Cross-Training \*\*Coassemble course.
</Note>

## **🗂️Process Overview**

In this job aid, agents will find the required steps to follow for a successful customer-obsessed call. Some steps are followed by an example. To see a guide on how to make a call from the Latchel App, [click here.](/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/TECH-101-How-to-Make-Outbound-Calls-Within-the-Latchel-Console)

<Note>
  To ensure a good user experience with Latchel, please call Residents, Vendors, and Property Managers during business time. That is, <u>Monday through Sunday from 8:00am to 5:00pm property time</u>.

  The only exception for this are emergency and property damaging/extreme habitability issues or if the resident requested for it to submit a work order.. For acceptable operations hours, please refer to [Informational: Basic Service Ops Guidelines and Rules](/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documention/informational-basic-service-ops-guidelines-and-rules).
</Note>

<Note>
  Before placing the outbound, double-check for any Critical Notes (refer to [How to: Navigate the Work Order Interface](/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/how-to-navigate-the-work-order-interface)) to identify any communication preferences for this customer, for example, some Service Providers may not be available during certain timeframes or some Residents may not speak English.
</Note>

<Note>
  If you are placed on hold by the customer or encounter an indefinite hold during a call, please refer to [(PREMIUM) How to: Handle Phone Contacts](/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/premium-how-to-handle-phone-contacts) for guidance.
</Note>

## 📋 Step-by-Step Process

<Note>
  ### Placing Customer on "Hold"

  If there is a need to place customer on “hold” or if we will keep the customer waiting while researching or working on the solution, please let the customer know you will take a couple of minutes to research. If “ hold” is taking too long, go back with the customer every 2 mins to thank for waiting, let him/her know what you are doing, and that you will need more time.
</Note>

### 1. Identify yourself and the company mentioning that the call will be recorded.

At the beginning of your call start by greeting the customer warmly. Then, identify yourself by saying your first name followed by the company. Also state that the call is recorded. Keep a warm and friendly tone at all times. The approach to present the company will depend on the person you are calling; please see the guidelines below.

<Warning>
  Federal law requires that at least one party taking part in the call must be notified of the recording.
</Warning>

### 2. Say why you’re calling.

State your intentions and what you require from the caller. This will allow the customer to know what are your expectations from them. Be specific and clear.

<Callout icon="note" iconType="duotone" color="#b19cf8">
  **To know what information you require, ask yourself some basic questions such as:**

  - _Who?_
  - _What?_
  - _When?_
  - _How?_
</Callout>

### 3. Use the customer’s name throughout the call.

Ask for the resident’s name or retrieve it from our console and note it if necessary. Repeating a customer’s name will create rapport and will ease communication.

Don't assume gender. Instead of asking for Mr./Mrs. \[last name\], just ask for their first name.

### 4. Take notes of important information.

Write as many notes as possible to gather as much information as possible. At the end of the call, make sure your notes are clear to read for everyone in our team. Gather enough information for anyone who will read the information after you.

### 5.  Ask questions to clarify information.

If the customer doesn’t provide you with enough information, feel free to ask the customer. Asking questions to gather details is an essential practice, especially when troubleshooting.

<Check>
  Ask as many questions and possible. If possible, request for a picture. For troubleshooting calls, refer to the probing question bank linked at the end of this job aid.
</Check>

### 6.  Repeat (paraphrase) and summarize.

Repeating the information back to your customer works as a proof that you listened to their concern and to confirm we got the information right. Feel free to add more information if the resident adds any at this time.

### 7. Offer further assistance or offer contact information.

Offering further assistance is a caring touch that distinguishes good customer service. It makes a customer feel listened to. Moreover, this ensures we have information in full.

There are situations (i.e. we are the ones asking for help) where it is extremely complicated to offer further assistance. In these situations please provide the caller with our phone number. If the caller is familiar with Latchel, remind them they can text us.

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Column>
    <Check>

    </Check>
  </Column>
  <Column>
    <Warning>
      Avoid using 100% scripted offers. Callers can be used to them and they will not feel authentic.
    </Warning>

    <Warning>
      Be careful with your tone. A tense or unpleasant tone can make your offer sound like a way to end the call.
    </Warning>
  </Column>
</Columns>

### 8. Tell the customer what you’re going to do.

Customers expect action after a follow-up. Please state the actions you will take after the call. This will assure the resident that we are taking care of their issue.

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Column>
    <Check>
      If a Work Order is Forwarded to PM, please tell the resident what are the next expected steps. Be clear that the Property Manager is taking action. Reassure the resident they can call us later on to keep following up in no answer is received.
    </Check>
  </Column>
  <Column>
    <Danger>
      Please avoid making promises beyond your field of action. Promising things we cannot deliver is not customer obsessed and may lead to customer’s frustration.
    </Danger>
  </Column>
</Columns>

### 9. Make the customer knows what’s coming next.

After you’ve stated the actions to take, tell the customer what to expect next from their point of view. Seeing the Work Order from the resident’s eyes allows us to see the full picture and ensure things are on the right track. This will ensure the resident that calling Latchel was the right thing to do, as they see how action will be taken.

<Check>
  Tell the customer what are you doing next. Be explicit of the actions you will take and what are the possible outcomes.
</Check>

<Danger>
  Do not give Contact Information of Property Managers. Refer to [Informational: Basic Service Ops Guidelines and Rules](/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/informational-basic-service-ops-guidelines-and-rules)  For vendors, please make sure they agree with sharing their contact information before doing it.
</Danger>

### 10. Thank the customer.

A little appreciation goes a long way. In certain situations, issues will make our customers feel frustrated. Thanking them for brining the issue to our attention and calling us for assistance is a nice touch that can improve the interaction overall.

<Note>
  **Example for Step 10:**

<Note>
  If the customer puts you on hold on AND you haven't heard back from the customer in 3 minutes, you may hang up. However, prior to hanging up, do the following:

  -Try to get a response from the customer by asking “Hello \[customer name\], are you still there?”.

  -If no response is received, advise the customer you’ll hang up due to not receiving a response “\[customer name\], I have to end the call since I haven’t heard back from you in 3 minutes, feel free to contact us back, thank you!”.
</Note>

## **🎯 When to Break This SOP**

**Do not break or deviate from this SOP on your own authority.** Always escalate to an Ops Support specialist or follow explicit instructions if you believe an exception is necessary.
