<Note>
  We want to prevent emergency work orders from getting stuck in incorrect or outdated statuses, which can lead to delays, missed follow-ups, and unresolved issues. The goal is to ensure every emergency work order is actively managed and in the correct status, so that urgent problems are addressed promptly, responsibilities are clear, and nothing falls through the cracks. This process helps us catch and correct work orders that might otherwise be overlooked, saving time, reducing risk, and improving service for residents and property managers alike.
</Note>

### Two Different Severity Judgments

<Check>
  ### **Guiding Principle & Severity Re-evaluation**

  There are two separate judgment calls in this process, and this SOP covers the second one:

  1. **Initial severity at intake** — governed by the AI judgment / intake policy and PM severity settings. This determines whether a job is created as an Emergency in the first place. This SOP does not change intake policy; intake settings dictate job creation, while this SOP governs how Ops manages an already-created WO that later sits in queue.
  2. **Ongoing severity while a job sits in queue** — covered by this SOP. Even when the initial Emergency classification was correct, Ops must reassess whether the WO is still being handled on an active emergency path. Re-evaluating it at that point is a distinct, later-stage operational decision — not a correction of the original intake call. This has two directions, and they use different tests:
     1. _De-escalating (Emergency → Urgent):_ an operational judgment that applies when the WO is no longer moving through active emergency handling. This includes cases where the immediate emergency condition has passed, the PM has taken ownership, the WO is in an intentional or open-ended delay, the PM has imposed scheduling/provider constraints, or follow-up has not produced concrete forward progress. In those cases, moving the WO out of Emergency reflects its current handling path, even if the original intake settings would still classify the issue as an emergency.
     2. _Re-escalating (after a resident follows up on an already de-escalated WO):_ reassess based on what the resident is reporting about the **current situation**. The key question is whether the resident is providing new information or describing a material change from the situation that existed when the WO was de-escalated — such as worsening conditions, new/spreading damage, a new safety concern, or another update that changes the current handling need. Do not re-escalate just because the WO qualified as an Emergency before, and do not keep it Urgent if the current report now shows an active emergency condition. Refer to the FAQ below for the full resident follow-up workflow.

  **Guiding principle:**

  Intentional delays and active emergency handling are fundamentally incompatible. When active emergency progress has halted, de-escalating, forwarding to PM, or actively intervening according to this SOP is the correct and expected action.
</Check>

<Warning>
  **De-escalation Documentation & Title Formatting**

  This callout is only meant to establish the documentation principle: when Ops intentionally de-escalates an Emergency WO during Bad Status / Too Old review, the reason must be clear in the WO so the next person can tell it was reviewed and intentionally moved forward.

  For the exact scenario-specific steps — including when to retain **"(EMERGENCY)"** in the WO title, when to complete the current task, and what internal note or routing action is required — refer to the matching FAQ scenario below.

  **Internal Note (Mandatory):** Leave an internal note documenting why the WO was de-escalated.

  **Standard Internal Note Template:**

  _De-escalated from Emergency to Urgent — \[reason, e.g., "PM scheduled provider for \[date\]" / "no active damage per resident"\]. Flagging for team visibility — reach out before re-escalating unless resident reports new/worsening damage._
</Warning>

<Note>
  **Task Routing Rule**

  This callout is only meant to establish the task-routing principle: do not create a task solely because a WO was de-escalated. A task is only needed when there is a specific pending follow-up action and a clear owner.

  For the exact routing decision by scenario — such as PM ownership, SP follow-up, resident follow-up, emergency-team handoff, subscription-specific routing, or On-Call handling — refer to the matching FAQ scenario below.
</Note>

## ⬅️ What is happening prior to this process?

Operations Support Specialist works from the plugin’s Bad Status report. The plugin is the single source of truth for this SOP. These are jobs showing a status that does not make sense for emergency Work Orders or that require manual review because they may be stuck.

## 📋 Step-by-Step Process

<Note>
  General Rules

  - If the issue is not genuinely an emergency, don't sit on it — inspect it and move it forward: de-escalate and update the WO. For required de-escalation documentation, title formatting, and task-routing rules, refer to the callout panels above.
  - If the PM explicitly asks us to treat a work order as an emergency and allows Latchel to obtain a confirmed appointment or alternative provider, treat it as an Emergency. If the PM requires use of one unavailable provider and does not authorize an alternative, escalate the conflict to PS team and de-escalate to Urgent while following the de-escalation documentation and title-formatting rules above.
  - Follow-up attempts (to an SP or a resident) are capped at 1 attempt before acting on the outcome the same day — no multi-day deferrals or "try again tomorrow" cycles.
  - PM Critical Notes / SP Restrictions: If a PM critical note restricts contacting the SP or states the PM/SP is handling scheduling directly, do not force SP contact attempts. Refer to the **FAQ section** for scenario-specific de-escalation steps.

The PM’s original severity setting reflects the situation at intake; it does not override the need to re-evaluate a job that has aged or stalled in the Bad Status process.

  **Note**: Reclassifying a WO from Emergency to Urgent automatically lowers its approved budget limit, since budget ceilings are tied to severity (e.g., a job not scheduled within 24 hours has its budget adjusted down automatically once no longer Emergency). </Note>

  <Tab title="Re/Scheduling with Tenant">
    **Action:** If the scheduled follow-up with the tenant has not resulted in a confirmed appointment, apply the 3-way decision framework:

    - **Intervene** — attempt to schedule directly and get the WO moving today.
    - If unsuccessful, **de-escalate to Urgent** when the immediate emergency condition has passed and there is no concrete near-term resolution date.
    - **Forward to PM** when PM input, approval, or prior PM instructions are needed.

    **Why is this considered "bad status/too old"?**: The concern is when the WO remains in this status after follow-up has not resulted in a confirmed appointment or concrete ETA. At that point, the WO needs active review instead of remaining in scheduling indefinitely.

    **Action:** If the scheduled follow-up with the SP has not resulted in a confirmed appointment or concrete ETA, apply the 3-way decision framework:

    1. **Intervene** — chase a concrete ETA/date or reassign the vendor if appropriate.
    2. If unsuccessful, **de-escalate to Urgent** when the immediate emergency condition has passed and there is no concrete near-term resolution date.
    3. **Forward to PM** when the PM has taken ownership, assigned their own SP, or Ops-side action requires PM input/approval.

    **Why is this considered "bad status/too old"?:** The concern is when the WO remains in this status after follow-up has not resulted in a confirmed appointment or concrete ETA. At that point, the WO needs active review instead of remaining in scheduling indefinitely.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Finding Vendor">
    **Action:**

    1. Don't do anything if it’s been less than 1 hour since the WO was updated to this status.
    2. After 1 hour has passed since this WO was updated to Finding Vendor status, follow up with the troubleshooter working on this WO to check if the troubleshooter may require assistance with the WO.
    3. If the WO is found abandoned and no one is working on it, create a task for the emergency team so that another troubleshooter can pick up where the previous troubleshooter left off.
       1. Submit feedback to the agent who left without passing the WO off in #ops-feedback.

    **Why is this considered "bad status/too old"?:** This would be considered an aging WO for emergency response standards.

    **Exception**: There will be instances where an emergency WO is left in this status for deferred dispatch and an automated task is created for troubleshooters by 9:00 AM property time. No action is required for these cases.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Pending">
    **Action**: Keep the current sub-scenario guidance below. If none of the listed sub-scenarios apply and the existing follow-up has produced no activity or response, treat the WO as a candidate for the 3-way decision framework: Intervene, De-escalate to Urgent, or Forward to PM.

    **When the resident reports an issue with the WO:**

    - Review the issue and create a task for the emergency team to follow up with the resident and the service provider. This is very common when the issue was not resolved.

    **When a RentRedi WO doesn’t have an owner payment method:**

    - Review the issue and create a task for the emergency team so the emergency team can follow up with the owner to request a payment method.

    **When we need to collect an estimate for approval:**

    - Review the issue and create a task for the emergency team so the emergency team can follow up with the Service Provider for the estimate.

    **When the Service Provider reports an issue with the job, for example, work was denied on site, SP needs to provide an estimate, SP could not access the unit, or SP missed the appointment:**

    - Review the issue and create a task for the emergency team so the emergency team can follow up with the Service Provider.

    **Why is this considered "bad status/too old"?:** An emergency WO in this status needs manual follow-up. If follow-up does not produce progress, Ops should not leave it pending indefinitely.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Waiting for Materials">
    **Action:**

    1. Contact the SP for a firm ETA on the materials.
    2. If the SP provides an ETA and confirms they will install promptly once materials arrive, lock in a scheduled installation appointment for that date. The WO stays on an active, concrete resolution path and does not require de-escalation solely because it is waiting for materials.
    3. De-escalate to Urgent if either: the SP responds but cannot provide an ETA or will not commit to prompt installation once materials arrive; or the SP is unresponsive with no ETA secured after repeated follow-up attempts. In both cases, there is no concrete plan to point to, so the job no longer meets the bar for active emergency handling.
    4. Forward to PM when the PM has taken ownership, assigned their own SP, or additional PM input/approval is needed.

    **Why is this considered "bad status/too old"?:** An emergency WO in this status needs a concrete resolution path. A confirmed materials ETA plus installation appointment can keep the WO active; an open-ended wait should trigger re-evaluation.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Troubleshooting">
    **Action:**

    1. After 13 mins has passed, follow up with the agent working on this WO to check if the troubleshooter may require assistance with the WO.
       1. Refer to [https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/3658612739](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/3658612739).
    2. If the WO is found abandoned and no one is working on it, trigger it out so that another agent can troubleshoot.
       1. Submit feedback to the agent who left without passing the WO off in #ops-feedback.

    **Why is this considered "bad status/too old"?:** This would be considered an aging WO for emergency response standards.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Diagnosing Issue">
    **Action:** Create a task for the emergency team to reach out to the tenant to diagnose and troubleshoot.

    **Why is this considered "bad status/too old"?:** An emergency WO doesn’t move from this status automatically.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Pending - Awaiting Payment Details">
    **Action:**

    An emergency WO is never in this status automatically. This would most likely be the result of human error if a resident-responsible WO was escalated to emergency manually by an agent. Review the WO and de-escalate to the original severity.

    **Why is this considered "bad status/too old"?:** This status in an emergency WO is not possible on its own without human error.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Confirming Completion">
    **Action:** If the resident has not replied and has not raised any issue after standard follow-up attempts, move the WO to Awaiting Invoice. This does not close the door on the resident — they can still report a problem after the move, which would trigger normal follow-up.

    **Why is this considered "bad status/too old"?:** The job may be functionally complete but sitting unconfirmed with no resident response and no reported issue.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<Expandable title="The PM requested to assign and dispatch a specific SP, but the service provider was unresponsive or unable to schedule within the expected timeframe.">
  **No restriction on Support following up (ordinary unresponsiveness):**

  - If PD/EH, call the escalation contacts — severity and status stay unchanged (Emergency, Scheduling Service Provider), since this is still being actively worked as an emergency.
  - If not PD/EH, de-escalate to Urgent, keep "(EMERGENCY)" in the WO title, and leave the internal de-escalation note — no PM task needed. The WO stays in Scheduling Service Provider status.

  **PM insists the job is an emergency but restricts us to one specific SP, and that SP is unresponsive (a genuine conflict, not ordinary unresponsiveness):**

  - "Emergency" and "you must use only this one, possibly unreachable provider" aren't compatible — if we must use that person and they don't answer, it isn't functioning as an emergency.
  - De-escalate to Urgent, keep "(EMERGENCY)" in the title so the SP still treats it with urgency, and leave the internal note — no PM task.
  - Escalate to Product Support or have CS raise the contradiction with the PM directly, since this is a policy conflict to resolve, not just a one-off WO decision. If the PM later pushes back asking why it was de-escalated, use the script below.
    - _“Hi \[PM Name\]. As part of our emergency process, we ensure that a job gets scheduled. If we cannot schedule with the current assigned service provider, we will find an alternative service provider. Unless you want us to find an alternative service provider, the appropriate status is urgent and we can keep emergency in the title so that your service provider is aware. But if we have an emergency job, our job is to get a service provider scheduled.”_

  **PM has an explicit critical note instructing us not to contact the SP or the PM once a provider is assigned (a clear instruction, not a conflict):**

  - Do not call the SP or the PM — that defeats the purpose of the instruction.
  - De-escalate to Urgent, keep "(EMERGENCY)" in the title, leave the internal note, and move it out of the active queue.
  - Escalate to Product Support as what is outlined in the above sample.
</Expandable>

<Expandable title="The SP paid an initial visit to the property, but the issue wasn't fixed, and they are building an estimate.">
  **Step 1 — Contact the SP first** (or ask directly if already on the phone with them):

  - Is the emergency part of this already resolved or made safe?
  - Can you get us the estimate today, or only on a later date?

  **Step 2 — If the SP confirms the emergency part is resolved:**

  - Check PD/EH separately — resolving the immediate trigger doesn't automatically clear this.
    - **Still actively property damaging or a habitability concern:** do not de-escalate. The WO stays Emergency, and escalation contacts get called as part of standard emergency handling — a true PD/EH situation must be treated as an emergency regardless of what the SP confirmed.
    - **Not currently property damaging:** proceed with de-escalation. Reclassify to Urgent, keep “(EMERGENCY)” in the WO title, complete the current task if applicable, and leave the internal de-escalation note. If there's any uncertainty about severity, verify directly with the resident rather than relying on the SP's word or WO notes alone.
  - Once reclassified to Urgent, route by PM subscription:
    - **Autopilot:** advise the SP to submit the estimate via the self-service portal — the system automatically advances the WO from there. No manual task needed.
    - **Premium:** create a free-flow follow-up task only if Support still needs to track a pending follow-up; assign it to Ops Associates, not the PM.
    - **24/7:** de-escalating automatically forwards the WO to the PM for non-emergency handling — no manual task needed, the PM picks it up directly.
  - Whether the estimate itself lands today or later doesn't matter here — the emergency's resolved, so it proceeds through the applicable path above either way.

  **Step 3 — If the SP says the emergency part is NOT resolved:**

  - **SP can give us the estimate today:** don't de-escalate yet — tell them to submit it and follow up same-day.
  - **SP can only give us a later date, or is unresponsive after the one same-day contact attempt:**
    - **PD/EH:** do not de-escalate. Call escalation contacts — same rule as Step 2, an active PD/EH situation stays Emergency regardless of timeline.
    - **Not PD/EH:** reclassify to Urgent, keep “(EMERGENCY)” in the WO title, complete the current task if applicable, and leave the internal de-escalation note. Do not create a PM task unless separate PM input or approval is actually needed.
</Expandable>

<Expandable title="A resident follows up on a WO that was previously de-escalated to Urgent. What do we do?">
  **Action:** Ask the resident to describe the **current condition**, then follow this decision tree:

  1. **New or Worsening Emergency Condition** (e.g., leak spread, structural damage, total loss of power/heat in severe weather):
     - **Decision:** Re-escalate to **Emergency**.
     - **Steps:** Resume standard emergency dispatch/follow-up procedures immediately. Post an internal note: _"Re-escalated to Emergency due to new resident report: \[details\]"_.
  2. **Unchanged / Stable Condition** (resident is asking for status or expressing urgency, but condition is unchanged):
     - **Decision:** Maintain **Urgent** status.
     - **Steps:** Reassure the resident, provide current status/ETA, create a task to PM as needed, and log the contact attempt in an internal note. Do not re-escalate solely because the initial intake was an emergency.
</Expandable>

<Expandable title="Assigned Service Providers Also Listed as PM or EEC">
  1. In some cases, the assigned Service Provider on a Work Order may also be listed as the Property Manager or Emergency Escalation Contact. When this occurs **do not assume** that their dual role changes our obligation to verify scheduling, completion, or issue resolution. Ops team should:
     - **Continue to treat the follow-up process as business as usual.**
       - Follow all standard communication and update procedures as you would with any regular service provider.
       - Ensure all updates are clearly documented in the Work Order, and create or update related tasks as needed.
       - If there's any confusion about responsibilities or roles, escalate to Product support for correction. ‼️**IMPORTANT**: When handling **PD/EH issues**, if you anticipate that you may need to escalate the case **after calling the service provider**, and the **escalation contact is the same person** as the service provider, **address the potential escalation during that initial call**. There is **no need to call them a second time as an escalation contact**—make sure to cover both aspects (service provider support and escalation) in your first conversation with them. **Say something like:** _“Thanks for the update. Since you’re also listed as the escalation contact for this work order, we’ll consider this conversation as addressing both roles. I’ll make sure your response is documented as the official direction moving forward.”_ _“Just to confirm—we’d typically escalate this to the PM due to the nature of the issue, but as you're also the escalation contact, your response here will serve as the PM’s direction. I’ll make sure that’s captured.”_
</Expandable>

## Script: Responding to a PM Question About a De-escalation.

_“Hi \[PM Name\]. As part of our emergency process, we ensure that a job gets scheduled. If we cannot schedule with the current assigned service provider, we will find an alternative service provider. Unless you want us to find an alternative service provider, the appropriate status is urgent and we can keep emergency in the title so that your service provider is aware. But if we have an emergency job, our job is to get a service provider scheduled.”_
