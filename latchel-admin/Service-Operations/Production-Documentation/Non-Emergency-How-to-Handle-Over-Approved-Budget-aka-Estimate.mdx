## 🧠 What This Process Is Trying to Solve

<Note>
We want to prevent delays, confusion, and unauthorized spending when a service provider requests work that exceeds the approved budget on a non-emergency work order. The goal is to ensure that all over-budget estimates are properly reviewed, documented, and sent to the property manager or owner for approval before any additional work is authorized—protecting our customers from unexpected costs and helping Latchel maintain clear financial controls and trust with all parties involved
</Note>

<Note>
### What are Estimates?

An estimate is a predetermined cost analysis for the amount that has gone over the Approved budget (while the Service Provider is on-site completing the job). Prior to this, the agent has received a call or message from the provider requesting the approved budget to be lifted to a new amount for a particular work order. This is the only circumstance in which Latchel follows-through with an "Estimate". 

If a property manager wants to use the Latchel system to request a "shopping type" estimate, in which several Service Providers arrive at the site to give an estimate of the job, the property manager will need to initially manage that work order. The property manager will be responsible for creating the work order, assigning a service provider, and locking the service provider into the work order. 
</Note>

<Note>
### When do we provide estimates 

We will provide an estimate for property manager's but we cannot track, collect, or enforce for Preferred service providers.  If we receive the estimate, we'll pass it on to the property manager for approval. For LCN -- we do NOT do free estimates at all. We will not send a Service Provider to a job to collect an estimate. We will, however, send a Service Provider to a site to perform a diagnostic and provide an estimate. This will incur a service charge. We do not handle collecting multiple estimates and bids. That is project management that is outside the scope of Latchel's services.
</Note>

<Note>
### "Quote" equals "Estimate"

When you receive an incoming Work Order and see the word "Quote", this also refers to "Estimate". Latchel uses the term "Estimate" while other organizations might use the word "Quote".
</Note>

<Note>
**⚠️ Automatic Budget Adjustment After Invoice Upload (System Update — June 2026)**  
Once an invoice is uploaded to a work order — whether by the Service Provider via their portal or by a Latchel associate — the system will **automatically reduce both the Approved Budget and the Hard Budget** by subtracting the invoice amount. This happens at the time of upload; the invoice does **not** need to be marked as paid first.

**What this means:**

- Any new estimate submitted after an invoice has been uploaded will be compared against the **remaining** budget, not the original amount.
- This prevents the system from auto-approving new estimates based on the full original budget when part of the work has already been invoiced.

**⚠️ Reminder:** When uploading an invoice, always ensure the file is properly **tagged as an invoice** in the system (not as an estimate or other document type) so the automatic budget adjustment triggers correctly.

**Example:** If the Approved Budget is $260 and an invoice for $260 is uploaded, the Approved Budget will automatically update to $0. If a Service Provider then submits a new estimate, it will be compared against the $0 remaining budget and will require PM approval.

---
</Note>

<Note>
**NOTE:** If an Owner or Property Manager requests that they want to approve **any amount before work is done** on a specific work order, **or do not want anything over $[X] approved without checking with them first** (especially when the Hard Budget Limit is higher), please refer to the **Frequently Asked Questions** section of this SOP.
</Note>

<Warning>
Whenever a Service Provider sends multiple estimates for the same Work Order (for example: one estimate to *repair* an existing equipment and another to *replace* the same equipment), we will seek Property Manager’s approval prior to proceeding, even if the estimates fall under the Hard Limit. Latchel will not make the decision of which estimate to choose, we will allow the Property Manager to make that decision. In such cases, follow the same procedure for when an estimate exceeds the hard limit.
</Warning>

<Warning>
For RentRedi and NOMAD related WOs, please refer to https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Handle-DIY-Owner-Contacts.

However, after approving the budget, do **not** proactively schedule the appointment or create a dispatching task — the system handles vendor automation. Only intervene if the work order did not move forward as expected, a task is created for Latchel, or the PM explicitly requests action. For full Autopilot handling, refer to https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/2999812097.
</Warning>

<Note>

- What was the total amount approved?
- Name of the SP who received the approval.
- When was it approved?

### **How to Handle Service Provider Visit Fees, Trip Charges, Diagnostic Fees**

<Note>
### What is a Visit Fee/Trip Charge/Diagnostic Fee?

- Visit Fee/Trip Charge - (n.) Minimum fee when the Service Provider is dispatched, even if they do not perform any work.
- Diagnostic Fee - (n.) Fee the Service Provider charges to assess the issue. They may charge this fee even though they can't complete the work.
</Note>

1. Service providers may charge visit fees, trip charges, and diagnostic fees.
  1. Each service provider and trade treats these fees differently. Property Managers are responsible for paying any visit fees, trip charges, or diagnostic fees.
  2. If a visit fee is outrageous, then PMs have a dispute process that they can go through. Please refer to https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/795967495.
2. The PM only pays what the Service Provider charges.
  1. If the Service Provider doesn't charge a visit fee,  the PM pays no visit fee. 
  2. The Property Manager pays visit fees via the normal invoice feature in our system
  3. The Property Manager only pays for things that the Service Provider invoices through our platform or via Preferred Service Provider relationships outside of our system
3. If the Service Provider charges a visit fee but doesn't complete the work, review Part 2, Section 2 "Service Provider Payment".

<Accordion title="What are the actions we can take for Service Provider Requests for Structural Changes">
Situations arise where structural alterations, such as wall cutting, become necessary to resolve maintenance issues. Below are common scenarios we have encountered, though this list is not exhaustive.

- Scenario 1: Resolution of a Problem Stated in the Work Order
- Scenario 2: Service Provider’s Error

**A. The service provider has stated that structural alterations are necessary to resolve the issue.**

1. If the total requested cost is within the approved or hard budget limit, associates may proceed with approving the request.
  1. Proceed to inform the service provider the work has been approved.
  2. Leave complete notes of the approval process.
    1. See https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/1058211367 
2. If the total requested cost exceeds the approved or hard budget limit, the request must be escalated to the Property Manager.
  1. Move the WO to “Needs Owner Approval”.
    1. Refer to [How to: Put a Job on Hold](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/766378165).
    2. Await the PM/owner’s decision before proceeding.

**B. The service provider has completed the work but left a hole/other issue that requires a different technician to fix.**

1. **Handle the original work order**
  1. If the SP has completed the original scope of work, proceed with the standard completion process for the original WO (invoice collection, etc.).
  2. Document in the WO notes that the SP's work resulted in additional damage requiring a separate repair (e.g., hole in the wall, exposed wiring, etc.).
2. **Create a new work order for the follow-up repair**
  1. Create a **new work order** for the follow-up repair (e.g., drywall patching, painting, etc.).
    - Link the new WO to the original WO for reference by adding them into the same project.
    - In the new WO description, clearly state:
      - What the original issue was
      - What work was performed
      - What damage/issue remains and needs to be addressed
      - That this is a follow-up to WO [original WO #]
  2. Move the new WO to **"Needs Owner Approval"**.
    - Include a message to the PM explaining the situation.
    - **Sample message:** *"During the completion of WO [original WO #] for [original issue] at [address], the service provider needed to [describe structural change, e.g., cut into the wall] to resolve the issue. The original repair has been completed, but a follow-up repair is now needed to [describe remaining work, e.g., patch and paint the drywall]. This new work order has been created for your review and approval. Please advise how you would like to proceed."*
  3. **Do NOT assign a new SP to the follow-up WO** until PM approval is received.
</Accordion>

  1. Review the estimate to confirm whether it is signed by the Property Manager or Owner.

<Accordion title="The service provider sent an estimate via email with a link to approve or deny it. Do I (Ops) have the authority to approve or deny the estimate using that link?">
Yes — Ops has the authority to approve or deny estimates submitted via a service provider's link, **as long as the standard budget rules are followed**:

- ✅ **If the estimate is equal to or under the Hard Budget** → You may approve via the link. After approving, update the Approved Budget in the work order and document the action in the WO notes.
- ❌ **If the estimate exceeds the Hard Budget** → Do **NOT** approve via the link. Escalate to the Property Manager.
- ⚠️ **If the SP submits multiple estimates** (e.g., repair vs. replace) → Do **NOT** approve via the link. Escalate all options to the Property Manager regardless of amount.

**Always remember to:**

1. Attach the estimate/email to the work order.
2. Leave clear notes documenting that the estimate was approved/denied via the SP's link, including the amount and reason.

The submission method (link, email, portal, phone, SMS) does **not** change Ops' approval authority — the same budget-limit rules apply in all cases.

**What if the SP sent the estimate link directly to the Property Manager (not to Ops) and the PM hasn't taken action?**

In some cases, the service provider's system will send the estimate link and/or a copy of the estimate directly to the Property Manager (e.g., via SMS or email), requiring the PM to approve through the SP's external link. If the PM has not acted on the link:

1. **Verify the estimate details** — Confirm the estimate amount and review whether it is within or over the Hard Budget.
2. **Upload the estimate to the work order** — If the estimate or a screenshot of the SP's link/notification is available, attach it to the WO files. Refer to https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/757039124.
3. **Create a task for the Property Manager** informing them that the SP requires their action on the external link:
  1. **Note: If this is the first time you are moving the work order to Needs Owner Approval, follow the below task information. If the WO is already in Needs Owner Approval, create a task.**
  2. For Property Damaging and Extreme Habitability situations:
    1. Subject: **Action Required: Service Provider Requires Approval via External Link**
    2. Who is this task about? *Select the PM*
    3. Select a Date and Time: *Leave as is*
    4. Assign to a User Group: *PM user group*
    5. Assign to a User: *Leave as is*
    6. Priority: *Leave as is*
    7. *Complete task only on Completed or Cancelled job States*
    8. Description: *"The assigned service provider has submitted an estimate that requires your approval through an external link. The service provider's system sent the estimate details and approval link directly to you via [SMS/email — specify the method]. In addition to any approval through the work order portal, the service provider requires you to access and approve the estimate through their link before they will proceed with the work. Please complete the approval at your earliest convenience. If you have any questions or did not receive the link, please let us know and we can coordinate with the service provider to resend it. Thank you!"*
  3. For non-Property Damaging and Extreme Habitability situations:
    1. Call the escalation contacts.
      1. Refer to https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Job-Aid-PM-Outbound-Communication-for-Emergency-WOs
    2. If the escalation contacts are unresponsive, go back Step 3.a.
4. **Move the work order to Needs Owner Approval** (if the estimate exceeds the Hard Budget) or **Pending** (if within budget but the SP still requires the PM's action on the link before proceeding).
5. **Follow up if the PM does not act on the link** — Follow the same follow-up cadence as standard estimate approvals. Refer to the *"When should we follow up with the Property Manager if a work order is in Needs Owner Approval"* FAQ above.
6. **Document your actions** — Leave clear notes that the SP sent the estimate link directly to the PM, the method used (SMS, email, etc.), the date it was sent, and that no action was taken by the PM. Refer to https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/1058211367 .
</Accordion>

## 🎯 When to Break This SOP

You can break the SOP in the following scenarios:

- **If the Property Manager or Owner has already provided explicit approval or denial for the estimate outside of the standard process,** you may update the work order and document the exception accordingly.
- **If the service provider insists on immediate payment for an initial visit while the estimate is still pending,** escalate to the Payments team or follow the payment escalation process as outlined, rather than waiting for the standard approval flow.
- **If you are unsure which action to take or encounter an unusual situation not covered by the SOP,** escalate to an Operations Support Specialist for guidance rather than proceeding on your own.
