## 🧠 What This Process Is Trying to Solve

<Note>
  We want to ensure that true emergencies are identified and handled quickly, accurately, and safely. The goal is to prevent delays, miscommunication, or missed steps that could put residents or property at risk. By standardizing how emergency work orders are assessed, documented, and escalated, we reduce liability, minimize property damage, and deliver a consistent, reliable experience for both customers and property managers. This process matters because every minute counts in an emergency, and clear protocols protect everyone involved—from residents to service providers to Latchel itself
</Note>

8. If the call disconnects, call the customer back until you have obtained enough details to move the Work Order forward.
9. **‼️IMPORTANT:** If the WO is for a **common area**, do not ask the resident to troubleshoot the issue itself — they can't fix a common-area problem. Instead, call the resident (or any other active resident in the unit) for two things:
10. Access info — ask if they can grant entry themselves, or whether they know the entry code/instructions.
11. Clarification — if the situation is ambiguous, use the Troubleshooter Question Bank to clarify the issue before deciding severity.
12. Say something like: _“We received a maintenance request for a common area at your property — [brief issue]. [Clarify anything ambiguous; see if they can verify the emergency.] Can you confirm: do you have access to that area, or do you know the entry code/instructions?”_

- If they don't have that information, do not ask them who to contact — escalate to the PM instead.

6. The service provider or access instructions indicate that the issue is in a common area. 9. **‼️VERY IMPORTANT** Troubleshoot the issue with the resident as far as they are willing to follow your instructions, don't force residents into doing troubleshooting steps that they may not feel comfortable doing.
10. The following documents will explain how to assess an emergency situation and determine if emergency dispatch is required or if the emergency can be de-escalated. It is also important to check for any potential duplicates to avoid double dispatch of vendors and determine if there's potential for property damage.

28. **Ask the Resident if the Issue Can Wait (Non-PD/EH Emergencies Only)**
29. ⚠️ _This step applies ONLY to non-Property Damaging and non-Extreme Habitability emergencies **and only for calls received between 5:00 PM and 9:00 AM**. For true PD or EH emergencies, skip this step and proceed with standard immediate dispatch flow._
30. 32. **If resident needs service tonight** → Proceed with standard emergency dispatch flow.
33. **Do NOT ask this question for PD or EH emergencies** — dispatch immediately per standard flow.
34. **Decide the Job Category** - Is the maintenance request an Emergency or a Non-Emergency?
35. If it's been determined to be an **Emergency**, you must:

37. Confirm attempting to dispatch tonight (some will want to wait until next day depending on emergency and time of day they place call).

## **📋**Handling Work Orders with Home Warranty Information

<Note>
  Refer to this guide on how to locate warranty information: [https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/LTG/pages/440074480](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/LTG/pages/440074480)
</Note>

<Warning>
  **Non-Emergency Work Orders:**

  \*\*If a property has a Home Warranty note, all non-emergency work orders require Property Manager approval before dispatching and are therefore automatically forwarded to the Property Manager. \*\*

  If an emergency WO is de-escalated to non-emergency and the property has a warranty note, the system will automatically place the WO in Pending with a task to PM to review warranty. Ops should not move the WO forward or override this status.

  1. Notify the PM/Owner that the WO has been de-escalated and moved to Pending due to the warranty note on file.
  2. If the reported issue does not match the warranty note, submit a PS ticket to flag the mismatch. In the PS ticket:

  - Note the specific WO ID and the mismatch between the reported issue and the warranty note.
  - Request that PS verify with the PM/Owner whether they want to update their intake settings to specify the exact warranty details so that only matching WOs are automatically held for warranty on non-emergency cases.
    - Refer to [https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Escalate-to-Product-Supporton](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Escalate-to-Product-Supporton) how to submit a ticket.

  3. Document in\* the WO notes that the reported issue does not align with the warranty information on file and that the PM/Owner has been notified.\*
</Warning>

Latchel **does not coordinate with Home Warranty companies** for emergencies or non-emergencies. Emergencies are handled **directly by Latchel** regardless of warranty status. For emergencies covered by warranty, Latchel attempts to resolve the issue directly.

Proceed to handle the emergency work order as usual following our guidelines in [https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Handle-Emergency-Work-Orders](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Handle-Emergency-Work-Orders).

If a Property Manager reaches out and asks Latchel to contact a Home Warranty company for an emergency work order, follow these steps:

1. Inform the Property Manager that Latchel does not coordinate directly with Home Warranty companies.
2. Advise the Property Manager that if they want Latchel to consider a Home Warranty company for emergency dispatching, they must add that company as a Service Provider in their account.
3. 🗨️ **Say something like:**\* "Hi [PM name], Latchel does not directly coordinate with Home Warranty companies. If you’d like us to consider dispatching to a warranty company for emergencies, please add them as a Service Provider in your account. This will allow us to include them in our dispatch process."\*
4. Explain that once added, Latchel can treat the Home Warranty company like any other listed Service Provider and may dispatch to them for emergencies when appropriate.
5. If the Property Manager agrees, proceed with assigning the Home Warranty service provider and schedule the work order accordingly.
6. See [https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Emergency-How-to-Handle-Scheduling-Requests](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Emergency-How-to-Handle-Scheduling-Requests)

  </Warning>

  **Step 1 — Confirm the mismatch**

  1. Politely verify: _"I'm looking for [Resident Name] regarding a maintenance request at [Property Address]. Is this the correct number for them?"_
  2. If the person confirms they are **not** the resident and have no connection to the property, treat the number as incorrect.

  - Do **not** continue troubleshooting with this person.
  - Do **not** send SMS communications to this number.

  **Step 2 — Attempt alternate contacts**

  1. Check for **additional phone numbers** on the resident's profile.

  - If available, call those numbers (up to 3 attempts each).

  2. Check for **other residents listed on the same property**.

  - If available, call them to verify the issue still exists and gather information.

  3. If you reach the correct resident on a different number:

  - Confirm their updated contact number.
  - **Update their profile** with the correct number.
  - Proceed with standard troubleshooting.

  **Step 3 — Attempt email contact**

  If no alternate phone numbers are available or all attempts are unsuccessful:

  1. Send an **email** to the resident (if an email address is on file) using the following blurb:
  2. _🗨️ **Email Blurb:**_\* \*\*\*_"Hello [Resident Name], this is [Your Name] from Latchel, the maintenance intake team for [PM Company Name]. We attempted to reach you by phone regarding your recent maintenance request for [WO Title], but were unable to connect with you at the number on file. Please reply to this email or call us at [PM's Latchel Communication Phone Number] so we can assist you. If the issue has worsened, please call us immediately. Thank you!"_
  3. Document in the WO notes that the phone number on file appears incorrect and that email was used as a fallback.

  **Step 4 — Escalate if all contact methods fail**

  If no email is on file **and** no alternate contacts exist:

  - **For PD/EH (Property Damaging / Extreme Habitability) issues:** Proceed with EEC escalation per the standard [https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Job-Aid-PM-Outbound-Communication-for-Emergency-WOs](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Job-Aid-PM-Outbound-Communication-for-Emergency-WOs). In the escalation notes, include:
    - _"Phone number on file appears incorrect — person who answered denied creating the WO and does not match the resident profile. Unable to reach resident via alternate methods."_
    - Recommend the PM confirm/update resident contact information.
  - **For non-PD/EH issues:** Forward the WO to the PM by following the same steps in the PM Input Needed path.

  **📝 Documentation Requirements**

  Always add a note to the WO that includes:

  - That the person who answered denied being the resident / denied creating the WO
  - Any indicators of mismatch (e.g., "Tenant name is female; male voice answered and stated no WO was submitted")
  - Which alternate contact methods were attempted and their outcomes
  - The fallback method used (email, PM escalation, etc.)

  **❌ What NOT to Do**

  | Don't | Why |
  | --- | --- |
  | Send SMS to the incorrect number | Could be a privacy concern / harassment to a stranger |
  | Continue troubleshooting with the wrong person | They cannot provide accurate info about the property |
  | Assume the WO is invalid and cancel it | The resident may have submitted it — the number is just wrong |
  | Skip documentation | QA and the PM need to know the number is incorrect |
</Accordion>

## 🎯 When to Break This SOP

<Note>
  There are rare situations where following the standard emergency work order process may not be appropriate or safe. Break this SOP and escalate immediately if:
</Note>

- The situation poses an immediate threat to life, health, or major property damage (e.g., fire, gas leak, active flooding, electrical hazard).
- The resident or anyone on-site is in danger and requires emergency services—always instruct to call 911 first, then notify the appropriate escalation contacts.
- You encounter a scenario not covered by the SOP, or you are unsure how to proceed safely—pause and escalate to an Operations Support Specialists.
- Multiple emergencies are reported at once and you are unsure how to prioritize or split work orders—seek guidance before proceeding.
- You make a mistake or cannot complete a required action after multiple attempts—immediately offer to transfer to a specialist for support.
