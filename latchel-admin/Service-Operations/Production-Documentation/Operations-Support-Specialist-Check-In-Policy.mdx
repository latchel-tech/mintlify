## 🧠 What This Process Is Trying to Solve

<Info>
  We want to prevent unnecessary delays and loss of visibility on tasks that may be sitting idle without updates. The goal is to ensure timely check-ins that help identify if an associate is stuck, needs clarification, or is still reviewing information — so we can maintain smooth workflow, uphold SLAs, and provide consistent service quality for both our customers and internal teams.

  This process is intended to support associates, help identify blockers early, and maintain SLA compliance — not to penalize or criticize.
</Info>

## ⏱️ When to Post a Check-In

A check-in should be posted in the appropriate channel when any of the following apply:

### 1 - 13-Minute Rule

- A task assigned to an associate remains unacknowledged or without update for 13 minutes after assignment or last visible activity.

<Info>
   📝 This time frame was derived from a team-wide survey where associates shared what they consider a fair duration before a follow-up. The average result was 13 minutes.
</Info>

### 2 - Unacknowledged Assignment

- The associate has been tagged or assigned a task but has not confirmed receipt or started handling it within 13 minutes.

### 3 - Potential SLA Breach

- The delay is likely to cause an SLA breach, backlog, or handoff issue between shifts.

## 💬 Where to Post a Check-In

The message should be posted in the corresponding operations channel, depending on the task type:

| Task Type | Channel | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emergency Task or Admin Ticket | 🧯 #ops-troubleshooter | "@AssociateName just checking on \[Admin Ticket #12345\] — been open since 10:15 AM." |
| Inbound Call or Non-Emergency Email | 📞 #task-force-graham | "@AssociateName following up on \[Ticket #54321\]. Any update so far?" |
| SMS or Webchat | 💬 #sms-squad | "@AssociateName hi! Noticed that a message from \[customer name\] is still in the queue — checking if you're working on this one." |

## 💬 How to Post a Check-In

Check-ins should be professional, neutral, and supportive. The purpose is to clarify the status, not to reprimand.

**Format Example:**

🔍 Check-in: @AssociateName — noticed that \[task/ticket #12345\] has been pending since \[time\]. Just checking if you're still working on it or reviewing the SOP for next steps. 😊

**Alternative for time-sensitive cases:**

⏰ Reminder: @AssociateName — following up on \[task/ticket #12345\]. Kindly confirm the status so we can keep it within SLA. Thank you!

## 🚦 If There's No Response

If the associate does not respond within 5 minutes after the check-in:

1. Notify the agent's supervisor privately.
2. If the ticket is close to breaching, reassign the task and document the reassignment in the channel.

## 🧩 Assume Positive Intent

When posting a check-in, always consider that the associate might be:

- Reviewing or confirming details through SOPs or internal documentation.
- Investigating the issue further to ensure accuracy before updating.
- Validating details or information related to the task.

Associates should not be assisting other teams or working on unrelated tasks unless coordinated. Original tasks remain the top priority within the shift.

## 📖 Reminders for Posting

- Keep messages concise and respectful — focus on progress, not performance.
- One check-in per delay is enough; follow up privately if needed.
- Always document the check-in for visibility and shift continuity.
- Maintain a supportive tone, e.g., "checking if you need help" instead of "why isn't this done yet."

## 🎯 When to Break This SOP

While the standard check-in timeframe is 13 minutes, the Support team may use its discretion to delay posting a check-in if:

- The issue is clearly complex or multi-layered, requiring deeper review or investigation.
- It's evident that the associate is actively checking resources or confirming details through SOPs or past tickets.

This flexibility ensures the process remains fair and context-aware rather than strictly time-based.
