## 🧠 What This Process Is Trying to Solve

<Note>
  _We want to prevent misdiagnosis, unnecessary emergency dispatches, and confusion when handling urgent maintenance requests. The goal is to ensure that each emergency situation is accurately assessed—verifying the severity, clarifying vague or incomplete resident reports, and confirming whether an issue truly requires immediate action or can be de-escalated. This process helps avoid double-dispatching, reduces unnecessary costs, and ensures that property managers’ definitions of emergencies are respected, ultimately saving time, money, and frustration for all parties involved_
</Note>

<Note>
  🎓**Training Content**

  The contents of this page have been included in the **Video Troubleshooting** and \*\*Troubleshooting refresher \*\*Coassemble course.
</Note>

<Warning>
  **📌 Note for CC and SMS/Webchat Associates**<br />This SOP is written for the Troubleshooting (TS) workflow — it covers the full diagnostic process including resident calls, probing questions, and live severity assessment. **You are not expected to perform this full workflow.**

  However, the severity assessment principles in this SOP (especially Step 4 — using PM intake settings to determine correct severity) apply to you when you spot a Harmonia misclassification in the course of your normal work. In those cases:

  - If the mismatch is **clear from intake settings** → correct the severity directly. You do not need to place a TS call or follow this full SOP.
  - If the situation **requires live assessment** (e.g., worsening conditions, PD/EH concerns) → create a TS task. Do not troubleshoot the situation yourself.

  For queue-specific guidance, refer to [https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/3576627319](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/3576627319) and the [https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/4035280901](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/4035280901).

## **🛡️**Why is my role as an Ops Troubleshooter so important?

As an Ops Troubleshooter, you are the first line of defense in minimizing any [potential property damage](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Identify-Property-Damage-and-Extreme-Habitability-Situations) and ensuring residents’ safety. Our Property Managers entrust us with the very important job of properly diagnosing emergency situations and only sending out Service Providers when absolutely necessary. They also trust that we’ll only contact their Escalation Contacts after hours in specific situations. On the other hand, they trust that we will take immediate action when a situation does qualify as an emergency based on their intake settings.

## **🔧**Ops Troubleshooter Mission

Your mission as an Ops Troubleshooter is to:

1. Resolve the issue(s) during your troubleshooting call by actively listening to the resident, [asking probing questions](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Job-Aid-Troubleshooter-Question-Bank) and suggesting troubleshooting techniques.
2. De-escalate the issue(s) to non-emergency if it is neither [property damaging, extreme habitability](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Identify-Property-Damage-and-Extreme-Habitability-Situations), nor considered an emergency based on the Property Manager’s intake settings.
3. Take swift action to dispatch if the issue(s) is property damaging, extreme habitability or considered an emergency based on the Property Manager’s intake settings.

<Warning>
  **Reminder:** If a TS task has been created and an issue assessment has been completed, ensure that the problem and location details are updated if they are missing from the work order. This also applies when changing the work order severity. Maintaining accurate information is essential for effective resolution and helps prevent miscommunication or delays in handling the work order.
</Warning>

28. This principle works in both directions. Just as intake settings prevent incorrect de-escalation, they also confirm when dispatch should proceed. If the PM's intake settings classify the confirmed issue as an emergency (e.g., “resident completely blocked from accessing a common area or common entrance due to doorway not functioning or keyless entry device broken”), proceed with dispatch — do not route the WO to PM simply because the issue is in a common area or because a legacy SOP step would otherwise forward it.
29. **‼️IMPORTANT**: Always refer to the guidelines on [Handling Work Order Severity via Harmonia Intake Settings](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/edit-v2/3576627319?draftShareId=3cfb8984-5808-40c6-a38e-aaed5ba1c5e9)
30. **📝 Temperature-Based Rules — Assessment Guidance:** When evaluating HVAC-related emergencies against the PM's intake settings:

- **Outside temperature rules:** Harmonia uses a **3-day weather forecast** by default. Check the temperature widget for the full 3-day outlook. If the PM's Intake Settings specify a shorter timeframe (e.g., "in the next 24 hours"), follow the PM's specified timeframe instead.
- **Inside temperature rules:** Some PM Intake Settings reference **indoor temperature** (e.g., "if inside temp is below 60°F"). Harmonia cannot determine this from weather data. You **must ask the resident** what the current indoor temperature is and document their response in the WO notes.
- **Always check** whether the PM's rule says "outside" or "inside" temperature before applying the default forecast window.

3. **‼️Fire Hazard / Power Shutoff Assessment Guidance**
4. ⛔ **Before proceeding:** Screen for immediate danger. If the resident reports active flames, active smoke, active sparking, or anyone in immediate danger → stop this workflow and follow the [911-Severity process](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Handle-911-Severity-Type-Work-Orders) immediately. Do not continue with the assessment below.
5. If the situation does **not** meet 911-Severity criteria but signs of electrical malfunction are present, continue with the Fire Hazard / Power Shutoff assessment below — this may still qualify as Property Damaging per the PD/EH SOP classification table.
6. When evaluating any report involving a major electrical system (HVAC, furnace, electric water heater, or other hardwired appliance) that the resident describes as "off":
7. If malfunction signs were reported only in the past and are not currently present, AND the resident now confirms the breaker/disconnect is off, this does not require escalation — proceed per the PM's intake settings. Ongoing or currently observed signs (even if the system control is off) still require Fire Hazard classification.  If the PM's intake settings classify the issue as non-emergency but PD/EH criteria are met during assessment, agents must escalate to the PM's Escalation Contacts per the [standard PD/EH process](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Job-Aid-PM-Outbound-Communication-for-Emergency-WOs) to inform them and get permission to dispatch.  \*\*⚠️ Cross-reference: \*\*If the equipment is instead behaving abnormally and cannot be controlled at all (e.g., running non-stop, unusual smells/sounds), use [Job Aid: Troubleshooter Question Bank](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Job-Aid-Troubleshooter-Question-Bank) → Step 1.5 Safety Assessment & Immediate Mitigation instead — that pathway already covers active mitigation for out-of-control equipment. This new guidance covers the separate, more common scenario: the system is simply reported "off" and de-escalation is being considered.

- Confirm how the system was turned off: thermostat, dial, or unit control, which may still leave power supplied, versus confirmation that the power supply to the system has been fully disconnected. 
  - ⚠️ **Safety boundary:** Never ask a resident to open a panel or equipment cover, or to approach heat, water, smoke, sparks, or exposed wiring. If the resident expresses concern about safely accessing the breaker, do not push — document power status as unconfirmed and classify accordingly.
- A system turned off at the thermostat, dial, or unit control still has power supplied to its internal components and can still short-circuit, spark, or overheat. This is NOT the same as eliminating fire risk.
- If the resident cannot confirm the breaker is off AND there are any signs of electrical malfunction (burning smell, sparking, smoke, visible damage) → this qualifies as a Fire Hazard / Property Damaging situation per [\[How to: Identify PD and EH Situations\]](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Identify-Property-Damage-and-Extreme-Habitability-Situations). Follow the PM's intake settings to determine severity. If the PM's settings classify the issue as non-emergency but PD/EH criteria are met, escalate to the [PM's Escalation Contacts](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Job-Aid-PM-Outbound-Communication-for-Emergency-WOs) per the standard PD/EH process to inform them and get permission to dispatch."
- If the resident cannot confirm the breaker is off AND there are **no** signs of electrical malfunction → ask the resident to shut off the breaker as a precaution.
- If resident confirms shutoff → proceed using PM's intake settings. No PD/EH escalation needed.
- If resident cannot or will not shut off → potential PD/EH. Power is unconfirmed on a reported electrical issue. Follow [PM's intake settings](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/3576627319) to determine severity; if PM classifies as non-emergency, escalate to PM's Escalation Contacts to inform them that power remains unconfirmed on a reported electrical issue and get direction.
- If the resident confirms the breaker is off AND there are no signs of malfunction → proceed using the PM's intake settings to determine severity for the underlying issue, following typical procedures .
- Document in the work order: how the system was powered down, whether malfunction signs were present, and the resulting classification and reasoning.

6. If the Property Manager settings indicate that it is not an emergency, but you assess it to be an issue that will continuously damage the property, and get worse with time, or an extreme habitability issue, then you will call the Escalation Contacts (in the specified order) to ask for permission to dispatch. If the Escalation Contacts do not answer the phone, we should send an email to the failed escalation email list and then follow the PMs intake settings. If you can't access Assembly to send an email, check the [https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Escalate-Between-Teams](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Escalate-Between-Teams) for guidance.
7. If you’ve verified that the PM considers the issue to be Urgent or Normal, and you determined that it is not property-damaging or extreme habitability, you may de-escalate.\*\* \*\***Please include in the Troubleshooting Guided Workflow the reason for de-escalation.** Ex: _"The 3-day forecast shows temperatures will remain above the Emergency Thresholds"_ or _"PM's intake settings state that \[issue\] is to be handled as Urgent"_
8. Repeat this process for **_all_** issues the resident mentions during your troubleshooting call. If the PM considers any of the issues to be an emergency, treat them as such.

This ensures accurate severity classification and avoids unnecessary escalation. Refer to [https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Job-Aid-Troubleshooter-Question-Bank](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Job-Aid-Troubleshooter-Question-Bank).

5. After addressing the initial problem(s), verify with the resident that there are no other remaining issues.
6. A secondary issue the resident mentioned during your call.
7. A consequence of one of the issues you have already mitigated or resolved. For example:
8. The water heater was leaking so you instructed the resident to close the water valve to the heater. Now the resident does not have hot water. You check the PM’s settings to define if no hot water is considered an emergency. Next, you assess if there is any standing water and if the resident is able to mop it up. If not, you dispatch a water extraction provider.
9. A tree fell, blocking the entrance to the home and breaking a window. You check the PM’s settings to determine if tree removal needs to be treated as an emergency. You then do the same for the broken window.
10. The electricity short circuited resulting in a partial power outage in the kitchen. You walked the resident through resetting the circuit breaker and were able to restore power! Next, you ask the resident to make sure the kitchen appliances are still working, especially the refrigerator. You check the PM’s settings to define which broken appliance needs to be treated as an emergency.
11. A resident has a leaking roof and the weather forecast indicates potential rain coming within the next 72 hours. You dispatch a Service Provider to tarp the roof to temporarily protect the property. Next, you create a work order for a roofer to do the final fix.

Reread your notes from the call. Provide the resident with a summary of all the issues you discussed and the next steps for each of them to make sure all issues have been resolved. Ask the customer if there are any outstanding or new issues they’d like to tell you about before you end the call. 6. Assess if another work order needs to be created.

Follow this same procedure if a new issue has arisen as a result or consequence of the original issue submitted.
2. **Important**: Create the new WO while you’re still on the phone with the resident, this will ensure that you don’t miss creating it and that you’re able to complete diagnostics for the issue accurately.
3. Repeat this process with all new issues mentioned by the resident.

## **📝Guidelines for Evaluating and Reassessing Work Order Information**

Residents may not always provide complete or clear details when submitting a work order. Whether you're reviewing a new request, following up with a resident, or reassessing an issue, it's important to take the right steps to understand the situation fully. The following guidelines apply to:

- Initial diagnosing
- Follow-ups
- Reassessment inquiries

Use these steps to confirm the issue’s severity, clarify any missing details, and decide if further action is needed—whether the issue should be escalated, de-escalated, or remain unchanged.

<Warning>
  To determine whether the PM is actively managing a work order, follow the guidelines in [https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/3805478920](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/3805478920).
</Warning>

1. **Evaluating Additional Information**
2. Whether the resident provides additional, valuable information that was not mentioned in the original work order summary, \*\*OR \*\*the original WO summary indicates the issue can be de-escalated/escalated
3. **Identify key triggers** in the resident's response to determine whether the issue should remain classified as an emergency or can be de-escalated.
4. Refer to [Ops Troubleshooters Quick Reference Guide: Emergency and Non-Emergency Triggers](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/3250290738)
5. **Evaluate the severity of the issue:**
6. If the assessment confirms there are no changes to the issue’s severity, NO additional action is needed.
7. **If the issue is an emergency (PD/EH) and:**
8. **The Property Manager is actively managing it:**
9. Call the escalation contacts if this has not already been done.
10. If there is no response, or if initial contact has already been made:
11. Leave the work order status as is.
12. Complete necessary follow-up actions for an unresponsive PM (e.g., sending an SMS).
13. Refer to the [Job Aid: PM Outbound Communication for Emergency WOs](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/Job-Aid-PM-Outbound-Communication-for-Emergency-WOs).
14. **If there is no evidence that the Property Manager is managing the work order:**
15. Proceed with the **next steps to advance the work order** without delay.
16. If the issue is a non-emergency and has **escalated to an emergency:**
17. 📝 **NOTE:** For work orders that start in **Needs Approval** due to the initial intake process, do **not** consider them under active PM management—even if they are later escalated. Proceed directly to Step 3.C.

21. If the **PM is not managing the issue**, Ops must proceed with escalation and handle it according to the applicable subscription type and SOP guidelines below.
22. **After-Hours Cases**

Instead, create a task to the PM following the task guidelines in Scenario B of [https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/x/AQBtow](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/x/AQBtow).

50. De-escalate the work order severity based on troubleshooting and confirmed conditions.
51. For 24/7 Emergency and After Hours subscribed PM
52. Leave the work order status unchanged, in accordance with the subscription coverage.
53. Review the intake settings to determine whether Property Manager approval is required for this type of concern.
54. 📝 **NOTE**: This process also applies to work orders with clear **critical notes** or \*\*pre-dispatch instructions \*\*explicitly indicating that the work order must be placed in “Waiting for Approval” after de-escalation
55. If Property Manager approval **is required** and the work order is in **“Waiting for PM”** status, place the work order on hold.
56. Update the work order status to\*\* “Needs Approval”\*\* to formally notify the Property Manager that the request has been de-escalated. Refer to [https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/766378165](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/766378165)
57. If the intake settings\*\* \*\*do \*\*not \*\*require Property Manager approval for this type of concern, confirm that Latchel is authorized to handle the request (Premium customers).
58. Check the work order status and confirm it is currently in **“Waiting for PM”**.
59. Proceed with\*\* rescheduling the work order\*\* by using the **Action** button.
60. Create a task for associates to follow up with the service provider.
61. \*\*If the resident does not provide new information AND the original WO summary is vague or unclear: \*\*
62. Err on the side of caution and **keep the WO as an emergency** to ensure the issue is handled promptly.
63. **Say something like**: _"_ _Hi \[Resident Name\], thanks for getting back to us! Since we haven’t been able to reach you by phone, your request has been forwarded to the appropriate team for proper handling. For the fastest resolution, please give us a call back at \[number\] so we can properly diagnose the issue at your earliest convenience. If you have any further details or if the issue worsens, feel free to let us know, and we’ll escalate it accordingly._
64. **Document the interaction thoroughly, including a clear justification for why the work order was either de-escalated or maintained as an emergency.**
65. **When resident submits actual photo of the issue**
66. **Review the Photos Against the Resident’s Description**
67. Carefully compare the submitted photos to the details provided by the resident in the work order description.
68. Check for consistency: Do the photos clearly show the issue as described? Are there any discrepancies or missing elements?
69. **If the Photos Match the Resident’s Description:**
70. Proceed with troubleshooting and severity assessment as usual, using both the written description and the visual evidence.
71. **If the Photos Do Not Match or Raise Questions:**
72. **Contact the resident** to clarify the discrepancy. Confirm what the photos are showing and whether they accurately represent the issue described.
73. **Say something like**\*\*:\*\* “_Hi \[Resident Name\], thank you for sending the photos. I noticed that the images don’t fully match the issue described in your request. Could you please confirm if the photos show the same problem you reported, or is there another issue we should be aware of? If possible, can you provide a brief explanation or send additional photos to help us better understand the situation? This will help us make sure we address your concern accurately and as quickly as possible.”_
74. Ask follow-up questions as needed to resolve any ambiguity.
75. **Basing Severity on Visual Information (Applicable to Unresponsive Residents):**
76. After clarification, base your severity assessment on the most accurate and up-to-date information available—including the photos and any new details from the resident.
77. If the photos reveal a more severe or urgent issue than initially described, escalate according to emergency protocols.
78. If the photos show a less severe issue, adjust the work order severity as appropriate.
79. **Document the actions taken and the outcome in the work order.**

## **🚨**Special note on after hours emergencies

If an emergency cannot be de-escalated, [**_ask yourself if this issue could potentially cause property damage, or is an extreme habitability issue_**](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Identify-Property-Damage-and-Extreme-Habitability-Situations). If so, dispatch immediately. Otherwise, set appropriate expectations with the resident that we may not be able to find a vendor who can visit the property until the next day. Avoid giving specific timeframes as this can cause further customer frustration if we are unable to deliver on that promise. 

You can say something like, “_As soon as we hang up, I will do my best to find a Service Provider who can address this issue as quickly as possible. Please be aware that because it’s already quite late, we may not be able to secure an appointment until tomorrow. You will receive a text message as soon as the appointment is confirmed, and you can always call or text us back in the meantime if the issue gets worse or you have any other concerns._”

## 🎯 When to Break This SOP

Break or make exceptions to this SOP and escalate immediately when:

- **The situation poses an immediate threat to life, health, or major property damage** (e.g., fire, gas leak, carbon monoxide leak, crime, hazmat, electrical pole or tree falling on the building, major flooding). In these cases, follow the [https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Handle-911-Severity-Type-Work-Orders](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Production-Documentation/How-to-Handle-911-Severity-Type-Work-Orders) instead of standard escalation steps.
- **The resident or anyone on-site is in danger and requires emergency services**—always instruct to call 911 first, then notify the appropriate escalation contacts.
- **You encounter a scenario not covered by the SOP, or you are unsure how to proceed safely**—pause and escalate to an Operations Support Specialist. **Note:** A Harmonia severity misclassification that is clear from intake settings is _not_ an "unsure how to proceed" scenario — correct it directly per the [https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/4035280901](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/4035280901). Escalate to Support only when severity genuinely cannot be determined and a TS task is also not appropriate.
- **You believe the issue is property damaging or extreme habitability, even if the Property Manager’s settings do not define it as an emergency**—contact the Escalation Contacts for permission to dispatch.
- **Multiple emergencies are reported at once and you are unsure how to prioritize or split work orders**—seek guidance before proceeding.
