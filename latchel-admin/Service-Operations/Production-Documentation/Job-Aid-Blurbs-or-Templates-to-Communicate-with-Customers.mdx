## 🧠 What This Process Is Trying to Solve

<Note>
We want to prevent inconsistent, unclear, or delayed communication with customers by providing associates with ready-to-use blurbs and templates for common and difficult scenarios. The goal is to ensure every customer receives a kind, complete, and prompt response—especially when the topic is complex, requires saying no, or involves correcting mistakes—so customers don’t have to follow up for more information.

**We want to include in this list the topics that are:**

1. **Difficult to communicate **- when we have to say no because we don’t do something, or it is a topic complex to explain, or we have made a mistake and have to fix that, etc
2. **Frequent questions or notes** - that we can simply copy and paste into emails, SMS/Webchat, notes and save some time
</Note>

<Note>
**🎓Training Content**

The contents of this page have been included in the **SMS & Webchat Cross-Training **Coassemble course.
</Note>

# Content

## **ℹ️Introduction**

We are providing these blurbs as **guidance **on how to better communicate, especially in situations that are hard to find the right words to say something that customers may not like, or be effective in communicating complicated topics.

We are **not **expecting associates to use this as a script and read them word by word during a phone call. We want associates to continue to use their own communication style and have a natural conversation with customers.

When engaging with customers via email or SMS/Webchat, associates can copy and paste the blurbs and customize as they feel it is needed.

We will continuously add new blurbs as we identify the need for them. If you are working on a message that we don’t have a blurb for it, you can use this Job-Aid as a guide [Job-Aid: Guide to write SMS/Webchat or email to customers](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Job-Aid-Guide-to-write-SMS/Webchat-or-email-to-customers), and add a request for this blurb at the bottom of this page.

#### **General Blurbs applicable to (R), (SP), (Owner) Latchel Positioning statements**

<Accordion title="Overall Positioning Strategy">
Latchel should consistently present itself as the **maintenance intake team** that ensures messages reach the correct party.

- We do **not** position ourselves as the PM.
- We do **not** distance ourselves as “just a third-party.”
- We do **not** shift blame or imply the PM is solely responsible.
- We keep language general unless the resident pushes for specifics.
</Accordion>

For the standard greeting, refer to this guide:

<Accordion title="When a Resident Asks Who the Work Order Is With">
If the resident asks who currently has the work order, use general language first:

**Primary Response:**
*“Your work order is currently with the appropriate team for next steps. I’ve passed along your message to make sure they’re aware.”*

If the resident pushes for specifics:

**Escalated Clarification:**
*“The team currently handling next steps is the property manager assigned to your unit. I’ll make sure your message reaches them.”*

This keeps us in the role of the **intake team** and avoids blame-shifting.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="When a Resident Requests to Be Transferred to the Appropriate Team">
If the resident asks to be transferred, the goal is to maintain professionalism and clarity without implying we’re refusing help:

**Response:**
*“I can take your message and make sure it reaches the right team handling your work order. They’ll follow up directly with any updates.”*

**Escalated Clarification:**

If the resident **insists on speaking directly with the PM**, it is acceptable to clarify politely:

*“The team handling your work order is your property manager. I can make sure your message reaches them and they will follow up directly with you.”*
</Accordion>
