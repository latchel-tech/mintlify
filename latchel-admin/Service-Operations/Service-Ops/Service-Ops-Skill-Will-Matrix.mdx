## **Purpose**

<Note>
Identifying skill or will issues is important because it helps provide targeted solutions and support for associates. It ensures effective development, saves time and resources, boosts morale, and fosters a positive workplace culture.
</Note>

## **Will vs Skill**

**Will Issue**: A will issue refers to a performance challenge where an individual's problems are primarily related to their motivation, attitude, or willingness to perform well. They might have the skills required for the job, but they lack the drive to meet expectations or achieve goals. Addressing will issues involves focusing on motivational strategies, feedback, coaching, or addressing any underlying concerns affecting the associate's motivation.

**Skill Issue**: A skill issue arises when an individual lacks the necessary abilities, knowledge, or expertise to perform a task or job effectively. It typically points to a lack of specific knowledge, training, or technical abilities required for their job. In this case, the individual may have the willingness to perform well but lacks the necessary skills. Resolving skill issues involves providing training, education, mentoring, or resources to enhance the associate's capabilities and improve their job-related skills.

### **How does this matrix work?**

Below are some questions to help you assess where an associate falls on The Skill Will Matrix:

| **Determining Skill Level** | **Determining Will Level** |
| --- | --- |
| How many years of experience do they have in their role?<br/>What are their strengths?<br/>What are their weaknesses?<br/>How much assistance do they need to complete their tasks or role successfully?<br/>How often do they receive negative feedback?<br/>How often do they receive positive feedback? | How ambitious are they?<br/>Do they take on tasks or new responsibilities without being asked?<br/>How engaged are they with their work?<br/>What are their professional goals?<br/>How often have they changed jobs in the past?<br/>What is their general attitude like?<br/>What are their working relationships with their team like? |

After evaluating an associate, you can use this framework to place them into one of four categories:

<AccordionGroup>
<Accordion title="High Skill, High Will">
These associates possess both the necessary skills and a high level of motivation and commitment. They are top performers who consistently excel in their roles. Typically, someone in this category is looking to advance in their career and would make a good manager or leader.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="High Skill, Low Will">
Associates in this category have the required skills but lack motivation or engagement. They have the skills to do their role well, but they aren't engaged in their work. This might be because they're not being challenged enough or are bored in their position and need to find a new goal or incentive to work toward.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Low Skill, High Will">
These associate show high motivation and a positive attitude but lack the skills to perform their duties effectively. They're typically early on in their career and are a great candidate for coaching or training, which can help close any skills gaps.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Low Skill, Low Will">
Associates in this category may require significant coaching, training, or performance management to improve their performance.
</Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
