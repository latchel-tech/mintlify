### 🧠 What This Process Is Trying to Solve

<Note>
  This SOP ensures that emergency work orders are handled efficiently and accurately by guiding troubleshooters to gather all relevant information, assess severity, and take appropriate immediate action. The goal is to prevent missed or delayed responses, reduce property damage, and ensure residents and property managers receive timely, confident guidance; saving time, avoiding unnecessary follow-ups, and improving overall customer experience.
</Note>

<Check>
  📍**Before taking action, reflect on the following:**

  - Does this reduce friction for the customer, or create more?
  - Am I communicating clearly, or leaving them to guess?
  - Am I truly solving the problem, or just applying a temporary fix?
  - Did I address the customer’s real question?
  - Will this interaction leave a memorable, positive impression?
  - If this issue recurs, am I escalating it appropriately, or merely reacting?

  At Latchel, we don’t just support customers—we improve the way support is delivered.
</Check>

<Note>
  These audits are based on [How to: Properly Diagnose Emergency Situations](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/2475851825)
</Note>

<Warning>
  Note for auditors: Only audit troubleshooting calls for the purpose of this Work Order Audit. If the assigned agent has no troubleshooting calls for the week, enter **“No TS call”** in the **“Reviews of the Week”** tab in place of your name.
</Warning>

### Audit Questions

#### Troubleshooting Call Execution

<Accordion title="1. Did the associate take the correct action after reviewing for duplicate or related Work Orders?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="What We're Looking For">
      - Before calling the resident, Troubleshooters reviewed the Maintenance tab and any AI-flagged "Additional issues detected" entries to identify active duplicate Work Orders, related issues, or potential recalls.
      - Review whether the associate identified any existing duplicate, related, or recall Work Orders and took the appropriate action based on the situation.
        - **Select "Yes"** when the associate correctly identified a duplicate, related issue, or recall and took the appropriate action after confirming the situation with the resident.
        - **Select "No"** when a duplicate, related issue, or recall existed but the associate failed to identify it, ignored it, or did not take the appropriate follow-up actions.
        - **Select "Not Applicable"** when no duplicate, related issue, or potential recall existed and no additional action was required.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">

<Accordion title="6. Did the associate properly document new information and outcomes in the WO activity page? (including references to PM rules, Intake Settings, next steps, and clear, actionable notes)">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="What We're Looking For">
      Writing communicative notes directly tells other people what's going on with the Work Order tasks. When the agent is effective in their note writing, other people can quickly understand the Work Order and be able to take subsequent actions quickly without having to research too much. If you want to learn more on writing effective notes you can review this SOP [How to: Write Effective Notes](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/1058211367)

      Notes quality: Should be clear, comprehensive, and action-oriented so others can quickly understand the Work Order and take subsequent steps without further research

      - _Document if any new information was tested_ using the Test Issue feature
      - _Note any adjustments to severity, dispatch decisions, or PM rule considerations_
      - _References to Intake Settings (not severity/responsibility defaults)_
      - _Clear, actionable next steps for follow-up or resolution_

      Scoring:

      - **Yes**: All relevant outcomes documented and notes are clear/actionable
      - **No:** Testing or documentation missing, incomplete, or unclear
      - **Not Applicable:** No new information was gathered (Q1=No) or scenario did not require testing
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - Ensure that the notes entered in the troubleshooting guided workflow and call log are both clear and comprehensive. The inclusion of additional details enhances overall understanding for others
      - Remember that the Property Manager has visibility of all status updates. Writing effective notes regarding these status updates should be informative enough so that when Property Managers are watching agents perform actions live, they can also easily understand the details of agent actions or circumstances of the maintenance request.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage/Actions">
      When you enter a note into the work order, please make sure you are answering the following questions: What is broken/defective in the property?

      - Include the part that is broken (for example: toilet, door, refrigerator, etc.)
      - Clarify the type of issue (for example: leaking, not working, overheating, etc.)
      - Clarify where in the house the issue is located (for example: living room, master bathroom, garage, etc.)
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>

<Accordion title="7. Did the associate appropriately use (or request, if not provided) photos/videos in the work order to guide their actions and decision-making?">

    <Tab title="What We're Looking For">
      - **IMPORTANT:** Any photos available in the work order, or submitted as additional information after troubleshooting, must always be reviewed and used to assess the situation.

<Accordion title="13. Did the agent set expectations for next steps based on the PM's subscription type?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="What We're Looking For">
      Checklist for Setting Expectations:

      1. **Know the PM's Subscription Type**
         - Review the Property Manager's subscription details, intake settings, critical notes, and pre-dispatch instructions.
         - Ensure you are familiar with any limitations or special conditions related to their subscription.
      2. **Communicate Next Steps to the Resident**
         - Inform the resident of who will be handling the next steps (Specify the individual or team responsible for each action using clear and precise language. Example: "Our internal emergency team," "The appropriate team" → when pertaining to the Property Manager, or "The Service Provider.")
         - Clearly state how the resident will be updated (e.g., via SMS, email, phone call).
      3. **Handle Changes in the Dispatch Process**
         - If there are changes during dispatch (e.g., no preferred Service Providers available, or the PM is not enrolled in Last Call Notification), send a follow-up SMS to the resident to clarify new next steps.
         - Use appropriate templates or scripts as necessary. Reference the Job-Aid: Blurbs or Templates to Communicate with Customers for wording suggestions.
      4. **Reconfirm Information When Necessary**
         - If any information is unclear or needs further verification (e.g., subscription details or instructions), double-check before providing expectations to avoid miscommunication.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - **Simplify the next steps**: Break down the upcoming actions in the WO into clear, simple points that the resident can easily understand.
      - **Be specific about roles**: "Our internal emergency team," "The appropriate team" → when pertaining to the Property Manager, or "The Service Provider."
      - **Confirm communication channels**: Double-check if the resident's phone is good for SMS follow-up otherwise add notes to set the best channel to reach him.
      - **Always follow up on changes**: If there's any shift in the process—like delays or changes in dispatch—immediately send a follow-up SMS. Proactively keeping the resident informed can reduce frustration.
      - **Set realistic timelines**: Avoid overpromising. If you're unsure about specific timing, give a reasonable estimate (within the following 24 business hours, during business hours, as soon as possible, etc) and let the resident know they'll be updated as soon as more information is available.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage/Actions">
      - **Emergency Work Order**: "Our emergency team is prioritizing this for you. The goal is to schedule it within the next 24 hours. You'll receive a text with the appointment details as soon as it's confirmed."
      - **Non-Emergency Work Order**: "We've shared the details with the appropriate team, and they will reach out to you with the next steps as soon as possible."
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

#### Defining Immediate Next Steps

<Accordion title="1. Did the agent's actions align with the critical notes, property's pre-dispatch instructions (property, PM, and resident), and escalation contact instructions (located in the escalation contact field)?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="What We're Looking For">

<Accordion title="3. For non-property-damaging or non-extreme habitability emergencies, did the troubleshooter proactively ask the resident whether the issue could wait before contacting pre-dispatch contacts?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="What We're Looking For">
      - After completing troubleshooting steps with the resident, the troubleshooter should assess whether the emergency severity is mild enough that waiting is a legitimate option
      - If applicable, the troubleshooter asked the resident whether they were comfortable waiting for service before proceeding to contact pre-dispatch contacts
      - **If the resident agreed to wait:** the troubleshooter selected "Work order does not need immediate dispatch" and documented the resident's agreement in the WO notes
      - **If the resident needed service immediately:** the troubleshooter proceeded with the standard emergency dispatch flow

      **Yes** - Troubleshooter asked and documented resident's response **No** - Troubleshooter proceeded to pre-dispatch without asking **N/A** - Property-damaging or extreme habitability emergency; standard immediate dispatch flow applies

      _This step does not apply to property-damaging or extreme habitability emergencies - those follow immediate dispatch flow regardless of resident preference._
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - Review the WO activity log notes and call recording to confirm whether the question was asked and how the resident responded
      - If the troubleshooter proceeded directly to pre-dispatch escalation without documenting resident preference, this should be marked **No**
      - Use the troubleshooting question guide as reference to determine whether the scenario qualified as non-property-damaging or non-extreme habitability
      - Refer the [Informational: Basic Service Ops Guidelines and Rules | Operations Hours](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/776701198/Informational+Basic+Service+Ops+Guidelines+and+Rules#%F0%9F%95%92Operations-Hours)
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage/Actions">
      - "Based on what you've described, would you be okay with us scheduling a service provider for tomorrow morning instead of tonight?"
      - "Would you be comfortable if we scheduled this for the next available time rather than dispatching someone this evening?"
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="4. If the resident agreed to defer, were pre-dispatch contacts handled correctly? (Only applicable for non after hours customers)">

      - **Yes:** Pre-dispatch contacts were correctly shifted to business hours and documented
      - **Yes:** Agent followed the pre-dispatch instruction to contact for all emergencies
      - **No:** Pre-dispatch contacts were skipped with no documentation of follow-up during business hours
      - **N/A:** Resident did not agree to defer; standard dispatch flow was followed
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - Review the WO notes and call log to confirm pre-dispatch contacts were not omitted entirely
      - Pre-dispatch instructions that explicitly require contact for all emergencies should still be followed - shifted to business hours, not ignored
      - If there is a conflict between the deferral and the pre-dispatch instructions, verify the WO notes explain how the associate handled it
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage/Actions">
      "Since you're okay waiting, we'll follow up with the necessary contacts during business hours and get this scheduled for you."

    </Tab>

      - Get familiar with property damaging situations

## **🎯 When to Break This SOP**

This SOP covers the majority of cases (80–90%), but unique situations may require deviation or escalation. Break the SOP when:

- _You believe a reA resident’s safety is at risk_
- _A property is at risk of being damaged_
- _You identify an unusual scenario the SOP does not address_
- _You believe an alternative action would better serve the resident or Latchel_
