<Accordion title="5. Did the agent give a warm greeting?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      - The agent started the interaction with a warm and friendly salutation, such as "Hello," "Hi," or "Good \[morning/afternoon/evening\]."
      - The agent personalized the greeting by addressing the customer by name.
      - If the agent was already on a call with the customer and initiated a text conversation simultaneously, the expectation is that the agent still acknowledges the transition in communication with a brief and relevant greeting. The greeting should reflect the ongoing nature of the interaction.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - Crafting a warm and welcoming greeting for customers via SMS is crucial for setting a positive tone and creating a pleasant interaction.
      - Don't assume gender. Instead of asking for Mr./Mrs. \[last name\], just ask for their first name.
      - Derivations of the greetings suggested in the Guidelines column are also acceptable as long as they are warm, friendly and professional.
      - If the customer has engaged us into a specific conversation in the same day, and was already greeted by another agent, the next agent should reply back with a warm acknowledgement. For example: "Thanks for your response.", "Thanks for your fast reply.", "Thanks for providing additional information.", etc.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage">
      - "Hello/Hi (customer's name)! Thank you for reaching out. I'll be happy to help you with …".
      - "Good morning! Please allow me a moment and I will get you an answer about …. ".
      - "Good afternoon! I'll be happy to assist you today. In order to do so, would you please..."
      - "As we discussed over the phone, I'm sending over the details here via text."
      - "Hi, this is (agent name) on the call with you—could you send more details here in the text so I can assist further?
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Coaching Opportunity">
      The greeting was a basic "hi," which came across as robotic and lacked warmth or personalization.
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="6. Did the agent demonstrate familiarity with the conversation history?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      - The agent did not make the customer repeat information that was already discussed previously in the chat, or already noted on the work order.
      - We want to reduce the customer's effort as much as possible, so it's completely fine to ask the customer to wait for a moment while you consult the history of their request!
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - Before responding to the customer, review the conversation history to understand the context and any previous issues or inquiries.
      - Provide context by referencing relevant details from past conversations (if there were recent interactions associated)
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage">
      - Resident adding information to a WO recently created: "I'm glad you could submit your request through our dashboard, I'll add these details to your WO…"
      - PM following up on an emergency: "Our emergency team is trying to reach the SP as you requested earlier, we will inform you as soon as we get the estimate".
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Coaching Opportunity">
      The agent did not explicitly reference details from the conversation history but responded in a way that maintained continuity and addressed the customer's concern.
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="7. Did the agent paraphrase the customer's request/problem to confirm understanding, or clearly state the purpose of our message?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      - The agent restated the customer's inquiry or issue in the agent's own words to demonstrate comprehension and ensure alignment
      - If the purpose of the message is to provide information or assistance, agents should clearly state the intention of the message upfront.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - Be short and to the point, but make sure you are providing enough context so the person knows what you are talking about.
      - If the customer has already discussed the same topic with another agent on the same day, and that agent already paraphrased their request, the next agent should reply back with a warm acknowledgement. For example: "Thanks for your response.", "Thanks for your fast reply", "Thanks for providing additional information.", etc.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage">
      - "Thank you for reporting this, our emergency team will contact the service assigned for your WO \[WO title or number\] to reschedule as soon as possible, aside from \[date/ time provided\] is there any other time you would be available?"
      - "I will take care of \[request, for example, assign SP, contact resident, etc.\] right away..."
      - "I'm reaching out regarding WO #, to inform/ update/ request"…
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Coaching Opportunity">
      The agent generally avoided making the customer repeat information already discussed in the chat but overlooked a minor detail documented in the work order or posted in Slack. However, this did not cause any noticeable frustration for the customer.
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="8. Did the agent empathize and show a sense of urgency?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      See guidance in Call section above (all but tone of voice are applicable)
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - Acknowledge their feelings using empathetic language
      - Choose words and phrases that convey understanding and support.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage">
      - "I'm here to help"
      - "I understand how you feel,"
      - "I'm sorry to hear that"
      - "Rest assured, we're working on this problem and will do our best to find a solution."
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Coaching Opportunity">
      The agent shows empathy but could make it more meaningful by referencing specific details of the customer's situation (e.g., "I'm sorry to hear about your inconvenience with \[specific issue\]"). An apologizing statement was not necessary in this situation, but the agent could have conveyed more empathy while demonstrating urgency.
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="9. Did the agent provide a direct and concise answer to the customer question, or make a direct and concise request for information?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      See guidance in Call section above
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - Before responding, make sure you fully understand what the customer is asking for.
      - Get to the point: Start your response with the most important information or your request right away.
      - If your response requires further action from the customer, such as providing additional information or taking a specific step, clearly communicate what they need to do next.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage">
      "Could you tell me more about the issue you're experiencing?"
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Coaching Opportunity">
      The response answers the customer's question but includes unnecessary details or could get to the point faster.
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="10. Did the agent use good grammar/spelling, and write in complete sentences?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      - The agent wrote using proper spelling and grammar (capitalization, punctuation, etc.)
      - Sentences are complete, clear, and easy to understand; the agent did not use abbreviations or in-house verbiage
      - The tone is professional, polite, and customer-focused.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      Proofread before sending: Before sending your response, take a moment to review it for any errors or unclear language. Ensuring clarity and correctness reinforces professionalism and avoids misunderstandings.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage">
      "Hello \[SP's name\]! We're reaching out about the job assigned to you at (ADDRESS) for (ISSUE). The \[Property Manager/owner\] has requested you submit a formal estimate with the cost breakdown. Please submit the estimate by clicking this link: (LINK). Let us know if you have any further questions. Thank you!"

      Find more helpful scripts in the [Job-Aid: Blurbs or Templates to Communicate with Customers](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/x/iAHoe)
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Coaching Opportunity">
      As long as these errors (e.g., double spaces or misplaced commas) do not alter the message or cause confusion, they can be considered minor.
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="11. Did the agent set appropriate expectations for next steps?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      - The agent clearly communicated what the customer can expect in terms of response times, resolution timelines, and the overall process of addressing their inquiry or concern.
      - When an agent is both on a call and texting with the customer simultaneously, it's acceptable not to set detailed expectations in the SMS conversation itself. However, the agent must use the SMS to refer to the ongoing phone conversation, indicating that the interaction will continue on the call. The key is to ensure that expectations are clearly set during the phone conversation. Failing to do so will result in a markdown.
      - See more guidance in Call section above
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - Put yourself in the customer's shoes: If you were on the other side, was the information provided clear enough to understand what you should expect? Were the actions to be performed after the interaction (by the customer or the agent) properly explained?
      - This is still applicable when the customer is unresponsive. See suggested language in [Job-Aid: Guide to write SMS/Webchat or email to customers](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/2028634993)
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage">
      - "Thank you for the photos, I will attach them to your work order and forward it to your Property Manager who will handle the next steps. Thank you, and please let us know if we can assist you with anything else in the meantime!"
      - "Thank you for providing those details, let's continue discussing your request over the call."
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Coaching Opportunity">
      Partial Clarity: The agent communicated the correct steps but could have been more detailed or concise.
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="12. Did the agent provide the CSAT survey at the end of the interaction?">

    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Coaching Opportunity">
      The associate ended the conversation but later realized the CSAT survey was missed and promptly sent it. (Immediately after the interaction).
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="13. Did the agent offer further assistance or ask about any other open WOs?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      - The agent offered support beyond the initial inquiry or request to ensure their needs were fully met.
      - This is still applicable when the customer is unresponsive. See suggested language in [Job-Aid: Guide to write SMS/Webchat or email to customers](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/2028634993)
      - If the customer is on a call with the agent, it should mention in the SMS that any further assistance will be provided over the phone, while offering the customer the option to use the SMS if needed
      - See more guidance in Call section above
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - Asking in the present ("Do you HAVE any other question?") is a better way to ensure we have answered all of the customer's questions during the interaction instead of only making a future offer ("If need further assistance please reach out")
      - Real-time offers of further assistance reduce customer effort
      - [Job-Aid: Guide to write SMS/Webchat or email to customers](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/2028634993)
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage">
      - "Is there anything else we can assist you with?"
      - "Do you have any other concerns that I can assist you with right now?"
      - "I'll continue assisting you over the call. Feel free to share any additional details with me there, or use this channel if you need to add anything else."
      - Find more scripts and inspiration in [this link](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/x/iAHoe)
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Coaching Opportunity">
      There was an offer of further assistance, but it fell short of demonstrating customer obsession (e.g., 'Anything else?').
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="14. Did the agent give a warm sign off/goodbye?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      - The agent ended the conversation on a positive and friendly note
      - The agent thanked the customer for reaching out or for their inquiry, and used a friendly closing phrase, some options are: "Best regards," "Warm regards," "Thank you for contacting us" or "Have a great day!"
      - Derivations of the above are also acceptable. We want to thank the customer and acknowledge that we are closing the conversation in a friendly way.
      - This is still applicable when the customer is unresponsive. See suggested language in [Job-Aid: Guide to write SMS/Webchat or email to customers](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/2028634993)
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage">
      - "Thank you again for contacting us. Have a nice day!"
      - "It was my pleasure to assist you today! Goodbye".
      - "Please message us again anytime! Have a nice one!"
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Coaching Opportunity">
      The associate provided a brief, warm sign-off, such as "Thank you," instead of offering a more complete and engaging closing, like "Thank you for contacting us" or "Have a great day!"
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="15. Did the agent take appropriate follow-up actions after the interaction? (whether due to a commitment made to the customer or because it was required to close out the inquiry)">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      - The associate should comply with the actions required to close out the inquiry.
      - If the conversation doesn't lead to any follow-up action, this question is "Not applicable".
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - Use the documentation available on how to handle each request: [How to: Handle SMS/Webchat Contacts (New Support Framework)](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/x/IgBELQ)
      - Use the [Help Center Reference Guide](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/x/XoDIm) as a guide for interactions associated with non-emergency requests. It shows when is necessary to take action and when not, based on different scenarios.
      - Make sure you complete the follow-up and leave record in the proper channel (WO, Slack escalations, creating tickets, etc.).
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Coaching Opportunity">
      The score will be adjusted once the associate has reviewed the markdown and provided proof of the actions taken.
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="16. Did the Customer (PM) inform Operations they were blocked?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      Only applicable if the PM informs they are blocked. Prioritize action immediately. Do NOT provide a help guide
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      Do not question their limitation or try to justify that they have access. Always assist them directly.
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="17. What was the blocker?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      A "blocker" is any situation where a Property Manager explicitly states they cannot proceed due to an issue on their end—regardless of what you see on your end. See [list of samples of PM blockers below](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/759431202/How+to+Handle+SMS+Webchat+Contacts+New+Support+Framework#Common-Examples-of-PM-Reported-Blockers).
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      Add a brief explanation of the blocker reported.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage">
      - PM was unable to log in to the portal
      - The PM reported error messages
      - PM unable to edit a work order
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="18. Did the agent escalate to Product support?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      - If the blocker is due to a technical or system limitation that needs investigation/resolution, escalate to Product Support → [How to: Escalate to Product Support](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/1485373477).
      - If the PM is not facing a technical or system limitation, no need to escalate to PS.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      If the PM encounters an error message, please ask them to provide a screenshot for reference.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage">
      "Hello! Thanks for reaching out. I've opened up a ticket for you with the Product Support team so they can take a closer look at what's going on. They will be reviewing your request and will send you a personal response via email (usually within one business day). Thank you for your patience."
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="19. If a Help Guide was shared, which one was it? (add the title of the Help Guide)">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      Always include the exact title of the Help Guide as it appears in the knowledge base (do not paraphrase). If multiple guides were shared, list all relevant titles. If no Help Guide was shared, mark as N/A.
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="20. If no guide was shared, would it have been appropriate to provide one?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      - If a Property Manager reaches out without being blocked by any triggers (simply unsure of how to perform an action) AND the request is not tied to an emergency work order, you can proceed by clarifying the process and guiding them through the applicable steps: We'll first attempt to provide the client with a help article to guide them on taking action themselves.
      - If there is no Help Guide relevant or available in [help.latchel.com](http://help.latchel.com): Take action on behalf of the customer. Submit a Public Latchel Help Article Request in the #svc-ops-requests Slack channel (video). Your input is key to growing and improving the Help Center.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - If the Property Managers express any resistance or dissatisfaction with our process of having them apply the changes themselves for PMs with Autopilot subscription, always escalate to support for a PS ticket.
      - This does not apply to Owners from NOMAD and RentRedi since they don't have access to the console
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage">
      "Absolutely, I can guide you through the steps so you can handle \[action\] directly anytime you need! We have a detailed help guide that explains how to do this. Here's the link: \[Insert Help Guide URL\]. Please take a moment to check it out and let me know if you need any further assistance."
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="21. If no guide was shared, but it would have been appropriate, which guide should have been provided? (add the title of the Help Guide)">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      If no Help Guide was shared but one would have been appropriate, specify the exact title of the guide that should have been provided (as listed in the knowledge base). Avoid using general descriptions—always reference the official title.
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="24. How did the agent demonstrate customer obsession during the interaction?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      - Let the agent know what they did great! Please include anything additional that the agent did that was not included in the questions.
      - For example: The agent went above and beyond on how they communicated or engaged with the customer, and you noticed the customer was happy or having fun during the conversation.
      - The agent went above and beyond to verify the whole situation (work order, settings, etc.) and agent found and resolved other potential issues.
      - The agent might have done something not currently covered in our training. If it was helpful, please note it so we can consider sharing with the rest of the team.
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="25. Provide detailed feedback for 'No' answers, including SOP links where applicable, along with specific suggestions, improvement opportunities, and pro tips in a constructive and supportive tone.">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">

      - Questions answered "Yes" may also have areas of improvement. Note any tips or suggestions in this field too.
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>

### 💡 If You Think This SOP Could Be Improved...

Noticed something clunky or confusing in this process? Or had a situation that wasn’t covered here? Or tried something new that didn’t work?<br /> 👉 Share your suggestion in the [SOP Feedback Form](https://slack.com/shortcuts/Ft07C0TDU4N9/9439ee41aae76a4a602b6c186ce4a6cd) or tell your manager— **we want your input!**

A note for Managers or individuals writing SOPs (you can delete this portion of the template before publishing):

- Avoid creating SOPs that are tightly scripted. Let’s encourage agents to think, not just copy/paste scripts. Incorporate language such as,
  - “Use your judgment here, if the \[WO\] looks slightly different, bring it to your \[manager/ops support team\] to review together.” or
  - “If you’re unsure whether to \[mark it duplicate or escalate\], flag it in Slack using the `[#wo-check` tag\] for peer review.”
- Replace static copy/paste templates/scripts with scripting frameworks. Instead of giving them the exact text to say, offer it like this:
  - **Before**: “Thanks for your help with this. I’m going to send you a text message right now…”
  - **After**: Say something like… “Thanks so much. I’ll text you in a moment. Can you reply once you hear from the utility company?”🎤 Tip: Ask yourself, would I feel reassured by this response if I were in their shoes right now? Empathy goes a long way here.
  - This teaches agents how to think about communication, not just repeat it.
