## Purpose

<Note>
  The purpose of this document is to align Customer Experience reviewers on how to assess the Operations Team’s interactions with our customers. This document also serves as a reference for Ops Associates & Ops Troubleshooters to understand Latchel’s expectations and provide an excellent customer experience.
</Note>

<Accordion title="5. Did the agent consistently communicate warmth and friendliness throughout the entire duration of the call?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      - Warm greeting: Derivations of standard greetings suggested are acceptable as long as they are warm, friendly and professional.
      - Evaluate the agent's ability to maintain a warm and friendly communication style consistently throughout the entire duration of the call.
      - The agent thanked the customer and acknowledged that we are closing the conversation in a friendly way [Job Aid: Call Flow for Outbound Calls | 10. Thank the customer ](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/2504688012/Job+Aid+Call+Flow+for+Outbound+Calls#10.-Thank-the-customer)**.**
      - The agent used a warm sign-off.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - Use a warm tone and appropriate volumen from start to end of the interaction
      - Smile and use your hands while speaking
      - Using the customer’s name at least 3 times throughout the interaction can increase the customer's engagement.[Informational: Latchel Customer Service Tenets - High 5 Your Customers Today! | Tenet 3   Treat Every Customer as a Friend](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/LTG/pages/1159823387/Informational+Latchel+Customer+Service+Tenets+-+High+5+Your+Customers+Today#Tenet-3---Treat-Every-Customer-as-a-Friend)
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage">
      - “Good morning/ afternoon/ evening, \[customer name\]”
      - “Hello, thanks for taking my call. Am I speaking with \[customer’s name\]?”
      - Thanks for that information, \[customer name\]
      - “Thank you again for calling us, \[customer name\], and have a nice day!”
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Coaching Opportunity">
      - The customer ended the call abruptly, without letting the agent complete a proper closure.
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="6. Did the agent notify the customer that the call is being recorded?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      - The agent should advise the call is being recorded at the beginning of the call or as soon as the customer allows to.
      - The call recording reminder **does not apply to voicemails and calls transferred via Harphonia**.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - If the resident is interrupting you at the beginning of the call, advise the call is being recorded as soon as you get a chance.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage">
      - "This call is being recorded for quality purposes."
      -  "I am calling on a recorded line."
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Coaching Opportunity">
      - If **the agent mentions the call recording reminder** but does so **late in the interaction,** it should be addressed in the feedback section not affecting the final score.
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="7. Did the agent introduce himself / herself properly?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      The agents should introduce themselves according to the type of customer with whom they are interacting:

      - **Preferred Vendors**: Call on behalf of PM name
      - **LCN Vendors:** Call as a maintenance coordinator  on behalf of PM name
      - **Property Managers:** Call from Latchel.
      - **Residents:**
        - Call from the dedicated maintenance line on behalf of PM name.
        - **Warm Transfers from Harphonia** (AI Call Center)- Call from the maintenance intake team for \[PM Company Name\].
        - Refer to this script:[Service Operations: Handling Warm Transfers from Harphonia (AI Call Center) | Step 2   Greeting and Verification](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/3774251011/Service+Operations+Handling+Warm+Transfers+from+Harphonia+AI+Call+Center#Step-2---Greeting-and-Verification)
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      Pay attention to the type of customer you are calling in order to introduce yourself properly and avoid confusion.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage">
      - **Preferred Vendors:** “This is \[agent name\] from Latchel calling on behalf of  \[PM name\].”
      - **LCN Vendors:** “Hello, I’m a maintenance coordinator for (name of property management company) Property Management.”
      - **Property Managers:** “This is \[agent name\] from Latchel.”
      - **Residents**: “This is \[agent name\] from your dedicated maintenance line on behalf of \[Property Management Company\]”
      - **Inbound calls:** _“Thanks for calling the recorded maintenance line. This is \[Your Name\]. How can we help you?”_
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Coaching Opportunity">
      - **Introduces themselves but does so in a way that is slightly off** (e.g., minor deviations in wording that do not confuse or mislead the customer).<br />saying "Hi, my name is XYZ from Latchel" instead of "Hi, this is Joner. I’m calling from your dedicated maintenance line."
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="8. Throughout the interaction, did the agent actively listen and adapt to the customer's pace?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      - The agent let the customer explain themselves without interjecting while using short acknowledgements as positive reinforcement to the conversation.
      - The agent may ask probing questions if the customer has slowed or lowered their tone of voice.
      - The agent made an effort to speak at the same speed and rhythm as the caller.
      - This question does not apply to voicemails.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - The main objective is to build effective communication throughout the call.
      - If the customer is struggling to find an idea or the correct word, suggest something!
      - Verbally state our will to listen i.e.. "We're here to help", "please continue", etc
      - Use your own words to repeat what you understood, in a concise way. Find more details on listening to the Customer in our Customer Service Tenets - Tenet 1 - [Informational: Latchel Customer Service Tenets - High 5 Your Customers Today! | Tenet 1   Answer the Question Asked](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/LTG/pages/1159823387/Informational+Latchel+Customer+Service+Tenets+-+High+5+Your+Customers+Today#Tenet-1---Answer-the-Question-Asked)
      - Find more on how to match the customer pace in our Customer Service Tenets page - Tenet 2  [Informational: Latchel Customer Service Tenets - High 5 Your Customers Today! | Tenet 2   Reduce Customer Effort](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/LTG/pages/1159823387/Informational+Latchel+Customer+Service+Tenets+-+High+5+Your+Customers+Today#Tenet-2---Reduce-Customer-Effort)
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage">
      - “I’m sorry to hear that”
      - “Got it!”
      - “Understood”
      - “I appreciate that”
      - “Thanks for letting me know”
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Coaching Opportunity">
      - **Adapts to the customer’s pace partially** but could improve further (e.g., slightly mismatched rhythm or tone, but not enough to disrupt communication).
      - **Occasionally there is a delay in the connection**, which causes brief pauses or overlaps in the conversation, but it doesn’t significantly disrupt the interaction.
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="9. Did the agent clearly state the purpose of the call and confirm understanding?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      - **Outbound calls:** The agent should clearly articulate the purpose of the call to demonstrate attentiveness to the issue.
      - **Inbound calls:** The agent parapharase the customer’s request to show understanding
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - Paraphrasing the customer’s request gives them the opportunity to correct, clarify, or add additional details about the issue.
      - Find more details on paraphrasing in our Customer Service Tenets page - Tenet 1 - [Informational: Latchel Customer Service Tenets - High 5 Your Customers Today! | Tenet 1   Answer the Question Asked](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/LTG/pages/1159823387/Informational+Latchel+Customer+Service+Tenets+-+High+5+Your+Customers+Today#Tenet-1---Answer-the-Question-Asked)
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage">
      - “We received your voicemail asking for a call back, how could I help you?”
      - "We received your WO for (WO title/ Description), do you have a few minutes for troubleshooting?"
      - "So you are experiencing \[paraphrase request/ problem\] and you want to submit a WO, is this correct?"
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Coaching Opportunity">
      - States the purpose of the call but could be more concise or specific to ensure full clarity.
      - The statement or inquiry is **correct but lacks confidence or includes hesitation**, indicating a need for practice or reinforcement.
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="10. Did the agent empathize and show a sense of urgency?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      - The agent used some “apolophrase” in a warm and genuine tone after listening to the customer’s concerns to show empathy and a customer-obsessed attitude.
      - The agent highlighting the severity of Emergency issues is a positive add to the previous but will result in a No answer if does not comply with the statement above.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - Combine an apology with a summary of the issue.
      - Sometimes, instead of apologizing, you can **show appreciation** for the customer having patience with what is going on.
      - Find more details on how to show empathy in our Customer Service Tenets page - Tenet 3 - [Informational: Latchel Customer Service Tenets - High 5 Your Customers Today! | Tenet 3   Treat Every Customer as a Friend](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/LTG/pages/1159823387/Informational+Latchel+Customer+Service+Tenets+-+High+5+Your+Customers+Today#Tenet-3---Treat-Every-Customer-as-a-Friend)
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage">
      - "Thank you for reporting this", "This is very important"
      - "Please allow me to check this for you right away"
      - “I’m really sorry you’re experiencing this and I want to help resolve it as quickly as possible.”
      - “I’m sorry for the inconveniences \[refer problem\] may be causing”.
      - "Oh, I see how frustrating it is that you can't remove this resident. I will remove it for you. To confirm, the resident to be removed is John Smith at property located on 123 Main st?”
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Coaching Opportunity">
      - **Uses empathetic language but could refine their tone** to sound more genuine or warm.
      - **Uses an appropriate response but could expand on it**, for example, by adding a short phrase of gratitude for the customer’s patience or understanding.
      - **Occasionally misses empathetic cues from the customer**, such as failing to acknowledge frustration or stress, but the overall interaction is still professional.
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="11. Did the agent provide a direct and concise answer to the customer's question, or make a direct and concise request for information?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      - The agent provided a solution or answer in a short response that was easy for someone without maintenance expertise to understand.
      - The agent answered the question(s) asked
      - Or, the agent made a clear and succinct request for information from the customer.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - The use of open-ended questions will provide you with more insights into the customer's problem/ request.
      - Don’t make assumptions! As obvious as some things could seem, use concise questions as your allies to make the right decisions.
      - When troubleshooting, ask the resident to take action
      - Rephrase the problem to assure the customer that you have correctly understood their issue and addressed all their concerns accurately.
      - Find more on how to be concise in our Customer Service Tenets page - Tenet 2  [Informational: Latchel Customer Service Tenets - High 5 Your Customers Today! | Tenet 2   Reduce Customer Effort](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/LTG/pages/1159823387/Informational+Latchel+Customer+Service+Tenets+-+High+5+Your+Customers+Today#Tenet-2---Reduce-Customer-Effort)
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage">
      - "Could you tell me more about the issue you're experiencing?"
      - “Would you mind resetting the breaker?” (or any other TS step).
      - "So from what I understood, you're encountering issues with... Is that accurate?"
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Coaching Opportunity">
      - **Provides a correct response but could make it more concise** to reduce customer effort.
      - The speech turned redundant but ended up in a correct solution/ answer
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="12. Was the agent clear and understandable? (language proficiency + connection quality/sound issues)">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      - The call quality was high (not breaking up, no interference or background noise).
      - The agent spoke at a volume that can be easily understood (not whispering or yelling) and was clear.
      - The agent used appropriate language to convey a professional service and avoided the use of jargon.
      - The agent used proper grammar.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - Check your headset prior calling
      - Make sure you have good connectivity
      - Be friendly, but remember we’re dealing with customers so keep it professional
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Coaching Opportunity">
      - Was generally clear but had minor lapses, such as slight headset issues or mispronouncing a word, without significantly affecting the customer experience.
      - There were audio issues caused by internal platform bugs (the issue was escalated in a public Slack channel on the interaction date or had been previously reported)
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="13. Did the agent get a confirmation from the customer that the issue was resolved or the customer was satisfied with the answer?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      - Even if the maintenance issue hasn’t been fully resolved, the customer seemed satisfied with the information/update provided by the agent.
      - The associate asked a question to get the customer's acknowledgement that the assistance (information/update) provided was satisfactory.
      - This question does not apply to voicemails.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - Find more on how to confirm the resolution in our Customer Service Tenets page - Tenet 5 - [Informational: Latchel Customer Service Tenets - High 5 Your Customers Today! | Tenet 5   Solve the Problem](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/LTG/pages/1159823387/Informational+Latchel+Customer+Service+Tenets+-+High+5+Your+Customers+Today#Tenet-5---Solve-the-Problem)
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage">
      - “Has this information been helpful?”
      - “Is this the information you were looking for?”
      - You can also explain the action you took and ask if there is anything else the customer needs - “I have assigned a vendor to the work order. Is there anything else you need help with?”  → If you have taken the wrong action, the customer will raise a question.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Coaching Opportunity">
      - **The customer was rude or uncooperative but** the agent made reasonable attempts to assist despite the customer's behavior.
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="14. Did the agent set appropriate expectations for next steps?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      ### **Checklist for Setting Expectations**

      1. **Clearly Communicate Next Steps**
         - Break down the specific actions in the course of the Work Order.
         - Explain what will happen next and make sure it's clear to the customer.
           - For Service providers, follow this SOP [How to: Set expectations with Service Providers when dispatching](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/x/FgD_mg)
      2. **Identify Who Will Handle Each Step**
         - Specify the individual or team responsible for each action using clear and precise language.
         - Example: "Our internal emergency team," "The appropriate team” → **_when pertaining to the Property Manager_**, or "The Service Provider."
      3. **Notify the custmer on WO/ request Progress**
         - Clarify how the resident will be informed about the progress (e.g., via SMS, email, or phone call).
         - Refer to[Job Aid: Call Flow for Outbound Calls | Step 8: Tell the customer what you’re going to do.](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/2504688012/Job+Aid+Call+Flow+for+Outbound+Calls#8.-Tell-the-customer-what-you%E2%80%99re-going-to-do)
      4. **Align Expectations with Key Factors**
         - Ensure expectations are aligned with the following:
           - **PM’s subscription type**
           - **Severity settings**
           - **Critical notes**
           - **Pre-dispatch instructions**
         - Refer to [Job Aid: Call Flow for Outbound Calls | Step 9: Make the customer know what’s coming next.](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/2504688012/Job+Aid+Call+Flow+for+Outbound+Calls#9.-Make-the-customer-knows-what%E2%80%99s-coming-next "https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/2504688012/Job+Aid+Call+Flow+for+Outbound+Calls#9.-Make-the-customer-knows-what%E2%80%99s-coming-next")
      5. **Follow Up on Changes in the Process**
         - If anything changes (e.g., delays or updated information), send a follow-up SMS to clarify the next steps.
      6. Refer to [How to: Set Expectations for Customers](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/~695965963/pages/edit-v2/3194880005?draftShareId=e1ccd51c-bb7a-4d01-b015-7011d470976a "https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/~695965963/pages/edit-v2/3194880005?draftShareId=e1ccd51c-bb7a-4d01-b015-7011d470976a")
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - **Use Professional and Reassuring Language**: Be clear but friendly in your tone. Assure the customer that their request is being taken care of.
      - **Adapt to Changes in the Process**: If any details change, quickly follow up with the customer to realign expectations.
      - **Reference Job Aids for Consistency**: Utilize job aids or pre-approved scripting to maintain clear, concise communication.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage">
      - **Emergency WO:** "_I will assign a vendor to your request as soon as possible. Once the appointment is confirmed, you will receive an SMS notification with all the details."_
      - **Non-Emergency WO:** "_I will inform_ _the appropriate team. They will handle this work order and reach out to you with the next steps ."_ **SP (Service Provider):** _"I have assigned you the work order and scheduled the appointment for \[date and time\]. After the job is completed, please update the status in your dashboard or contact us with an update_."
      - **_PM (Property Manager):_**\_ "I will inform the appropriate team."\_
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Coaching Opportunity">
      - **Partial Clarity**: The agent communicated the correct steps but could have been more detailed or specific.
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="15. Did agent offer further assistance, ask about any other open WOs, or let the customer know they can text us?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      This question checks to make sure we’ve answered all of the customer’s questions, and that they understand their options for getting in touch with us should anything else come up.

      - The agent must make sure we do not miss addressing any outstanding maintenance during the call.
      - If the resident has open WOs for other issues, the agent should provide them with a status update in an attempt to reduce customer’s effort [Informational: Latchel Customer Service Tenets - High 5 Your Customers Today! | Tenet 2   Reduce Customer Effort](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/LTG/pages/1159823387/Informational+Latchel+Customer+Service+Tenets+-+High+5+Your+Customers+Today#Tenet-2---Reduce-Customer-Effort)
      - The agent let the resident know they can text us anytime for further assistance
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - Ask the customer if you have answered all their questions
      - Listen to the customer, they could either  sound satisfied or uncertain, if that’s the case, make sure you clarify
      - When offering assistance make it real, show genuine interest in helping.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage">
      - “Is there something else I can assist you with today?”
      - “Please let me know if I can help you with anything else.”
      - “While I have you on the phone, do you have an update about work order number….?”
      - “Once you know if you can fit the job in today, you can also text us back at this number if that’s more convenient for you.”
      - Any other derivations of the above.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Coaching Opportunity">
      - There will be no deduction if the caller releases the call before the associate can offer further assistance.
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="16. Did the agent take clear and comprehensive notes of the call?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      - The agent added detailed information on the call log (if they miss adding it to the call log a note should be created).
        - Briefly note:
          - Why you called
          - Who you talked to
          - What was the outcome
      - The agent documented the interaction or updated the WO accordingly, adding notes to the TS workflow, task creation, special notes, etc. (if required).
      - The notes are clear, professional, and grammatically correct for a PM, SP, or Latchel colleague to be able to quickly understand (the use of jargon, abbreviations, and acronyms will result in a No answer) [Job Aid: Call Flow for Outbound Calls | 4. Take notes of important information ](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/2504688012/Job+Aid+Call+Flow+for+Outbound+Calls#4.-Take-notes-of-important-information)**.**
      - This question is not applicable for incoming calls that are not associated with a WO.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - The addition of comprehensive notes, is essential for Property managers and colleagues during the WO follow-up.
      - Write key words during the interaction and complete your notes once the call ends.
      - Ask yourself, what would make my and other’s life easier if I add it to the call log/ note.
      - You can refer to this Job Aid [How to: Write Effective Notes](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/x/JwYTPw) to get more inspiration on how to improve your note-taking skills.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage">
      “Called PM to escalate because there were no vendors available to take the job. PM requested to have the WO forwarded, their team will handle internally”.

      “Called SP to offer job, it was rejected; they are booked until next week.”

      “Called to troubleshoot, I spoke with (resident’s name), who stated the leak started last night and it can't be contained (she has no access to the main water valve). Photos have been attached, and she will be available anytime for service”.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Coaching Opportunity">
      - The agent documented the interaction but **could refine the language for better clarity** or professionalism.
      - The notes summarize the main points of the interaction but **omit a small detail that does not affect the clarity of the message.**
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="17. Did the agent take appropriate follow-up actions after the call? (whether due to a commitment made to the customer or because it was required to close out the inquiry)">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      #### **Follow through check list:**

      1. **Completion of Request:**
         - The associate followed up according to the expectations set during the call: Ensure that all commitments made during the interaction are addressed (create a task, forward the WO, contacted SP, etc)
      2. **Tech Issues Verification:**
         - If dealing with tech issues, the associate posted in Slack (in the #ops-requests channel) using the WO link or the caller account
      3. **WO Notes Review:**
         - The associate added WO notes to refer an escalation to CS including Freshdesk ticket
         - The associate linked any related calls or conversations to the WO
      4. **Resolution Check:**
         - If the concern was resolved during the call and no further action was required, this question is not applicable<br /><br />Refer to [<u>SOP: Work Order Follow-ups and Updates</u>](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/2511372328 "https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/2511372328")
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      - Getting familiar with the request will ease the process of taking the proper actions, this is great guidance for emergency requests [How to: Handle Emergency Work Orders](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/x/HYBCLQ)
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Example Verbiage">
      - Create a task (Dispatch task, PM follow-up task, etc)
      - Escalate to the appropriate team (PM, PS, etc).
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Coaching Opportunity">
      - The associate completed most follow-up steps but **missed a minor follow-through action that did not impact the customer.**
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="18. How did the agent demonstrate customer obsession during the call?">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      - Let the agent know what they did great!
      - Please include anything additional that the agent did that was not included in the questions. For example:
        - The agent went above and beyond on how they communicated or engaged with the customer, and you noticed the customer was happy or having fun during the conversation
        - The agent went above and beyond to verify the whole situation (work order, settings, etc.) and agent found and resolved other potential issues.
      - The agent might have done something not currently covered in our training. If it was helpful, please note it so we can consider sharing with the rest of the team.
    </Tab>
    <Tab title="Tips and Remarks">
      This will raise your highlights during the interaction
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title='19. Provide specific feedback (including links to SOPs when applicable) for any "No" answers.'>
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      - For any questions that received a “No” answer, provide tips and suggestions or sample verbiage to the agent on how they can improve to receive a “Yes” next time.
      - Questions answered "Yes" may also have areas of improvement. Note any tips or suggestions in this field too.
    </Tab>
  </Tabs>
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="20. Escalate here anything in this interaction that could lead to PM complaints, or behavioral issues (ex: being rude to a customer)">
  <Tabs>
    <Tab title="Guidelines">
      - The reviewer should note anything observed that could lead to a PM complaint
      - The reviewer should note any associate behavioral issue
    </Tab>

##  **Grading**

The score depends on the number of questions we're evaluating for each form as seen above. Each question on the review will share the same value, depending on the number of total questions being evaluated.

Example: The outbound call form has 14 questions. Therefore:

100 / 14 = 7,14

This means that for outbound calls, each question would be worth 7,14 points. As a consequence, each markdown would represent a minus 7,14 on the score.

Please note that SMS, WebChat, Email, and Call scores represent an average of all the Customer Experience Reviews received in that category.
