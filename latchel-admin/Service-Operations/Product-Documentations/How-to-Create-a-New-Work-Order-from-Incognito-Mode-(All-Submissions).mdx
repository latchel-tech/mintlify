## 🧠 What This Process Is Trying to Solve


<Note>
We want to prevent errors, delays, and lost information when creating new work orders for maintenance requests received by phone or other channels outside the standard resident portal. The goal is to ensure every work order is accurately created—even when the caller or property isn’t already in the system—by following a secure, step-by-step process in incognito mode.
</Note>


<Note>
**🎓Training Content**

The contents of this page have been included in the **Call Center Cross-Training **Coassemble course.
</Note>

## 📋 Step-by-Step Process


### Process Overview


<Note>
#### Gathering the right information is critical

Residents contact us to request maintenance and if the request is assessed as an emergency, it is routed to the emergency team to handle it. In order to support our customers promptly, **the information collected in this form needs to be accurate so we can find the resident and the property in our system.** If you are having issues understanding the customer, please ask the customer to repeat the information slowly and also confirm the spelling. You can use common words to confirm if the letter you are using is correct. For example: "A" as in "Apple", "B" as in "Boy", "C" as in "Charlie", etc.
</Note>


<Note>
#### Do not log-in

**Do not** log-in to any of your personal accounts when using incognito mode. This will result in an error while creating the Work Order.
</Note>


<Note>
Use the process outlined in this page for all Work Order creation. Some requests will turn out to be Non-Maintenance Requests or from a Non-Resident caller. From there, you will follow this SOP: [How to: Intake Non-Resident Requests (Non-maintenance Requests)](https://help.latchel.com/how-to-intake-non-resident-requests-non-maintenance-requests).
</Note>

### Step 7: Create a New Work Order

Create a Work Order

1. Enter a "title" for the work order and a short description of the issue based on the information you collected in the prior step. Please make sure you are answering the following questions in your description and that the title is a short sentence including ~3 keywords from that description
  1. What is broken/defective in the property?
    - Include the part that is broken (for example: toilet, door, refrigerator, etc.)
    - Clarify the type of issue (for example: leaking, not working, overheating, etc.)
    - Clarify where in the house the issue is located (for example: living room, master bathroom, garage, etc.). Specify as much as you can. For example, if the issue is with a living room door, ask if that is an access door to the enter the unit, if the issue is with the toilet, ask if there is more than one bathroom and if so, which bathroom that toilet is located. The goal is to identify if the issue is to a part of the house that is unique (there is no other alternative for the resident) and needs to be working immediately to allow the resident to have their basic needs addressed.
    - Clarify if the issue is continuous (e.g. non-stop leaking) or if it is intermittent (e.g. leaks only when the faucet is open)
    - Clarify if the issue is damaging the property, if any (e.g. water is infiltrating through the walls, water puddle is damaging hardwood floors)
    - Clarify if the resident has already tried to resolve or mitigate the issue. For example, if the resident has tried to shut-off water valve


2. Example:
  1. Title: Toilet leaking
  2. Description of the issue, including your initials at the end: The master bedroom toilet is leaking at the bottom junction of the toilet with the floor. The leak is continuous and it is creating water puddles in the bathroom. This is an apartment unit and downstairs neighbor is seeing water infiltration in the ceiling. There are other toilets in the unit, but this one is damaging the property. The resident has already tried to shut off the water valve, but water is still leaking.
  3. **‼️IMPORTANT:** If you're creating a Work Order for a common area following the “Requesting maintenance at a home they do not live in” option, as outlined in Step 1, include the following text at the end of the description:
    1. “This Work Order is for a common area, do not contact the resident”


3. During work order creation, you will be prompted to provide resident availability or confirm whether the service provider has permission to enter the property.
  1. If the request was submitted by the Property Manager for a **vacant **unit:
    1. Confirm access instructions with the PM.
      1. Ask how the service provider should access the property (e.g., lockbox code, onsite contact, office key pickup).


  2. Indicate property access correctly in the system (if provided by the resident or the Property Manager)
    1. Select **“Yes”** if the service provider has permission to enter.
    2. Provide detailed and specific access instructions in the designated field (e.g., lockbox location and code, gate code, contact person, etc.).

  3. If a specific schedule or access window was provided, select the appropriate availability option in the system.
  4. **📝 NOTE: **This step is not applicable for On-call customers as they are no longer relevant to On-call product.

How to Handle Maintenance Issues Reported by a customer (Resident/SP/Property Owner) that are Not Yet on Record (Email and Call)


<Note>
_For all non-maintenance requests, follow the guidelines outlined in Step 4.1.c. Requests received via SMS must follow the standard guidelines of providing the resident their dashboard link and asking them to submit the work order on their end, indicating if someone (either the PM or an SP) is already working on it._
</Note>

Sometimes, maintenance-related issues may come in through a customer’s call or via email—whether reported directly by a resident or by someone else, such as a property manager, service provider or third party. In some cases, the message may indicate the following (sample below are not limited):

- A service provider is already on-site working or somebody went to the property to address an issue
- A service provider received a work order but it was not from Latchel (e.g., the PM directly dispatched them without a Latchel WO)
- An owner/resident is following up on a maintenance request that the Property Manager mentioned they are resolving but we do not have them on record

While it might seem like no further action is needed for some of those scenarios, it’s still important that we officially log the concern to ensure proper tracking, communication, and accountability.

Here’s how to handle the situation:

1. **Step 1: **Let the reporter know you appreciate them calling and clarify the current status.

  1. Be sure to note important information like the following to help avoid confusion later when teams or property management review the ticket.

What should I do if a work order is created and I think it might be non-maintenance?

If you reviewed that a work order created is a non-maintenance request, follow the below steps:

1. **Determine the nature of the request:**
  1. If it is clear that the request is non-maintenance (e.g., questions about rent, lease, or other non-repair issues), move to **Step 2**.
  2. If it’s **unclear** whether the issue is maintenance-related or not, **call the resident** to confirm.
    1. If the resident confirms that the issue is maintenance related, keep the work order and proceed to handle the request.
    2. If the resident confirms that the issue is non-maintenance related, proceed to Step 2.
    3. If the resident is unresponsive, create a task to the Property Manager using the Action Required task guidelines.
      1. Refer to [How to: Put a Job on Hold](https://help.latchel.com/how-to-put-a-job-on-hold)



2. **Cancel the Work Order and resubmit the request following the correct path for non-maintenance requests.**
  1. Cancel the work order that was created.
  2. Proceed to re-submit the request following the “**Request Non-Maintenance Help**” path.

3. **Notify the Resident (SMS):**
  1. **Confirmed non-maintenance request:**_Hi [Resident Name], we are reaching out about your submitted work order for “[Title of the Work Order].” We reviewed your request and confirmed that it is non-maintenance, so we’ve cancelled the work order and resubmitted your request to your Property Manager. They’ll be handling this directly for you. Thank you and please let us know if we can help you with anything else._
  2. **Vague or unconfirmed non-maintenance request:**_Hi [Resident Name], we are reaching out about your submitted work order for “[Title of the Work Order].” We tried calling to confirm whether this request involves a maintenance issue but weren’t able to connect with you. Since we couldn’t confirm that this is a maintenance-related request, we’ve notified your Property Manager so they can review and assist you with this request directly. If you still need help with a repair, please reply with more details about the problem so we can reopen this as a maintenance work order if needed._

4. **Notify the Property Manager (if necessary):**
  1. Only relay the request to the Property Manager if absolutely necessary and it does not fit the above scenarios.
    1. For work orders in **“Needs Approval”** status, ensure that a notification is sent to the Property Manager once the work order is cancelled. If no automatic notification is triggered, follow the established process to email the Property Manager’s notification group manually.

What do I do if someone (resident, fire department, police, or other emergency services) reaches out about a 911 or PD/EH situation, but the PM/property management company is disabled or inactive (no subscription)?

Resident safety always comes first — even without an active subscription.

1. **Determine the severity of the situation:**
  1. **If the issue is PD/EH only (not 911):** Route the customer to their Property Manager.
  2. **If the issue is a 911 emergency:** Route the customer to call 911 immediately.

2. **Customer Script:**🗨️ **For PD/EH issues (not 911), say something like:**_"I understand you're dealing with an urgent situation. Since your property management company is currently inactive in our system, we're unable to create a work order at this time. I'd recommend reaching out to your Property Manager directly for assistance. In the meantime, we will send a notification to your Property Manager to make them aware of the situation."_🗨️ **For 911 emergencies, say something like:**_"Your safety is our top priority. Please call 911 immediately for emergency assistance. Since your property management company is currently inactive in our system, we're unable to create a work order, but we will send a notification to your Property Manager to make them aware of the emergency."_
3. **Send a courtesy email to the PM** using the **Failed Escalation Contacts** email under the Emergency escalation contacts tab.

## **🎯 When to Break This SOP**

You may **deviate** from the SOP only in specific, documented scenarios. Here’s when deviation is allowed:

- **You receive explicit instructions from a Property Manager, Owner, or company leadership** that override the standard process (for example, special handling or a unique request).
- **A situation arises that is not covered by the SOP** or you are unsure how to proceed—**in these cases, you must pause and escalate to Operations Support Specialists for guidance**.
- **Following the SOP would cause harm, violate compliance, or prevent urgent escalation** (such as a safety risk or emergency). In these rare cases, escalate immediately and document your actions.
