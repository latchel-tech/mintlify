## 🧠 What This Process Is Trying to Solve


<Note>
We want to prevent delays, confusion, and frustration when customers or service providers have payment-related questions or issues. The goal is to provide clear, consistent, and efficient responses to all payment inquiries—ensuring payments are processed correctly, disputes are handled promptly, and everyone knows what to expect.
</Note>

**Content***


## 📋 Step-by-Step Process


<Note>
For service providers inquiring about invoices, direct them to the relevant help guides for assistance. You can find the applicable resources in the [Service Provider Quick Reference Table](https://help.latchel.com/help-center-reference-guide). Q1: What should be done if the Service Provider refuses to wait for the payment processing time?

Answer: Acknowledge the service provider's desire for a quick resolution and respond as follows:

1. _** “Hi [Service Provider Name]. Thank you for following up regarding your payment. Upon review, I can confirm that the payment is [already being processed/scheduled for processing on {processing date}]. To ensure there are no conflicts with the system that could cause further delays, we recommend allowing the standard processing time to complete. If you haven’t received the payment after the expected timeframe, please don’t hesitate to reach out for further assistance. Thank you for your patience and understanding!”**_
2. If the Service Provider insists on immediate action and expresses frustration:
  1. **Preferred Service Provider:**
    1. Escalate the issue to **Product Support team** by following the guidelines outlined in [How to: Escalate to Product Support](https://help.latchel.com/how-to-escalate-to-product-support).

  2. **LCN/LVN Service Providers:**
    1. Post a request in the _**work-order-payments**_ channel for assistance, utilizing the 'Payment Inquiry' workflow.

  3. Ensure to document all relevant details before escalation to assist with a timely resolution.
    1. Refer to [How to: Write Effective Notes](https://help.latchel.com/how-to-write-effective-notes)



Q2: What if a service provider follows up about a payment that we cannot locate in our system?

Answer:

1. Probe further by asking the appropriate questions to gather more details. Ask for essential details to help identify the payment and related work order (aside from the work order and property address):
  1. **Name of the Resident or Property Manager That Was Serviced**
 _**"Hi [Service Provider Name]! To help us locate the job you invoiced for, could you provide the name of the resident and the Property Manager they belong to? This will help us narrow down the work order."**_
  2. **Invoice Number or Reference Number**
 _**"Hi [Service Provider Name]! Do you have the invoice or reference number for the job? This will be critical in cross-checking the payment details in our system."**_
  3. **Date the Invoice Was Submitted**
 _**"Hi [Service Provider Name]! Could you let us know the date you submitted the invoice? This helps us match it to the correct work order in our records."**_
  4. **Service Completion Date**
 _**"Hi [Service Provider Name]! Can you confirm the date the job was completed? We’ll cross-check this with the invoice submission date and work orders in our system to help us locate the job in question"**_
    1. **Details About Other Jobs Completed by the Service Provider**
 _**"Hi [Service Provider Name]! Have you completed any other jobs recently? Sharing details about those might help us locate the invoice or work order more quickly."**_

  5. **Additional Relevant Information**
 _**"Hi [Service Provider Name]! Is there any other information you can provide, such as the type of service, location, or unique details? This will help us identify the work order or invoice."**_

2. If after doing this, the payment is still unable to be found, advise the service provider that we will need information to proceed.
  1. _** "Hi [Service Provider Name]. Thank you for reaching out regarding your payment. To proceed with processing your request, please provide either the Latchel order number or the property address associated with the service. This will help us locate the correct work order and ensure timely resolution of your inquiry. We suggest reaching out to us once you have the necessary information related to the job. Thank you for your cooperation!"**_

3. **If the work order has ALREADY been posted:**
  1. Do **not** create a duplicate post.

Q5: Can I process payment through a payment link included in a service provider’s invoice or request?

Yes, Ops team **may process payment through a payment link** if the total amount is **below $500**. However, please proceed with caution and ensure the following before making the payment:

- **Verify that the invoice corresponds to the correct work order.** Double-check that the property address on the invoice matches the one on the work order.
- **Confirm that the work described matches the services completed.**
- **Make sure no prior payments have been made** for the same invoice to avoid duplicate payments.
- Ensure the job has been confirmed completed by the tenant or verified via completion photos.

This extra diligence helps prevent errors and ensures we’re paying accurately and securely.

Q6: What should I do if an Owner or Property Manager wants to pay an LCN/LVN Service Provider directly instead of following our standard process?

1. **Reiterate the Standard Payment Process**
  - Inform the Owner/Property Manager that all payments for LCN/LVN Service Providers must be processed through our system.
  - Clarify that this ensures proper documentation, payment tracking, and compliance with our procedures.
  -  **Say something like**: "_Thanks for letting us know. Since this work order is assigned to an LCN/LVN Service Provider, payment must be processed through our system via credit card. This ensures that the payment is properly documented, tracked, and compliant with our process. If you’d like to move forward, we’ll continue with the standard payment process._"

2. **Confirm Willingness to Proceed**
  - If the Owner agrees, continue with the work order as usual.
  - If the Owner does **not** agree and insists on handling payment out-of-pocket:
    - **Do not change the Work Order status.**
    - Escalate the case to the **Payments team via task** so they can communicate directly with the Property Manager.


3. **Important Notes**
  - Do **not** approve or process direct payments outside of the LCN/LVN payment system.
  - Always document the Owner/PM’s request in the Work Order notes for reference.


Q7: What if a Service Provider follows up for payment from a Property Manager who no longer has a Latchel subscription?

Since the Property Manager is no longer subscribed with Latchel, we are not responsible for coordinating or processing any payments on their behalf. _This applies regardless of whether the Service Provider is LCN, LVN, or preferred._

1. **Redirect the Service Provider**
  - Inform the Service Provider that they will need to follow up directly with the Property Manager for payment.
  -  **Say something like**: "_Thanks for reaching out. Since the Property Manager associated with this work order is no longer active with Latchel, we are unable to coordinate or process payment on their behalf. You’ll need to follow up with the Property Manager directly regarding payment for this service._"

2. **Documentation**
  - Add notes in the Work Order summarizing the request and your response for recordkeeping.


Q8: How should I handle Service Provider inquiries about W-9 forms?

These documents are managed exclusively by the Payments team.

**If there is a related work order:**

1. **Create a Task for Payments.**
  - When you receive an escalation or direct request regarding a W-9, immediately create a task assigned to **Payments**.
  - Include any relevant details (e.g., Work Order number, Service Provider name, and the context of the request).
  - Once the task is created, the Payments team will take ownership of handling the W-9 process.

2. **Document the Action.**
  - Add a note in the escalation or Work Order confirming that the task has been created for Payments.


**If there is no related work order:**

- **If the request was received via email:** Escalate to Operations Support Specialists via the `ops_info_center` so they can reassign the ticket to Finance under Samantha.
- **If the request was not received via email:** Post the query in the `ops_info_center` and tag Samantha for further assistance.

Q9: What should I do if a service provider requests a down payment after an estimate has already been approved? Is there a maximum percentage allowed?

The standard practice of our Payments team is to allow up to 50% of the total approved amount as a down payment. The percentage may vary depending on the reason (e.g., securing an appointment or purchasing materials), but it should never exceed 50% of the approved budget.

When an Ops associate receives this type of inquiry, the associate must:

- Ensure the service provider has submitted an estimate.
- The exact amount they are requesting for down payment must be confirmed.

**Steps**

- For PD/EH situations:
  - Call the escalation contacts to notify them of the requested amount and confirm approval.
  - Once the PM approves, proceed with:
    - Processing the amount (if less than $500)
    - Create a task to Payments team and posting it in the [https://latchel.slack.com/archives/C07HPH1ELUD](https://latchel.slack.com/archives/C07HPH1ELUD)channel to process it. (if more than $500)


- For non-PD/EH situations
  - Create a task to the Property Manager notifying them that the service provider is asking for approval of the down payment.
  - Once the PM approves, proceed with:
    - Processing the amount (if less than $500)
    - Create a task to Payments team and posting it in the [https://latchel.slack.com/archives/C07HPH1ELUD](https://latchel.slack.com/archives/C07HPH1ELUD)channel to process it. (if more than $500)



Q10: What should I do if a Service Provider follows up on payment while the Work Order is not yet marked as Completed?

**📝 NOTE**: **Payment can only be processed once an invoice has been received and we’ve confirmed that the work has been completed. **If the Service Provider (SP) has already submitted an invoice, treat this as their confirmation that the job is complete. Refer to [How to: Confirm Completion of the Work Order with Resident and Service Provider](https://help.latchel.com/how-to-confirm-completion-of-the-work-order-with-resident-and-service-provider), [How to: Upload an Invoice for a Work Order](https://help.latchel.com/how-to-upload-an-invoice-for-a-work-order)

1. **If no invoice has been submitted:**
  - Request that the SP submit an invoice if they believe the work is complete and the resident does not dispute it.
  - Advise the SP that payment cannot be processed until the work order is marked as completed.

2. **If there is a resident dispute or the work order is still pending due to unresolved issues:**
  - Focus first on resolving the resident’s concerns or confirming completion with the resident.
  - Communicate to the SP that payment will be processed only after the work order is confirmed as complete.

3. **If the SP is unaware of any unresolved issues:**
  - Use this as an opportunity to confirm with the SP whether they are aware of the resident’s feedback.
  - Proceed based on the outcome and apply the appropriate SOPs for completion and payment.

4. **Always document all communications and actions taken in the work order notes.**
  - If the situation is unclear or there is a dispute, escalate to Operations Support Specialists for further guidance.

Q14: What should I do if a Service Provider reports that they submitted a duplicate invoice on a work order?

If a Service Provider contacts us to report a duplicate invoice submission and requests cancellation to avoid double payment, follow these guidelines based on the system behavior:

**Understanding System Behavior:**

- **Simultaneous Uploads:** If two identical invoices are uploaded at the same time, the system automatically cancels one to prevent duplicate payment (regardless of whether the amounts match).
- **Sequential Uploads:** If an invoice has already been paid and the provider uploads it again later, the system **will not** stop it automatically. These must be manually cancelled and marked as "Duplicate" to protect the budget.
- **Budget Overages:** If a new invoice exceeds the approved budget, the system will automatically cancel the transaction due to insufficient funds.

**Steps:**

1. Navigate to the work order and open the **Invoices** tab.
2. Identify the duplicate invoice by comparing invoice numbers, amounts, and upload dates.
3. **If the system has not already cancelled the duplicate:**
  - Manually cancel the redundant invoice.
  - Add a note on the cancelled invoice indicating it is a **"Duplicate"**.

4. **If one of the invoices has already been paid:**
  - Do **not** cancel the paid invoice.
  - Cancel the unpaid duplicate and mark it as "Duplicate."
  - If both invoices appear as paid, escalate to the **Payments team** via the **#work-order-payments** channel immediately.

5. Document the action in the work order notes, including:
  - Which invoice was cancelled and why.
  - The SP's confirmation that it was a duplicate.
  - Any relevant work order or invoice numbers.

6. Confirm with the Service Provider that the duplicate has been resolved:
  - _**"Hi [Service Provider Name], thank you for letting us know. I've reviewed the invoices on work order [work order number] and cancelled the duplicate. You will only be paid once for this job. If you have any other concerns, please don't hesitate to reach out."**_


**When to Escalate to the Payments team** via the **#work-order-payments** channel **:**

- If both duplicate invoices show as **"Paid"**,
- If you are unable to cancel the duplicate invoice (no cancel option available)

Q16: What should I do if an SP's invoice doesn't match the approved budget because of taxes?

The approved budget is the **total amount the SP is authorized to be paid**, and this includes taxes. When Service Providers submit an estimate, they are expected to factor in all costs — including taxes, materials, and labor — into that estimate.

**Key Rule:** Always compare the **invoice total (including taxes)** against the approved budget — not the subtotal.

**If the invoice total (with taxes) exceeds the approved budget:**

- This is a **budget mismatch** — even if the subtotal (before taxes) matches.
- Follow the standard budget approval process per the SOP.
  - Refer to [(Non-Emergency) How to: Handle Over Approved Budget (aka Estimate)](https://help.latchel.com/non-emergency-how-to-handle-over-approved-budget-aka-estimate)  and [(⛑EMERGENCY) How to: Handle Changes to the Approved Budget](https://help.latchel.com/emergency-how-to-handle-changes-to-the-approved-budget).

- Do **not** approve or process the invoice without proper budget approval.

**If the SP says the overage is "just taxes":**

- Remind the SP that their estimate should have already included taxes:
  - _**"Hi [SP Name], thank you for submitting your invoice. Please note that the estimate you provide should already include all costs such as taxes, materials, and labor. Since the invoice total exceeds the approved budget, we'll need to go through the budget approval process before we can proceed with payment."**_


**💡 Remember:** This applies to all budget discussions with SPs — estimates must always reflect the full cost including taxes. This is consistent across all budget-related SOPs.
