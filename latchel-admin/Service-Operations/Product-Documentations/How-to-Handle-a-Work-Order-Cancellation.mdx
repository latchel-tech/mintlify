## 🧠 What This Process Is Trying to Solve


<Note>
We want to prevent confusion, missed communication, and unnecessary work when a work order needs to be cancelled—whether the request comes from a resident, property manager, or service provider. The goal is to ensure every cancellation is handled promptly, with clear documentation and proper notification to all parties involved.
</Note>


## Content


<Note>
Can a vendor cancel a Work Order?

Service Providers can "take themselves off the job/Work Order", but Service Providers cannot cancel an entire Work Order _through their portal_; they don't have the option to do that. For any service provider requests for cancellation, refer to [https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/768999990/How+to+Handle+a+Work+Order+Cancellation#Handling-a-Service-Provider-cancellation](https://help.latchel.com/how-to-handle-a-work-order-cancellation).

For requests related to LVN assigned WOs, refer to [Handling Work Orders Assigned to LVN Service Providers](https://help.latchel.com/handling-work-orders-assigned-to-lvn-service-providers).
</Note>


## 📋 Step-by-Step Process


### Handling a Property Manager cancellation

1. Upon receipt of the cancellation, the agent acknowledges the cancellation.
  1. _ _**Say something like**_: “Thank you, we have received your request to cancel work order ### (subject) at (address)..."_
  2. If the WO is Non-Emergency :
    1. Continue to process the Cancellation.
      1. Review the cancellation message. Which of the following types of messages did they write?
        1. **<u>Generic cancellation.</u>** For example, "I want to cancel this Work Order".
          1. If there is an indication that this cancellation might have any "concerning factors", ask the Property Manager for more information about why they are cancelling.
          2. If the Work Order maintenance request "makes sense" then proceed with [Cancelling the Work Order](#).

        2. **<u>Cancellation that indicates damage to Property Manager relationship with Latchel.</u>**
          1. Notify the Customer Success team.
          2. Refer to [How to: Escalate to Product Support](https://help.latchel.com/how-to-escalate-to-product-support)

        3. **<u>Mass Cancellation of Work Orders.</u>** For example, the Property Manager has unexpectedly cancelled multiple Work Orders at one time.
          1. Notify Customer Success team.
          2. [How to: Escalate to Product Support](https://help.latchel.com/how-to-escalate-to-product-support)




  3. If the WO is an emergency:
    1. Forward the cancellation to the emergency team.
      1. Create a task and assign it to the Troubleshooter Team. Refer to [Job Aid: What is a task?](https://help.latchel.com/job-aid-what-is-a-task)


  4. Service Provider notification
    1. Refer to [https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/768999990/How+to+Handle+a+Work+Order+Cancellation#Service-Provider-Notification](https://help.latchel.com/how-to-handle-a-work-order-cancellation).




### Handling a Resident cancellation

1. Upon receipt of the cancellation, the agent acknowledges the cancellation.
  1. **Say something like**_:_ _"Thank you, we have received your request to cancel work order ### (subject) at (address)..."_

2. Double-check that "cancellation" of the Work Order is what the resident is trying for.
  1. Often residents will say "cancel" when they mean "reschedule".
    1. Agents also need to make notes in the work order as to why the resident canceled because the property manager expects to see this information.

  2. If the job is a Non-Emergency
    1. Continue to process the Cancellation.
      1. Review the cancellation message. Which of the below types of messages did they write?
        1. **<u>Generic cancellation.</u>** For example, "I want to cancel this Work Order".
          1. If there is an indication that this cancellation might have any "concerning factors", ask the resident for more information about why they are cancelling.
          2. If the Work Order maintenance request "makes sense" then proceed with [Cancelling the Work Order](#).




  3. If the WO is an emergency:
    1. Forward the cancellation to the emergency team.
      1. Create a task and assign it to the Troubleshooter Team. Refer to [Job Aid: What is a task?](https://help.latchel.com/job-aid-what-is-a-task)


  4. Service Provider notification
    1. Refer to [https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/768999990/How+to+Handle+a+Work+Order+Cancellation#Service-Provider-Notification](https://help.latchel.com/how-to-handle-a-work-order-cancellation).


3. A resident may request to cancel a WO to hire their own service provider. When you received this request, follow the below steps:
  1. **📝 NOTE:** If the resident has not requested cancellation but has _indicated they plan _to hire their own vendor and bill the PM due to frustration with updates or timing, see **item #5** below.
  2. _If the service provider has already gone out of the property:_
    1. _ _**Say something like**_: “Hello [**resident's name**]! Thank you for reaching out. Since a service provider has already been dispatched to your property, we cannot cancel the work order without approval from your Property Manager. I've created a task to notify your Property Manager of your request. If they approve, you may then hire your own vendor. We'll update you once we receive a response from your Property Manager. Thank you and have a great day!”_
    2. Create a task for the PM without changing the work order status. In the task notes, include:
      1. _'The resident is requesting to cancel the work order as they plan to hire their own vendor. We are holding off on cancellation pending your confirmation. Please advise how you’d like to proceed._


  3. _If the service provider has not been out yet or no SP has been assigned:_
    1. _Blurb: “Hello [**resident's name**]! Thank you for reaching out. You can cancel this work order directly through your resident dashboard. Please include a note indicating you plan to hire your own vendor. Here's a guide that walks you through the process: _[_https://help.latchel.com/s/topic/0TO5e000000h9wrGAA/Cancelling-A-Work-Order-Resident_](https://help.latchel.com/s/topic/0TO5e000000h9wrGAA/Cancelling-A-Work-Order-Resident)_. If you have any questions or need further assistance, please don't hesitate to contact us. Thank you!”_
    2. If the resident refuses to cancel on their end or asks you to do it for them, you may proceed with assisting them.
      1. Refer to the guidelines in [Cancelling the Work Order](#).



4. A resident may request to cancel the service if the job is no longer needed and they have resolved the issue themselves.
  1. Update the work order status to **“Cancelled.”** Include detailed notes explaining why the work order was moved to Cancelled status, including confirmation from the resident.
  2. Contact the currently assigned service provider and advise them to disregard the job. Confirm whether they intend to bill for any cancellation or dispatch fees.
    1. _ _**Say something like**_: _ ”_Hi (Service Provider). We would like to inform you that this job has been canceled, as the resident confirmed the issue has been resolved. Please disregard any work related to this order._
_If your team will be billing for cancellation or dispatch fees, kindly send the invoice to _[_**team@latchel.com**_](mailto:team@latchel.com)_.”_
    2. Create a task for property management advising them of what the resident has informed us, and provide them with information on whether the currently assigned service provider is billing or not. A sample task for property management would be:
      1. **Who is this task about?** PM
      2. **Set a date and time for the follow-up task: **[leave blank]
      3. **Task priority: **Normal Severity - Medium, Urgent Severity - High, Emergency Severity - Highest
      4. **User group*: **Choose the user group with “PM” in the title
      5. **Assign to a User: **[leave blank]
      6. **When should this cancel? **Cancel on any state change
      7. **What action or information is for the Property Manager? **Hi. The resident informed our team that they resolved the issue independently, and service is no longer needed. The work order has been canceled, and the assigned service provider has been notified to disregard the job.
(Include additional details here if the service provider confirmed they will be billing for cancellation or dispatch fees.)



5. A resident states they plan to hire their own service provider and bill the PM if they don't receive an update by a specific time.
  1. The resident may give a specific timeframe (e.g., "if I don't hear back by 5 PM") or may state they want to hire someone right away. In either case, this is different from a cancellation request — the resident has not asked to cancel the WO. They are expressing frustration with the pace of updates or service.
    1. **Acknowledge the resident's frustration and provide an immediate update on the WO status.**
      1. Review the WO for the latest activity (SP scheduling status, pending estimates, etc.) before responding.
        1. 💬 **Say something like:** _"Hi [resident's name]! Thank you for reaching out, and I completely understand your frustration. Let me look into the current status of your work order right away."_

      2. After reviewing, provide a specific update (e.g., SP is scheduled for X date, we're waiting on an estimate, etc.). Avoid vague responses like "someone will be in touch."
        1. **Do NOT agree to or validate the resident hiring their own vendor and billing the PM.**
        2. We cannot authorize a resident to hire their own vendor and send the bill to the PM. That decision is up to the PM.
          1. 💬 **Say something like:** _"I understand your concern, and I want to make sure this gets resolved as quickly as possible. Unfortunately, we're unable to authorize hiring an outside vendor and billing your Property Manager on your behalf — that would need to come directly from them. However, let me make sure we're doing everything we can on our end to move this forward."_

        3. **If the resident still wishes to proceed with hiring their own vendor**, inform them that the PM may not reimburse them, and that they would need to cancel their current work order request first before making their own arrangements.
          1. 💬 **Say something like:** _"If you'd still like to go ahead and hire your own vendor, please be aware that your Property Manager may not reimburse you for those costs since it was not authorized through them. If you'd like to proceed, you would need to cancel your current work order request first. You can do that through your resident dashboard, or I can note your cancellation request here. Would you like to move forward with cancelling?"_
          2. If the resident confirms they want to cancel, follow the standard cancellation process outlined in **item #3** above (resident wants to cancel to hire their own SP).


      3. **Take action to move the WO forward.**
        1. If the SP is unresponsive or hasn't scheduled, follow the standard follow-up procedures in [SOP: Work Order Follow-ups and Updates](https://help.latchel.com/sop-work-order-follow-ups-and-updates).
        2. If the SP is locked in/PM-assigned and unresponsive, escalate per existing guidelines.

      4. **Create a task for the PM to make them aware of the resident's frustration and their intent.**
        1. **Subject:** _Urgent: Resident Dissatisfaction — Intent to Hire Own Vendor_
        2. **Who is this task about?** PM
        3. **Task priority:** High
        4. **User group:** Choose the user group with "PM" in the title
        5. **Assign to a User:** [leave blank]
        6. **When should this cancel?** Cancel on any state change
        7. **Description:** _"Hi, the resident has expressed frustration with the timeline of their work order and has indicated they plan to hire their own plumber/vendor and send you the bill. [If the resident gave a specific timeframe, include it here, e.g., 'They stated they would do so if they don't receive an update by 5 PM today.'] We have provided the resident with a status update and informed them that hiring their own vendor and billing the PM would require your direct authorization. Please advise how you'd like to proceed. Current WO status: [insert status]. Thank you."_

      5. **If the resident's issue qualifies as property damaging or extreme habitability**, follow the standard PD/EH escalation process. Refer to the [Job Aid: PM Outbound Communication for Emergency WOs](https://help.latchel.com/job-aid-pm-outbound-communication-for-emergency-wos).





### Handling a Service Provider cancellation

1. Upon reception of the cancellation, the agent acknowledges the request.
_ _**Say something like**_:_ _"Thank you, we have received your request to cancel work order ### (subject) at (address)...”_
2. Clarify the Intent behind the cancellation request:
  1. Sometimes, Service Providers say “cancel the job” but actually mean:
    1. They want to be removed from the WO (but the job still needs to be done).
    2. There is no longer an issue, and therefore, no further work is required.
_ _**Say something like**_:_ _“We just wanted to clarify whether no work is needed for this work order or if you would like this reassigned to another vendor.”_

  2. **Scenario 1**. If the service provider informs that work is no longer required because the resident canceled it directly with them, or after discussing with the resident they confirmed that the work is no longer needed
    1. Proceed to call the resident to confirm this information.
      1. If the resident confirms that service is no longer required, proceed to cancel the work order. Refer to [https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/edit-v2/768999990#Handling-a-Resident-cancellation](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/edit-v2/768999990#Handling-a-Resident-cancellation)
      2. If the resident indicates that the issue is not resolved and service is still needed, proceed to schedule the work with the service provider.
      3. If the resident is unresponsive, move the work order status to Pending and create a task for the Property Manager
        1. Subject: **Action Required: PM Instructions or Information Needed**_._
        2. Who is this task about? _Select the PM_
        3. Select a Date and Time: _Leave as is_

Kindly provide the requested information or guidance at your earliest convenience to help ensure a smooth continuation of the process. If you have any questions or need further clarification, please don’t hesitate to reach out. Thank you!_



  3. **Scenario 2.** If the Service Provider indicates they have directly discussed the work order with the Property Manager—whether or not they’ve advised that the work is complete—and there is any indication of pending work or ongoing discussion with the PM that is not documented or they will not invoice/have been paid directly by the resident/owner
    1. Move the work order status to Pending and create a task to the Property Manager
      1. Subject: **Action Required: PM Instructions or Information Needed**_._
      2. Who is this task about? _Select the PM_
      3. Select a Date and Time: _Leave as is_
      4. Assign to a User Group: _PM user group_
      5. Assign to a User: _Leave as is_
      6. Priority:_ Leave as is_
      7. _Complete task only on Completed or Cancelled job States_
      8. Description: _We’ve created this task for the Property Manager to [insert specific information or instructions needed]. The current status of the work order is [insert status]. Kindly provide the requested information or guidance at your earliest convenience to help ensure a smooth continuation of the process. If you have any questions or need further clarification, please don’t hesitate to reach out. Thank you!_


  4. **Scenario 3.** If the Service Provider (on site) advised that they are with the PM and the PM instructs to cancel the work order
    1. Proceed to cancel the work order. Refer to [https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/edit-v2/768999990#Handling-a-Property-Manager-cancellation](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/edit-v2/768999990#Handling-a-Property-Manager-cancellation).

  5. **Scenario 4.** If the Service Provider requests to be removed from a Work Order:
    1. If the job is a Non-Emergency, and:
      1. The Service Provider is assigned by the PM/locked into the work order
        1. Escalate to Operations Support Specialists to move the WO to "Waiting for PM." Refer to[How to: Escalate Between Teams](https://help.latchel.com/how-to-escalate-between-teams)

      2. The Service Provider is NOT assigned by the PM
        1. Proceed to remove the Service Provider from the WO.
          1. **Note**: For Autopilot WOs, you will allow the automation to handle the next steps. Refer to [How to: Handle Autopilot WOs](https://help.latchel.com/how-to-handle-autopilot-wos)
          2. WOs for Rentredi/NOMAD → Follow appropriate critical notes before proceeding to reassign a service provider. Refer to [https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OM/pages/786859062/TECH+101+How+to+Reassign+a+Service+Provider?atl_f=EOPREC](https://help.latchel.com/how-to-reassign-a-service-provider)



    2. If the job is an Emergency, and:
      1. The Service Provider is assigned by the PM, you will need to escalate this following one of the paths below:
        1. If the issue is Property Damaging/Extreme Habitability
          1. Proceed to call the Emergency Escalation Contacts to relay the information and obtain further instructions. Refer to [Job Aid: PM Outbound Communication for Emergency WOs](https://help.latchel.com/job-aid-pm-outbound-communication-for-emergency-wos)

        2. If the issue is NOT Property Damaging/Extreme Habitability
          1. Move the work order status to Pending and create a task for the Property Manager
            1. Subject: **Action Required: PM Instructions or Information Needed**_._
            2. Who is this task about? _Select the PM_
            3. Select a Date and Time: _Leave as is_
            4. Assign to a User Group: _PM user group_
            5. Assign to a User: _Leave as is_
            6. Priority:_ Leave as is_
            7. _Complete task only on Completed or Cancelled job States_
            8. Description: _We’ve created this task for the Property Manager to [insert specific information or instructions needed]. The current status of the work order is [insert status]. Kindly provide the requested information or guidance at your earliest convenience to help ensure a smooth continuation of the process. If you have any questions or need further clarification, please don’t hesitate to reach out. Thank you!_



      2. The Service Provider is NOT assigned by the PM

## **❓Frequently Asked Questions:**

FAQ: How should we handle work orders that remain open beyond a reasonable timeframe (e.g., over 6 months) and continue to appear as available jobs for vendors?

1. **Confirm the timeframe:**
 If the work order has remained **Open **beyond the acceptable timeframe (including but not limited to _Finding SP_ status), it should no longer remain active.
2. **Notify the Property Manager/ Owner**
  1. **Property Manager:  **
    1. Create a **task for the PM** informing them that the work order will be **canceled due to inactivity or prolonged open status**.
    2. Clearly explain the reason for the cancellation (e.g., _Service Providers are reaching out to confirm if the job is still available, but the work order has been open for more than 6 months_).

  2. **Owner**:
    1. **Call the owner** to relay the situation. If they remain **unresponsive**, send an **SMS notification**.
      1. **Say something like: **_“Hello (Owner), we are reaching out regarding your maintenance request. We would like to inform you that the work order (##) will be canceled due to prolonged inactivity, as it has remained open for over six months (date).  __Service Providers have recently reached out to confirm whether the job is still available. As there have been no recent updates, we will proceed with canceling the work order.__If the issue still requires attention, please feel free to submit a new request or contact us, and we will be happy to assist.”_



3. **Cancel the work order:**
  - Proceed with canceling the work order.
  - Document all actions taken in the work order notes.



### Cancellation of Test WOs

Test work orders are not real maintenance requests and typically need to be canceled after review.


<Note>
**How to identify test work orders:**

- The work order description or title includes the word “test,” “sample,” “demo,” or similar language.
- The work order was created by a team member or system user known to run tests (e.g., Operations, Product, or QA staff).
- The property address or resident information is obviously fake, incomplete, or uses placeholder text (like “123 Test St” or “John Doe”).
- There is no real maintenance issue described, or the issue is clearly not actionable (e.g., “Testing system,” “Please ignore,” or “Sample WO”).
- The work order was created during a known testing window, training session, or after a system update.
- The work order has not been assigned to a real service provider or has no follow-up activity typical of real requests.
</Note>

Occasionally, you may encounter a work order that appears to be a test (not a real maintenance request). To ensure these are handled consistently and do not disrupt reporting or workflows, follow these steps:

1. If you believe a work order is a test, escalate the case in the `ops_info_center` Slack channel.
  - Include the work order link and a brief note explaining why you believe it is a test.

2. Operations Support Specialists will post about the test work order in the [https://latchel.slack.com/archives/CL5216FQF](https://latchel.slack.com/archives/CL5216FQF) Slack channel.
  1. They may tag the person who created the test work order for confirmation.

3. In the same post, Operations Support Specialists can ask the creator if the test work order can be canceled.
  1. If the creator confirms, proceed to cancel the work order. (Canceling the work order will also complete all associated tasks.)
    1. If this does not happen, manually cancel all open tasks.

  2. If there is no response for a while:
    1. Check whether the person you tagged is still online in Slack.
      1. **If they are online:** Expect a response from them by the end of the day. Wait until then before proceeding with cancellation. (Support can handle the cancellation if the associate who posted the test work order is already out.)
      2. **If they are offline:** Proceed with instructing the associate to cancel the work order, following step 3.
      3. Comment in the thread that you have cancelled the work order already since no response is received.



4. Clearly note in the work order and in Slack that the cancellation was due to the work order being a test.
  1. This ensures transparency and prevents confusion in future audits or reporting.


**Note:**
Do not cancel suspected test work orders on your own authority. Always escalate for review and confirmation before taking action.


## 🎯 When to Break This SOP

You should only deviate from the process in the SOP in specific, documented scenarios:

- If you receive direct instructions from a Property Manager, owner, or company leadership that override the SOP: Follow their direction and document the exception.
- If the situation is not covered by the SOP or you are unsure how to proceed: Pause and escalate to Operations Support Specialists for guidance—do not proceed on your own.
