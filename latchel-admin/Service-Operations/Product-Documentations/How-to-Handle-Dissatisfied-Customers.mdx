## 🧠 What This Process Is Trying to Solve

<Info>
We want to ensure every dissatisfied customer is heard, respected, and guided toward a resolution. The goal of this SOP is to provide associates with clear steps for responding to complaints, so no customer feels ignored or dismissed. This process prevents escalation, protects Latchel’s reputation, and helps us learn from feedback to improve our service.
</Info>

**Content***

## 📋 Step-by-Step Process

A customer may convey dissatisfaction in many different ways while interacting with the support team. The customers will usually explain that they expected X but Latchel or the team didn’t meet their expectations. See the following examples:

- "I'm not satisfied with the quality of your [product/service]."
- "I expected better communication from your team."
- "The [scheduling/finding vendor/job cycle time] was longer than expected, and it caused inconvenience for me."
- "The price is too high compared to what I'm receiving."
- "You didn't address my concerns."
- "The process is too complicated and confusing."
- "I've had repeated issues with the [product/service/work orders], and it's affecting my experience."
- "The [product/service] doesn't meet my specific needs and requirements."
- "I've received better service from your competitors, and I'm considering switching."
- "The [product/service] didn't live up to the promises made in your [sales/website/marketing] material."

The situations you may encounter are not limited to the ones outlined above, so please be wary of any complaints you receive. Once you have identified that your interaction follows this pattern, proceed as follows:

**Step 1.**Make sure your response follows the quality standards for customer experience and our customer tenets:

- Refer to [Job Aid: Customer Experience Review Calibration Guidelines](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Quality-Program/Job-Aid-Customer-Experience-Review-Calibration-Guidelines)
- Refer to Informational: Latchel Customer Service Tenets - High 5 Your Customers Today!

**Step 2. Apolophrase the customer’s complaint:**

- “I’m sorry [what the customer is complaining about] didn’t meet your expectations.

**Step 3. Provide a brief explanation of how the Process or Product is supposed to work:**

- If the complaint is related to the Work Order life cycle (e.g, intake, troubleshooting, dispatching, etc.) refer to the[Latchel Processes and Product Wiki](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/edit-v2/775553066)for an overview.
- Note: More resources will be added here as we uncover more popular topics.
- If you aren’t sure about what is the correct explanation to provide to the customer:
  - Escalate to your Operations Support Specialists, refer to: [How to: Escalate Between Teams](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/776044545/How+to+Escalate+Between+Teams)

**Step 4**. **Ask the customer where we didn’t meet their expectations.**

- “Could you please tell me where you felt we fell short? I'd like to get this feedback to our team.”

**Step 5**. **Once the customer provides more insight into what went wrong:**

- Escalate to Product Support so that our team can address their concern in detail.
  - Refer to [How to: Escalate to Product Support](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/1485373477/How+to+Escalate+to+Product+Support).
    - Do not forget to use the blurbs on this page to inform the customer that the issue will be escalated.

Any variations of the above are accepted as long as the quality standards for customer experience are met and the structure of the response is maintained:**Apolophrase + Explain + Ask + Escalate**

**For the next example**, let’s say that the following complaint is shared by one of our PMs:

**<u>PM</u>**: *“It appears troubleshooting is not going as far as it should be in WO XX”*

**<u>Agent’s Response:</u>**“Hi, [Customer’s name]. Thank you for reaching out. I’m sorry that Latchel's workflow on this work order didn't meet your expectations. Normal work orders are triaged through a first layer of automated troubleshooting. Emergency work orders (as defined by your intake settings) go through a second layer of troubleshooting with a member of our team. If they are unable to de-escalate, Latchel calls the service providers and/or, in certain cases, the escalation contacts identified in your settings. Could you please tell me where you felt we fell short? I'd like to get this feedback to our team.”

**<u>PM</u>**:*“I thought a human was supposed to troubleshoot all Work Orders, the automated troubleshooting doesn’t seem to be good enough.”*

**<u>Agent’s Response (based on</u>**<u> </u>[How to: Escalate to Product Support](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/1485373477/How+to+Escalate+to+Product+Support))<u>:</u> “I have created a ticket on your behalf with the Customer Success team to investigate the matter further. Your dedicated Customer Success Manager will be reviewing your request and will send you a personal response via email (usually within one business day).”

## 🎯 When to Break This SOP

- If the resident’s request is abusive, illegal, or violates Latchel policies, follow the escalation and reporting protocol instead of the standard dissatisfied customer steps.
- If the customer starts being hostile towards you, refer to [Job Aid: How to Deal with Hostile Customers](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/2557378561/Job+Aid+How+to+Deal+with+Hostile+Customers).
