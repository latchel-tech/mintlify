## 🧠 What This Process Is Trying to Solve

<Note>
  We want to ensure that work order severity is classified accurately and according to each property manager’s preferences. The goal of this SOP is to provide clear guidelines for configuring and applying intake settings, so that emergencies, non-emergencies, and approval-required issues are handled appropriately. This process prevents misclassification, delays, and miscommunication—helping Latchel deliver prompt, tailored service, reduce operational errors, and build trust with both property managers and residents.
</Note>

<Note>
  When changing a work order severity, assess any situation that may indicate severe property damage or 911 situations. Always notify the Property Manager of any issues that indicate a 911 situation/severe damages, whether they are ongoing, contained, part of the current work order you are addressing, or related to a different work order. Ensuring timely notification is critical in all such cases to prioritize safety and proper escalation.
</Note>

## **WHY DOES LATCHEL OFFER THIS?**

Our customers are located throughout the United States, and residents may experience vastly different conditions.

**Example:**

- A PM in Florida (high humidity) may request that broken air conditioning is treated as an emergency if temperatures exceed **85°F**.
- A PM in San Diego (low humidity) may set the threshold at **90°F**.

Latchel associates should always follow the appropriate workflow when creating a work order. The system will automatically guide the work order to the correct **Priority Status**: _Emergency, Normal, or Urgent_.

You can check a PM’s **Custom Emergency Definitions** in their profile under the **Workflow Automation Settings** section.

Refer to the guidance in [Handling Work Order Severity via Harmonia Intake Settings](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPS/pages/edit-v2/3576627319?draftShareId=3cfb8984-5808-40c6-a38e-aaed5ba1c5e9) for more information on how to identify work order severity through the PM’s Intake Settings

- **Intake Settings** include the PM’s custom rules for determining severity, next actions, and fallback settings.

## 🎯 When to Break This SOP

- If the Property Manager requests to change the severity of the work order, we take their word even this conflicts with their intake settings
