## 🧠 What This Process Is Trying to Solve

<Note>
We want to ensure troubleshooters can quickly and accurately determine whether a resident-reported issue is an emergency or non-emergency. The goal of this SOP is to provide clear triggers, example phrases, and guidelines for classifying the urgency of work orders. This process prevents misclassification, delays, and unnecessary escalations—ensuring that emergencies are addressed promptly while non-urgent issues are handled efficiently.
</Note>

**Content***

## 🗂️Guidelines for Identifying Emergency vs. Non-Emergency Triggers

<Note>
**Emergency Triggers:** Focus on health, safety, structural damage, and urgent functional issues that can’t wait.

**Non-Emergency Triggers:** These are typically minor issues, inconveniences, or ongoing problems that can be postponed without impacting the resident’s immediate living conditions.
</Note>

<Note>
While the guidelines below highlight common indicators of emergency, it’s important to remember that each Property Manager may have different intake settings and definitions of what constitutes an emergency. This means that not all cases that seem emergent—such as minor water leaks or slow drains—will automatically be treated as emergencies.

If a resident describes an issue that falls into one of these categories but you're unsure about the urgency level, consult the PM's specific intake settings to confirm the appropriate response. This ensures that all cases are handled in line with the PM's preferences and guidelines. If you’re still unsure, escalate to Operations Support Specialists [**<u>How to: Escalate Between Teams</u>**](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/How-to-Escalate-Between-Teams)**.**
</Note>

### **Triggers to Classify the Issue as an Emergency**

1. **Water and Flooding:**
  1. **Any active or continuous leak**
  2. **Big leaks or flooding:** If water is coming from the ceiling or a pipe burst, causing potential damage or risk.
  3. **Standing water that won't drain:** Water pooling on the floor that can’t be drained could damage floors and walls.

**Sample verbatim and triggers:**

"Water is leaking everywhere!"

"The ceiling is dripping, and it’s getting worse."

"There’s water pooling on the floor and it’s not stopping."

"A pipe burst, and water is flooding the room."

"The walls are getting wet from a leak."

2. **Plumbing and Sewage:**
  1. **Sewage backup or smell:** If sewage is coming up from drains or there’s a strong sewage smell, it’s urgent for health reasons.
  2. **Completely blocked drain: **Water doesn’t drain at all, leading to standing water in sinks or showers.
  3. **No running water:** Especially during cold weather when pipes may freeze, which could prevent access to water.

**Sample verbatim and triggers:**

"The toilet is overflowing and won’t stop."

"There’s sewage backing up into the sink/shower."

"The shower is completely clogged and filling up with water."

"There’s a really bad sewage smell in the bathroom."

"The sink/toilet won’t drain, and water is backing up."

3. **Electrical Problems:**
  1. ⚠️ **Important nuance — Fire Hazard / Power Shutoff distinction:** When a resident reports a major electrical system (HVAC, furnace, electric water heater) as "off," the triggers above still apply BUT classification depends on whether malfunction signs are *currently present* vs. *past-only*, and whether power is confirmed disconnected at the breaker vs. only at the thermostat/dial. A system turned off at the thermostat still has power supplied and can still spark or overheat.🔗 For the full assessment workflow, see the Fire Hazard / Power Shutoff section in [Job Aid: Troubleshooter Tips for Tricky Situations](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Job-Aid-Troubleshooter-Tips-for-Tricky-Situations) and the classification table in [How to: Identify Property Damage and Extreme Habitability Situations](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/How-to-Identify-Property-Damage-and-Extreme-Habitability-Situations).
    - If signs are **currently present** → Fire Hazard / PD criteria are met regardless of shutoff method. Follow PM's intake settings to determine severity; if PM's settings classify as non-emergency, escalate to EEC via PD/EH process.
    - If signs are **past-only** AND breaker is confirmed off → Past-only exception applies; follow PM's intake settings
  2. **Burning smells or sparks:** If there’s a smell of burning wires or electrical sparks, it could be a fire risk.
  3. **Power outage in most or all rooms:** If there’s no electricity in large areas or the entire home, it needs quick attention.
  4. **Exposed wiring:** If wires are visible and could cause electric shock or sparking.

**Sample verbatim and triggers:**

"I see sparks coming from the outlet."

"There’s a burning smell near the fuse box."

"The power went out in most of the apartment."

"I can feel a shock when I touch the light switch."

"The wiring is exposed, and it’s sparking."

4. **Structural Damage:**
  1. **Leaking roof or ceiling issues:** Water coming through the ceiling or roof could be dangerous if the ceiling starts to weaken.
  2. **Cracks in walls or ceilings:** Large cracks that might mean a wall or ceiling could collapse.

**Sample verbatim and triggers:**

"The ceiling looks like it’s going to fall."

"There’s a big crack in the wall that keeps getting bigger."

"The roof is leaking badly."

"The floorboards are coming loose, and it feels unsafe."

"Part of the wall is starting to separate."

5. **Fire and Safety Hazards:**
  1. **Fire or smoke:** If there’s any sign of fire or smoke from appliances, it’s urgent to prevent a larger fire.
  2. **Gas smells:** Smelling gas could mean a leak, which is dangerous and requires immediate attention.
  3. **Broken locks on windows or doors: **If doors or windows can’t be locked, there’s a security risk.

**Sample verbatim and triggers:**

"I smell gas in the kitchen."

"There’s smoke coming from the oven/stove."

"I can’t lock the front door, and it won’t stay closed."

"The fire alarm keeps going off because of the smoke."

"I saw flames or sparks near the appliance."

6. **Health-Related Concerns:**
  1. **No heat in winter: **If the heater isn’t working in very cold weather, it could make the home unsafe.
  2. **No A/C in extreme heat:** Without air conditioning in hot weather, it could become unsafe to stay indoors.

### **Triggers to Classify the Issue as a Non-Emergency**

1. **Minor Maintenance Issues:**
  - **Slow-draining sinks or showers** that don’t present an immediate risk of water damage (e.g., "The shower drain is a bit slow").
  - **Minor leaks** that don’t create flooding or immediate damage (e.g., "Faucet drips when turned off").
  - **Broken appliance** that doesn’t affect daily living (e.g., "The microwave isn’t working, but I have another one").
2. **Non-Urgent Issues:**
  - Problems that don’t affect daily life or can be **postponed** (e.g., "The window doesn’t close all the way, but I can still use and secure it").
  - **Broken lightbulb** or other minor repairs that don’t affect the safety or function of the home (e.g., "One light is out in the living room").
3. **Ongoing Issues That Aren’t Worsening:**
  - **Slight discomfort** that isn’t critical (e.g., "The refrigerator makes a buzzing noise, but it still works fine").
4. **Resident’s Description of the Issue:**
  - If the resident describes the issue as something **manageable** or **non-urgent** (e.g., "I can still use the sink, but it drains slowly" or "The issue isn’t critical, just inconvenient").
  - **Minor inconvenience** with no immediate impact on health or safety (e.g., "The bathroom light keeps flickering, but it still works").
5. **Issues Related to Existing Work Orders:**
  - If the resident is referencing an **existing open issue** and expresses that they can **wait** or that it’s **not urgent** (e.g., "The same issue is still happening from last month, but it’s not too bad").
  - Problems that were already identified and have been deemed **non-urgent** previously (e.g., "The sink is still clogged, but I can live with it").
6. **No Immediate Impact on Health or Safety:**
  - **Inconveniences that don’t affect safety** or basic functionality (e.g., "A door handle is loose, but I can still use it").
  - **Minor cosmetic damage** (e.g., "The paint is peeling, but it’s not affecting anything").
  - **Broken or missing furniture** that doesn’t impact living conditions (e.g., "The dining chair leg is loose, but it’s still usable").
7. **Resident’s Reassurance of Non-Emergency:**
  - When the resident explicitly mentions that the issue is **not an emergency** (e.g., "It’s not urgent, it can wait").
  - The resident may also **give time flexibility** (e.g., "It’s fine to fix this next week" or "I’m not in a rush").
