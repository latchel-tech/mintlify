## 🧠 What This Process Is Trying to Solve

<Note>
We want to prevent miscommunication, delays, and inconsistent support quality when handling customer requests via SMS and webchat. The goal is to provide clear, actionable guidelines for associates on what to do—and what to avoid—so every customer receives a prompt, accurate, and professional response.
</Note>

**🎓Training Content**

<Note>
The contents of this page have been included in the **SMS & Webchat Cross-Training **Coassemble course.
</Note>

Operations Associates working on SMS/Webchat have 3 goals as a mission:

1. First time reply within 1 min, 90% of the time
2. Provide a great experience
3. Solve the customer issue / answer their questions

In order to accomplish that mission, there are a few things we would like the team to do and avoid while handling the SMS/Webchat queue and customer interaction:

# 1
</Column>
<Column>
# Do

<Tip>
Open the 1st chat at the top of the queue (the oldest one).
</Tip>
</Column>
<Column>
# Don’t

<Warning>
Search for chats that you feel more comfortable handling.
</Warning>
</Column>
<Column>
<Note>
**Why**

1. Grabbing the oldest chat will ensure that we are reducing our response time and meeting our goal of replying within 1 minute.
2. Challenging yourself to handle chats that you may not be comfortable with at first is a way to learn new things and grow in your role, and later within the company. To become a leader, you will need to be proficient in handling hard situations.
</Note>

# 2
</Column>
<Column>
# Do

<Tip>
Assign the chat to yourself as soon as you open the chat window.
</Tip>
</Column>
<Column>
# Don’t

<Warning>
Spend time reading and researching about the customer question before assigning the chat to yourself.
</Warning>
</Column>
<Column>
<Note>
**Why**

1. By assigning the chat to yourself right away, you are committing to it and removing that chat from the queue. This prevents other associates from reading and researching the same situation and wasting time (yours or your peer’s).
2. In order to achieve our goal of replying to customers within 5 min, we can’t afford wasting time. We need to have a productive team, so let’s eliminate ambiguous situations that could cause overlapping efforts.
</Note>

# 3
</Column>
<Column>
## Do

<Tip>
Always look at the top of the work order to see if someone else is also looking at it. If so, and the customer request will take you more than 10 min, reach out to the other people in that work order and verify you are not working on the same thing.
</Tip>
</Column>
<Column>
# Don’t

<Warning>
Assume you are the only one taking action and proceed without communicating with the team.
</Warning>
</Column>
<Column>
<Note>
**Why**

1. If 2 associates are looking at the same work order, it could be that one is working on a task related to the same topic you are handling in the SMS, and you should avoid overlapping efforts (only one should proceed).
2. Or, it could be that one associate is researching something and not taking any action in the WO. If you jump off without checking, that request may not be addressed by anyone.
3. It is always good to clarify.
</Note>

# 

# 4
</Column>
<Column>
# Do

<Tip>
Minimize the conversation while you are researching or working on the issue.
</Tip>
</Column>
<Column>
# Don’t

<Warning>
End the conversation before resolving the customer’s issues (unless they are unresponsive – see #5)
</Warning>
</Column>
<Column>
<Note>
**Why**

1. By minimizing the conversation, you are keeping that conversation assigned to you, so no other associate will be prompted to work on the same thing you are already working on. This also saves your time, so you don’t have to go find that conversation again to enter other updates.
2. This also gives some time for the customer to reply to any question you had and avoid two issues: (1) the customer going back to the end of the line and having to wait longer for being helped again.
(2) another associate having to start the same conversation from scratch, which will take more time than you will take (customer will need to wait longer), as you already know what is going on.
</Note>

<Note>
Only “end the conversation” with the customer when you have completed ALL of the work related to that conversation, including entering your notes.
</Note>

<Note>
If you are waiting on a response to a Slack escalation in order to resolve the customer's issue and it takes more than 30 seconds, you may end the conversation for now by telling the customer, *“I am working on getting an answer to your question and we will get back to you as soon as possible. Please don’t hesitate to reach out in the meantime if there’s anything else I can assist you with!”*
</Note>

# 5
</Column>
<Column>
# Do

<Tip>
Wait at least 3 minutes after sending a clarifying question or reply to a customer before ending the conversation.
</Tip>
</Column>
<Column>
# Don’t

<Warning>
End a conversation if the customer is typing a reply.
</Warning>
</Column>
<Column>
<Note>
The more you can resolve the customer issue and answer their questions within one conversation, the better will be the customer experience, as they are not pushed back to the “end of the line” and don’t have to wait for another associate to catch-up on what is going on.

You can use those 3 minutes to make notes in the work order and grab other available conversations. If the customer has not responded after 3 minutes, ***send another message to wrap up the conversation per our Customer Experience standards before closing the chat.*** See [Job-Aid: Guide to write SMS/Webchat or email to customers](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Job-Aid-Guide-to-write-SMS/Webchat-or-email-to-customers) for suggested language.
</Note>

# 6
</Column>
<Column>
# Do

<Tip>
Follow our [Customer Service Tenets](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Informational-Latchel-Customer-Service-Tenets---High-5-Your-Customers-Today) and the [Customer Experience Review guidelines](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/Quality-Program/Job-Aid-Customer-Experience-Review-Calibration-Guidelines).
</Tip>
</Column>
<Column>
## Don’t

<Warning>
Take shortcuts to finish a soon as possible, sacrificing customer experience.
</Warning>
</Column>
<Column>
<Note>
Remember to show Customer Obsession at all times!
</Note>

# 7
</Column>
<Column>
# Do

<Tip>
Solve the customer’s issue or answer their question DURING the conversation with them.
</Tip>
</Column>
<Column>
# Don’t

<Warning>
Avoid creating tasks for other team members. If you need to create a task, follow [SOP: Work Order Follow-ups and Updates](https://help.latchel.com/latchel-admin/Service-Operations/SOP-Work-Order-Follow-ups-and-Updates).
</Warning>
</Column>
<Column>
<Note>
If there is a task for the same request the customer is asking about in your chat, check when that task was created. ***If that is older than 2 days, just take care of that task and resolve it.***
</Note>

<Note>
1. The customer is taking the energy and effort to reach out to us because they believe something needs to be done right away, or has been taking too long. So let’s take care of that right away!
2. By creating tasks, we are just pushing that resolution to the bottom of the task queue, which could take a few days, which is not a good customer experience
3. If you think the issue will take **more than 10 min to resolve**, escalate to Operations Support Specialists for guidance for a faster resolution, or get support to resolve it quicker. It is also good to let Operations Support Specialists know you are on a longer chat session than usual.
</Note>

# 8
</Column>
<Column>
# Do

<Tip>
Use appropriate communication methods when we need to engage PMs as a result of requests received from other parties.
</Tip>
</Column>
<Column>
# Don’t

<Warning>
Call or contact the PM unnecessarily.
</Warning>
</Column>
<Column>
<Note>
**How to communicate with a Property Manager:**

**<u>Non-Emergency Work Orders:</u>**

1. For situations when we need PMs to do something or provide info to us so we can take action (e.g. approvals, clarifications, etc.), Ops should ***create a task for the PM***.
2. For situations that we don't need a response from them, we ***send the PM an email from Assembly to the PM user group for that property***. Some examples: Resident asking to talk to the PM, Resident complains about something unrelated to maintenance that we think it would be appropriate to let them know as a courtesy, Resident following up on a WO that was forwarded to the PM, etc.
a) If we can map the request to a property, email the PM user group (property level).
b) If we cannot map the request to a property, email the primary user on the PM’s account.

**<u>Emergency WOs:</u>**

Attempt to call the PM (if meeting the calling guidelines, e.g. appropriate hours and reasons). If the PM doesn’t answer, leave a voicemail and send an SMS.
</Note>

# 9
</Column>
<Column>
# Do

<Tip>
The initial message or response should include essential details for verification and identification, such as the WO number, property address (if applicable), and a brief summary.
</Tip>
</Column>
<Column>
# Don’t

<Warning>
Sending a message without specifying the related issue or work order for reference.
</Warning>
</Column>
<Column>
<Note>
Including the WO number, property address (if applicable), and a brief summary of the issue in customer communications helps streamline the support process, reduces errors, and ensures that each customer's needs are met effectively and efficiently.
</Note>

### For Spanish SMS/chats received by non-Spanish-speaking associates:
</Column>
<Column>
# Do

<Tip>
Respond with this blurb: 
*'**Un momento por favor mientras buscamos un agente de habla hispana para poder asistirle.**'*
Translation: '*One moment while we look for an available Spanish-speaking associate to assist you.*' 

Then, tag an available Spanish-speaking teammate or mention Operations Support Specialists so they can assist you in assigning the SMS.
</Tip>
</Column>
<Column>
## Don’t

<Warning>
Leave the SMS/chat unanswered while looking for an available Spanish-speaking teammate or seeking assistance from Operations Support Specialists.
</Warning>
</Column>
<Column>
<Note>
The purpose of the initial response is to minimize the wait time for customers seeking assistance and to avoid breaching our SMS/chat SLA by leaving the message unanswered for an extended period.
</Note>

## **🎯 When to Break This SOP**

**Do not break or deviate from this SOP on your own authority.** Always escalate to an Ops Support specialist or follow explicit instructions if you believe an exception is necessary.
