## Purpose

<Info>
**Purpose of this document**
This resource will provide guidance on how to deliver an excellent customer experience when interacting with our customers via any contact channel (phone, chat, email, SMS). It will provide 5 tenets or guidelines that should be applied to each customer interaction, as well as practical examples on how to apply those guidelines.  These guidelines are generic enough that can and should be applied to all customer types, including Property Managers, Residents, and Service Providers.

#### **Process Overview**
“A *tenet* is a principle or belief honored by a person or, more often, a group of people.”  It provides generic guidelines for decision making and expectations on how to behave, so the whole team can provide a consistent experience to our customers.
At Lachel, our Customer Service Tenets are summarized into 5 tenets:
1. Treat Every Customer as a Friend
2. Reduce Customer Effort
3. Answer the Question Asked
4. Solve the Problem
5. Escalate Problems
These are the **5 things you need to keep in mind EVERY TIME you are interacting with the customer** via any method: phone, email, SMS, webchat, leaving notes in the work order, etc.
So now, High 5 your customers today!! 🙂

#### **Tenet 2 - Reduce Customer Effort**
***We ensure that the customer experience is easy, simple and time effective***
In the [CEO letter about Customer Obsession](/A-Letter-from-the-CEO-Customer-Obsession), Ethan mentions that *“Latchel will become the most Caring, Convenient, Transparent, and Affordable maintenance coordination company to ever exist*.”
This customer service tenet is very related to “Convenient” and “Transparent”
- **Convenient**means being able to proceed with something with little effort or difficulty.  Our goal is to make it super easy for customers to interact with us, our tools and resources.  We will look for ways we can save our customers' time and energy by making it easy for them to understand something or take an action.
- **Transparent**- *“We communicate often. We communicate with transparency, honesty, and guidance” - “We make sure all of our customers know what is happening and why it is happening” - Ethan.* Our goal is to keep the customer informed and confident on what is happening and what we will do next.

***We carefully evaluate each customer question so we can answer the rightquestions***

|  | **How to “Answer the Question Asked”** | **Why this is helpful?** | **Tips / Examples** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **LISTEN to the customer** | By carefully and attentively listening to the customer, you will be able to make a list of all of the questions and requests that customer has for you to take care of. Take notes of issues as they talk. Find the actionable portion of the customer's question. Try to see through the fog of uncertainty. Second*,*[paraphrase] and wait for confirmation*.” If the customer sent email or SMS/Webchat, start your reply with introducing yourself and paraphrasing. #### **Tenet**4 - Solve the Problem
***We strive to fulfill the needs of our customers and resolve their concerns***
We are not committing to fully resolve all of their problems because some of them may be outside of our control. We are also not committing to do exactly what the customer wants us to do or how they want the issue to be resolved. For example, a tenant wants a certain item to be replaced and the property manager, or the owner, doesn’t approve that replacement. Another example is when we scheduled the right type of service provider to do the job, but that service provider did a poor job and the issue was not resolved.
Our goal is to:
1. Answer all questions / address all concerns, including other related or account issues
2. Avoid creating other issues as you are addressing the initial ones
3. Get confirmation the customer understood the resolution
4. Do our best to make them leave that interaction feeling good about the resolution, or at least feeling that it was fair

| 1 | **Confirm the problem you are about to solve** | If you are not clear on what problem the customer wants you to solve, you most likely will solve the wrong problem and potentially create a new one; while the actual problem will remain unsolved. | 3 | **Solve the issue quickly, but QUALITY comes FIRST** | We appreciate our customers' time and should do our best to use that time wisely. We should be fast in resolving the customer issue; however, we should NEVER let speed get in the way of providing quality | **If the issue can’t be resolve immediately, let the customer know what the next steps are and offer to follow-up.**You can say:*“I need to do a deeper research to resolve this, would it be ok if I call you back within X hours/ by tomorrow?“*If you already have an idea on what needs to be done, share with the customer the next steps you will take. #### **Tenet**5 - Escalate Problems
***We seek help and escalate potential or actual problems as they happen***
**Escalation**means "an increase in the intensity or seriousness of something; an intensification". In operations, this usually means the issue is **getting out of your control** **AND the issue needs to receive immediate attention**,
This could happen in different scenarios:
1. **You can't resolve the issue** - you don’t know what to do and have already done your research in our library, OR you haven’t been trained or don’t have access to tools needed to solve the issue, ORthe issue needs management approval, OR you concluded that all tools you have were already attempted and the work order is “moving in circles or not progressing”, therefore someone needs to step in immediately to finally resolve the issue
2. **The customer is requesting to talk to a supervisor**- the customer is very upset and is not willing to work with you to resolve the issue
3. **You notice there is a trend or something doesn’t seem right -**You start seeing the same issue happening over and over with the same customer or with different customers, ORyou think it is odd this issue happened, based on your understanding of our tech and processes. This is a sign that something else could be wrong in our processes or system and needs immediate attention to be resolved before it gets worse. To put it simply, when your see a potential problem, please do something about it 🙂
Sometimes you may need to reach out to the shift Operations Support Specialists or leadership **to ask for help or confirmation**. Although this is not quite an escalation, you should definitely take actions feeling confident that you are doing the right thing to solve the customer issue.
This SOP explains our process supporting this tenet: [UNDER CONSTRUCTION (PREMIUM): When and How to Ask for Help and Escalate](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OPPM/pages/781747140/UNDER+CONSTRUCTION+PREMIUM+When+and+How+to+Ask+for+Help+and+Escalate)  – note, this SOP needs revision, so use the current designated channel for problem solving and Operations Support Specialists
