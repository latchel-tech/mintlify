### Customer Obsession at Latchel

Team, I’m so proud to be working with each of you. You give me confidence that Latchel will become the most Caring, Convenient, Transparent, and Affordable maintenance coordination company to ever exist. Because Customer Obsession is our most cherished Leadership Principle, I thought it would be helpful to give you my own interpretation of Customer Obsession. So, let's get on the same page on what that means and how to apply Customer Obsession to our diverse customer base.

#### Customer Obsession

We serve 4 different types of customers at Latchel.

1. Property Managers
2. Property Owners
3. Service Pros (aka Vendors)
4. Residents 

Our 4 different customers often have competing interests. Take the case of scheduling a regular maintenance request like a garbage disposal replacement. Residents want service done as soon as possible on their own schedule. Service Pros can only do a job if they’re available. Property Managers and Owners want the job done when and with whom it will be most affordable. In this case, a Resident’s interests may seem opposite of the Property Manager and Owner. The Resident wants it done now, the Property Manager and Owner want it done when an affordable Service Pro is available. This is just one example of the many times that our customers may have competing interests. 

How do we grapple with these competing interests of our customers? First, it is important to know that there are a few basics for Customer Obsession that apply to EVERY customer interaction no matter who you talk to:

#### Approach Customers with Empathy And Respect

**We ALWAYS approach a customer with empathy and respect.** My high school drama teacher always said that everybody carries struggles. Theater (whether a movie or a play) helps us escape from all the BS that we have to deal with day to day. Mental, emotional, financial, physical, or family related, there’s always something people are struggling with. Throw in the pain of dealing with a home maintenance problem and the tiniest of issues can feel like a big deal. Not just for residents! It can feel like a big deal for Service Pros (aka Vendors), Property Managers, and Owners too. Each of them are dealing with their own struggles.

- Think of the Owner that just took out a \$15,000 loan to pay for their kid’s first year of college. They suddenly discover from their Property Manager that they may need to spend \$8,000 to replace an HVAC system. How do you think they feel?
- Think of the Property Manager struggling to build a profitable business. They find out one of their biggest Owners isn’t happy with their portfolio’s performance. Any maintenance issue that pops up for that portfolio will feel like the most important thing in the world.
- Think of the Service Pro hustling to find enough work to take their family on a vacation. They find out that one of their projects goes over-budget and that the new budget was turned down by the Property Manager. Imagine how they’ll feel if that news isn’t delivered well. 

The Resident is most acutely impacted by a maintenance issue. It doesn’t take much effort to understand how a Resident must feel when their AC goes out in 90F weather. We may have to tell them that their issue is not an emergency, but we’ll make every effort to get a Service Pro scheduled during business hours. They head to bed sweaty, a bit uncomfortable, unlikely to have the sweetest dreams. They still have to go to work the next morning and perform at work despite a sweaty night. 

We never know what someone on the other side of the phone is dealing with, so we ALWAYS approach our customers with empathy and respect. We understand that a maintenance problem isn’t just a maintenance problem - it affects the lives of all of our customers. 

There are many ways that we can show empathy and respect. Start by imagining our customers as if they’re friends. Saying things like, “Hello!”, “Thank you,” and “Please.” It means apologizing for what our customer is going through in the case of a maintenance issue. It means apologizing when they’re having issues due to a mistake that we made. It means thanking customers for their patience as we investigate their issues. It means taking the time to discuss all the issues that a customer is dealing with because their time is valuable and we need to respect that. We build relationships with our customers because being transactional is not customer obsessed.

#### Communicate Transparency, Honesty, and Guidance - Do it Often

**We communicate often. We communicate with transparency, honesty, and guidance.** Our customers don’t always understand the world of rental maintenance. This includes Property Managers and Property Owners. Many Property Managers choose to use Latchel _because_ they don’t understand maintenance. Being transparent, honest, and providing guidance is far more important than doing exactly what a customer says they want. What a customer says they want isn’t always aligned with their own best interest. Sometimes they don’t have enough experience and don’t know a better way of solving an issue. That’s why Latchel exists. We guide customers to the best outcome. 

We can’t and won’t always deliver what our customers want. More often than not, what a customer wants is IMPOSSIBLE to deliver. This is the real world and we need to work under the limitations of the real world. Customer Obsession isn’t about getting everyone what they want. Customer Obsession is about understanding the real world, guiding our customers, and fulfilling the needs of our customers to the best of our ability. We help our customers understand the situation and make them our allies in helping us get stuff done. We make sure all of our customers know what is happening and why it is happening. One of the best ways to do this is to present options to a customer and let them choose. These creates ownership in our process and buy-in on resolutions. 

For example, you could say, “Attached is the estimate from the plumber. The estimate is very expensive because they want to replace the bathtub and can’t do a patch fix. We may be able to find a different plumber that can patch it for cheaper. We can (A) go with this estimate and do a more expensive, permanent fix; or (B) we can take another couple days to get other plumbers out that may be able to do a patch fix. I’m not sure if we’ll be able to find plumbers for a patch, but we can put in the effort. Which option sounds best to you?” 

Another example - a Resident wants a job done NOW. “I’m free tonight after work, send someone tonight!” You already know that this Resident doesn’t understand the way the real world works. Unfortunately, there won’t be a Service Pro available tonight. We can’t make the impossible happen. What the Resident, Property Manager, and Owner all need is the job to be done within a reasonably appropriate time. What our customers need is for us to tell them what is reasonable. They need our guidance. They also need us to be communicative to move the job forward. 

When our customers misunderstand our purpose, it is Customer Obsessed to explain it to them. “I understand how frustrating it is to sweat through your clothing in your house. I’m sorry your AC does not work. I’m going to help get someone scheduled for you. We work with a trusted group of Service Pros and we’ll do our best to work around their availability and yours. They work Monday to Friday, 9-5pm. Can you be available this week during business hours?” 

Property Managers won’t always get what they want either. We won’t find unlicensed and uninsured Service Pros for them. That means we won’t be able to get them the best priced handyman. This is too risky for us and puts them and their Owners at risk too. However, what that Property Manager NEEDS is to get an affordable and fair price on behalf of the Owner. (The Owner also deserves a fair price, otherwise they may actually LOSE money on their rental property.) 

Being Customer Obsessed means making sure that all parties know what is happening with a work order, especially when the work order isn’t going as planned. Approach every interaction with transparency, honesty, and an effort to provide guidance.

#### Owning your mistakes

**We own up to our mistakes.** We won’t always do the right thing or the best thing, so we own up when we make mistakes and we say, “I’m sorry.” We make the best effort to fix mistakes and hold ourselves accountable for them. Understand that a Property Manager might be angry because we put their business at risk. 

In order to own up to a mistake, you must first know what went wrong. We take responsibility for finding out what went wrong and working with internal team members to deep dive and fix both the issue at hand and the root cause. We are transparent with customers when things go wrong and we tell them exactly what we’ll do to fix it. 

We work with the customer success team or management to escalate processes that are too ambiguous or require judgement calls - especially if they are not documented or unclear. Are there opportunities to clarify our standard work? Can the software guide us in a better direction or prevent mistakes that we typically make? 

In some cases, we reimburse customers when justified. This is how we hold ourselves accountable. If we repair an appliance, but our customer had clear instructions in the system NOT to dispatch for appliance repair, then we owe it to that customer to reimburse them for the work. 

Remember, we’re talking about the livelihood of our Property Manager’s business and their Owner’s financial livelihood. It would be a disgrace for us to ignore the financial harm we did to our customer by not following directions. 

Ultimately, our duty is to resolve maintenance issues. We must resolve maintenance issues quickly and with quality. Put this goal in combination with our approach to every type of customer:

- **We always approach a customer with empathy and respect.**
- **We communicate often. We communicate with transparency, honesty, and guidance.**
- **We own up to our mistakes.** 

I think it is important to remember, we don’t have to make every customer happy in order to be Customer Obsessed. The important part is that our customers feel heard. That they feel we did the best job possible. That they feel we are experts in maintenance. 

Despite doing our best, we won’t always have happy customers. Maintenance creates pain for people. Sometimes, someone may not be happy at the end of the day. That said, you should know that you did your best because our customers should ALWAYS be able to say:

- “Latchel kept me in the loop every step of the way.”
- “Good thing the amazing folks at Latchel explained everything to me.”
- “Thankfully the Latchel team dealt with most of the BS on that one.”
- “Luckily Latchel saved me a bunch of time coordinating that, even if I had to get a mouthful from \_\_\_\_\_\_.”
- “I sweated through all my clothes without my AC, but Latchel was there to help me when I needed them.”
- “Latchel is totally stalker status. They practically message me everytime my plumber sneezes… I love them!” 

Each type of customer has specific needs and interests. That means there are specific things we need to do for each of them to be Customer Obsessed.

#### Property Managers

Being Customer Obsessed for our Property Managers often means thinking, “What would the Property Owner do?” Keep in mind, the Property Manager always acts with the Property Owner’s interests in mind. This means we need to:

1. **Only spend their Owner’s hard earned money on maintenance when necessary.** The Property Manager has a fiduciary duty to protect their Owner’s financial livelihood. That means being frugal with money. Spending carelessly on maintenance will quickly lose an Owner and harm a Property Manager’s business. Being Customer Obsessed means protecting an Owner’s money by:
   1. **Troubleshooting simple maintenance requests before scheduling vendors.** Look at the description on a work order. Has anyone tried to troubleshoot yet? Do we even have an understanding of what the real issue is? Residents won’t troubleshoot issues without prompting. You are the first line of defense to protecting the Owner’s financial livelihood. _(The awesome thing with troubleshooting, it is the best way to save the Ops team time if you can effectively troubleshoot a work order so that it never even goes through the scheduling/completion/invoicing process.)_
   2. **Only schedule work if Property Managers are responsible for it.** The Resident is responsible for many issues (e.g. paint patching, fixing blinds, replacing light bulbs, removing pests). If the Resident requests something that isn’t part of the property manager’s responsibility then we must see if the Property Manager actually wants this work done.
   3. **Sending the appropriate Service Pro.** Any Property Manager and Owner makes sure that they’re getting the right Service Pro to the job. Otherwise, they’ll end up wasting cash on an unnecessary visit from a Service Pro that can’t even fix the issue. Do you know enough about the maintenance request to send the right Service Pro? Call the Resident to deep dive the issue. Now you know enough. Next step, are you sure the Service Pro works those types of issues? If you aren’t positive, then call the Service Pro to confirm. You’re being Customer Obsessed to all of our customers by doing this.
2. **Get them quality work at a fair price.** If we’re sourcing a Latchel Network Service Provider, we need to make sure we’re getting Property Manager’s quality work at a fair price. Nothing stings more than getting ripped off. Even worse if you feel like you got ripped off because of Latchel.
3. **Get them the right Service Pros**. Not all Service Pros will be a good fit to work with us even if the PM would like them to be. Owners and Residents want work coordinated efficiently and timely. If Service Pros aren’t able to do that then we need to tell Property Managers about it. A Service Pro that ignores requests is the biggest risk to a Property Manager’s business. We escalate these types of issues to the customer success team so that they can work with the Property Manager on finding new Service Pros.

#### Service Pros (aka Vendors)

Being Customer Obsessed for our Service Pros is very similar to Property Managers. Service Pros are business owners too. They’re in it to make a living and keep their families financially healthy. Service Pros can be a little more gruff than the business owner sitting behind a desk, but they have the same needs. Remember, for any business owner, time is the most valuable resource:

1. **Provide clarity on the work that needs to be done.** Being clear with Service Pros on what we need them to do is the most important thing they need from us.
   1. **Only send work orders when it is clear what needs to be done.** Sending an unclear work order without scope wastes their time. Service Pros need to be able to read a work order and understand the work that needs to be done. Otherwise, they’ll be in a back and forth with you, wasting their time and yours to figure out what has to be done.
   2. **Only schedule them on jobs that they’re qualified for.** Sending a Service Pro a job or an estimate that they’re not qualified wastes their time and does not provide clarity for them. Not only will they charge a PM for the visit, but they’ll still be bitter that we wasted their talents on a job they couldn’t do. Make sure you know the Service Pro is qualified for the job before sending them the work order.
   3. **Take care of multiple work orders with a single call.** Getting consistently called throughout the day as issues pop up sucks for Service Pros. They’re out in the field trying to get stuff done. Taking care of everything with a single call makes it clear what has to be done and doesn’t require the Service Pro to keep everything organized in their head.
2. **Show gratitude and appreciation.** Service Pros rarely get the gratitude and appreciation they deserve. Remember, Service Pros make our business possible. If every Property Manager used their own in-house labor, Latchel wouldn’t exist. We exist because our Property Managers depend on our Service Pros. Thank them for every job and make sure they know how much we appreciate them and want to grow business with them.
3. **Be clear on pricing expectations.** Service Pros hate haggling and negotiation. They’d rather be told up front what the price expectations are. Ask Service Pros for special pricing before we ever engage in a relationship. We have thousands of units that we manage. We can get them consistent work.
4. **Help Service Pros understand our workflow.** Most Service Pros don’t use technology to run their business. They need help understanding how this stuff works. Imagine receiving a text message about scheduling and never talking to anybody about this new system called Latchel! This can be intimidating for Service Pros. Help them. Call new Service Pros to introduce Latchel. Tell them we like to work via text. Tell them how easy our system is - they don’t even need to login anywhere! Understand them when they have difficulty using the system. Some people don’t learn as quickly and need a little bit of extra help.

#### Residents

Being Customer Obsessed with Residents mostly means doing the 3 core principles discussed earlier. There are 2 things that I’d add to the list for Residents:

1. **Be proactive with Residents.** Residents receive the least amount of automated communication from our system. Be proactive messaging Residents to keep them updated. Similarly, be proactive in reaching out to other Residents on the property to get help with scheduling and things like that.
2. **Assist with scheduling when Residents have limited availability.** Some Residents will literally only be available evenings and weekends. We need to make our best effort to accommodate for this before we say it can’t be done. For issues like this, you should be calling out to Service Pros to get a verbal confirmation from them for evening or weekend dates. Then you can immediately book the appointment for them. If you’re not able to get verbal confirmation, then you need to let the Resident know that none of our trusted Service Pros is available evenings or weekends and we have to schedule during the day. We can get permission to enter and access instructions in order to do this.

#### A list of Do NOTS!

I think it is helpful to focus on things that at first appear Customer Obsessed for Residents, but may actually make things worse for them! Yes, you read that right. It is counter-intuitive, but some things feel customer obsessed for Residents, yet they actually have the opposite effect. Here are a few of the most important examples:

1. **Telling a Resident that you’ll get on this ASAP/immediately/right now.** Residents don’t know that we’re not the actual Service Pro. When you say, “I’ll take care of this right now,” they’ll often think that means you’re going to send a technician immediately, right now. Instead, tell them the transparent, honest, guidance they need: “We will send someone out during business hours. First, I need to first get your availability or get permission to enter the home. Then I’m going to reach out to our trusted Service Pros with your availability. They’ll pick the earliest possible time. As soon as we confirm a time, you’ll get a text from us confirming the appointment.” We must set the right expectations with Residents and explain how the maintenance process works. Educating customers is part of being Customer Obsessed.
2. **Rescheduling jobs when we have permission to enter and access instructions.** If we have permission to enter and a Resident attempts to reschedule a job, it is best to respond to their request by saying, “I’m so sorry, but we aren’t able to reschedule this. We received permission to enter or gave ample notice to enter. This is an issue that we must attend to in order to keep the property in excellent condition. I understand this is inconvenient. You may contact your property manager directly to discuss.”
3. **Promising something out of your hands.** Never make promises you can’t keep. Be honest with our limitations. If they complain then so be it. It is better for Latchel and our customers to be honest than to promise something out of our hands. For the most part, we can’t even promise that we’ll send the right type of Service Pro. Who knows what they’ll find when they get out there. Instead, be brutally honest that this is the real world and we never know what types of issues we find when on site trying to fix something. Who knows if a Service Pro discovers an electrical issue that requires a special part to be made?! The competing interests of our customers don’t have to deter us from being Customer Obsessed. Remember that everyone deals with baggage and maintenance problems can be the straw breaking the camel's back. We’re on the right track if every customer interaction abides by the below rules:

- **We always approach a customer with empathy and respect.**
- **We communicate often. We communicate with transparency, honesty, and guidance.**
- **We own up to our mistakes.** 

Doing the Customer Obsessed thing won’t always feel Customer Obsessed. Telling someone, “No,” can feel like a disappointment, but to be an advocate for our customers, we _have_ to say “No” sometimes. When you’re unsure of the Customer Obsessed thing to do, follow this step by step as a shorthand for being Customer Obsessed in ambiguous situations:

1. Listen to the customer and empathize with them.
2. Ask yourself, “What would a responsible Property Owner do? What is best for their financial livelihood?”
3. Take action and communicate often. 

First and foremost our job is to protect the resident's safety, then the rental property, and then the financial wellbeing of the property owner. Beyond these, we work to get the best outcome for everyone involved. As long as we do that, everyone will see us as Customer Obsessed. 

Let’s build the most Customer Obsessed company that the real estate industry has ever seen!
