## A Letter from Customer Success
Key Guidelines to Handling Latchel Network & Latchel Affiliate Service Provider Relationships

### **Key Guideline 1: Trust with Verification**
“The best way to find out if you can trust somebody is to trust them.” – Ernest Hemingway
At Latchel, one of our core values is [Earn Trust](/Company-Wide-Information). Earning trust is a two-way street, given Latchel’s needs and the Service Provider’s needs. When we send a service provider to an Emergency Work Order at 2 o’clock in the morning, Latchel is putting an incredible amount of trust in the Service Provider’s ability to resolve the issue. They are expected to be courteous, professional, and have reasonable rates. Unfortunately, it is difficult to assure that these traits are something you’ll be able to ascertain or judge from the Service Provider through a 5-minute conversation or browsing through Google reviews.
For those interested in being an **official**part of the Latchel Network, there are **some** items that our Operations team will confirm:
1. Verify that the Service Provider has a smartphone
2. Verify that the Service Provider has an e-mail address
3. Verify that the Service Provider has a bank account
4. Verify that the Service Provider has any necessary licenses
5. Verify that the Service Provider has Certificate of Insurance (COI)
6. Verify that the Service Provider can take photos to prove their work completion.
After verifying the above list of items, Latchel is comfortable knowing they are at minimum qualified to receive requests. This process does not always apply to Affiliates due to the urgency required to dispatch these service providers. When there is an active leak we can’t play 20 questions with the service provider. We trust immediately, then verify with resident satisfaction!
As the Service Provider completes several Work Order assignments for Latchel, the Service Provider gradually earns a mutual trust with our company as well. Although Latchel cannot 100% guarantee the manner in which the Service Provider will represent both us as a company and our Property Management customers, Latchel must trust the assumption that newly sourced Service Providers will do their best to attempt to complete each work order we send their way. Latchel may not have the resources to inspect the quality of completed work orders in-person, but we trust the service provider to take photos and prove their work. We look to other means of [feedback from the Resident](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/LTG/pages/776603595/PREMIUM+Job+Aid+Completed+Work+Order+-+Tenant+Left+a+Review) for evidence on the performance of the Service Provider.

### **Key Guideline 2: Gratitude is the Best Attitude**
“No one who achieves success does so without the help of others. The wise and confident acknowledge this help with gratitude.” – Alfred North Whitehead
A prevalent issue encountered by Property Managers is being confronted with a poor Service Provider experience where the Service Provider may increase their rates to unreasonable levels after establishing themselves over time. This isn’t a result of a service provider making a conscious effort to push their customers away, but it’s that they find less and less value in the mutual relationship over time. Eventually, if you find no value in a relationship you just stop caring about doing a good job, or you have to find value elsewhere, such as raising prices to justify what you’re doing.
On the flip side, Service Providers who stay around for years and years are Service Providers who have built an outstanding trust relationship with their customers. This is where Latchel’s quality of care for [Customer Obsession](/Company-Wide-Information) comes in. [Services offered by Latchel](/Informational-Latchel-Customers-Customer-Subscriptions) act as the glue between the Property Manager and Service Provider relationship.
The most effective way we can exemplify this guideline is as simple as telling them! The next conversation you have with a service provider, use their name, simply compliment them on something they did. Review the Work Order for things you can easily compliment such as
- How quickly they scheduled the job
- The rates are incredibly reasonable
- How quickly they completed the job
- Great before and after photos
- We got a good review from the resident

<Tip>
**Latchel Blurb** - “*Thanks Carlos, I really like the estimate, it was super clear. I know our customers appreciate that and so do I.*”
**Latchel Blurb -**“*Jim, the resident will really appreciate having a work date scheduled and so do I*”.
</Tip>
When they perform to our standards, the Service Provider is making Operations run more smoothly by working in tandem with us; don’t be shy to let these customers know that they are valued as pillars of the Latchel community. These small positive interactions will help building long lasting relationships where the service provider will bend over backwards to keep us happy because when the time calls for it we’ll do the same for them.
“Gratitude makes sense of our past, brings peace for today, and creates a vision for tomorrow.” – Melody Beattie

### **Key Guideline 3: Solution-Focused Point of View**
*"You can have the most beautifully designed experiment with the most carefully controlled variables, and the animal will do what it damn well pleases.” - Barbara Ehrenreich*
Many Service Providers are looking for ways to make as much money with as minimal effort as possible; we must accept this possible outcome, and **prepare from a solution-focused point of view**.  These types of interactions can still lead to incredibly positive results.
A great example is how photos of work completed could be seen as a “nice to have” instead of a necessity. *Not*providing photos puts us in a direction where invoices can be disputed and Latchel is left with poor insight into a resolution. *Every single invoice submitted without photos has a risk that the work wasn’t even done in the first place!*Although this outcome is certainly not preferable , we must acknowledge and prepare for the fact that it could happen. It would be assumed that Latchel would not have that issue because Latchel’s platform absolutely requires photos to be submitted, correct? Well, not exactly. The issue Latchel prepares for is that a Service Provider could submit incorrect photos, or fake photos, and unfortunately there is no super artificial intelligence being used currently that can verify the authenticity of photos. We are soon to be dealing with solutions to verify the invoices submitted by Latchel Service Providers, and this will also surely come with a whole new subset of conditionals and exceptions.
Another solution Latchel implemented that constantly requires an eye for improvement is invoicing service providers. We built it in a way that denies invoices being submitted that are above the approved budget limit, but that is not a solution to getting quality invoices. If an approved budget limit is $500, and the average cost of something like yard work is $200, there is a $300 margin of trust we provide the service provider. So while we “solved” the problem of invoices coming in above the hard limit, we haven’t yet addressed how to more accurately pin point invoice prices and still have plenty of complaints on the overall cost of service providers.  We must handle these complaints with the understanding that we aren’t perfect today, but we’re constantly thinking about how to improve the experience so they don’t have to.

The best way to prepare for this is to **acknowledge the possibilities and discuss these risks with your peers and supervisors as they come up**. This is just an opportunity to more closely tie into Latchel company values of [Continuous Improvement and Transparency](/Company-Wide-Information).

### **Key Guideline 4: Money on the Mind**
Money is not the only answer, but it makes a difference. -Barack Obama
Nothing is more unappetizing as incentive for working with Latchel than not getting paid. We want to take Service Provider financial security incredibly serious, as some Service Providers will be dependent on Latchel to pay them on time and when we say we will. Like with any other services contractor who works for themselves for a living, if you don’t get paid for your services, you’re not going to continue working for the employer/client. This problem is magnified by the fact that most service providers are paying out of pocket for materials, so most jobs immediately put them in the negative and risk of non-payment, or any delay to it, is a risk to their livelihood. **Latchel targets 3-10 day payout times unless there is a prearrangement made with the property manager to shorten/lengthen the processing date.**

The only payment method Latchel supports for Providers in the Latchel Service Provider Network is **Direct Deposit**. For [Latchel Affiliate Service Providers](https://latchel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/LTG/pages/768999595), we only support **credit card**. The type of Service Provider who is more modernized to technology will accept credit cards and direct deposit. Overall, it is not common practice for a service to be paid via direct deposit, even though Direct deposit is a more convenient method for everyone involved, so the potential struggle with sourcing is that a large group of Service Providers only accept cash, or check (paper version of direct deposit).
**In the event a service provider states they are frustrated with an invoicing issue that you can not immediately resolve, escalate to a supervisor immediately.** The longer we don’t fulfill on payment expectations, the higher our risk of losing contractor as a trusted member of our network. The quickest way for a service provider to turn their back on our company is not paying them on time or at all.
There is only one boss. The customer. And he can fire everybody in the company from the chairman on down, simply by spending his money somewhere else. -Sam Walton (Walmart)
