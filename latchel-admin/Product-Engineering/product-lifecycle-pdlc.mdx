## **_Identify Opportunity_**

- **Description**: One of two contenders for the most important aspect of the PDLC. This is where research and analysis are conducted to validate the opportunity and define the scope and scale of the problem to be solved. For the highest-level opportunities, the process begins with one or more company objectives. For smaller and/or follow-up initiatives, background product knowledge and one customer interview may be enough to thoroughly identify the opportunity. 
- **Entry Criteria**: None!
- **Exit Criteria**: A clear, written definition of the problem. This must include (1) a description of where the problem starts and ends (2) a hypothesis of what customer benefits will be achieved by solving this problem. Desired outcomes may be proposed and documented but implementation details must not be included.

## **_Set Goal_**

- **Description**: For larger epics, a SMART goal should be established. For smaller initiatives and follow-up tickets, goals can be stated in terms of verifiable “success criteria” or even a core user story that can be easily validated with one or more customers. 
- **Entry Criteria**: Opportunity Identified
- **Exit Criteria**: A clear, written description of the initiative’s goals. Goals should be clearly aligned with at least one of the roadmap key results. If it takes longer than an hour to determine what a metric target should be, half it or double it!

## **_Discovery_**

- **Description**: This represents the ideation and solutioning phase. For larger epics this might start with a product brief presented to a small group of product, engineering and design representatives. For smaller initiatives this could be done in a less formal setting over several meetings or even conducted in a few minutes in a tech grooming meeting. Consistent throughout is that this phase marks the place where high level user stories are aligned on and tech and/or process solutions are imagined. 
- **Entry Criteria**: Opportunity identified
- **Exit Criteria**: Core user stories are defined. Potential technology and/or process routes are determined to be feasible and complete enough to conduct lower-level grooming. 

## **_Plan_**

- **Description**: The planning phase represents all the steps needed to get the initiative ready and optimized for the engineering process. This could involve several product-design iterations and multiple tech grooming meetings or could be as simple as a product manager spending a few minutes clarifying acceptance criteria following an intake meeting. The product manager should have a fairly decent understanding by this point of what their “assess and learn” plan will look like. 
- **Entry Criteria**: Opportunity identified, goal set
- **Exit Criteria**: P0 user stories and acceptance criteria evaluated as technically feasible, design handoff complete (where applicable), technical tasks created sized and ready for prioritization. At least one “vertical slice” in the spirit of the core user story(ies) can be delivered in \<1 sprint. As much as is reasonable, individual user stories are testable and deployable. The product manager can articulate with a high degree of precision how the ‘asses and learn’ phase will be conducted. 

## **_Build_**

- **Description**: Engineering work required to bring user-stories to real life takes place! Begins at sprint kick-off, ends at production deployment. Engineers design and build. Product managers answer questions and get status updates during standups. Design resources are consulted as needed. Product testing occurs. 
- **Entry Criteria**: All previously listed exit criteria. Prioritized technical tasks. Any product launch and/or assess and learn prep activities are completed. 
- **Exit Criteria**: Technical tasks resulting in customer-facing vertical slices are deployed to production. 

## **_Launch_**

- **Description**: The most important aspect of this stage is communicating to users and stakeholders the (hypothesized) value that was delivered. For larger initiatives this could involve a multi-channel marketing campaign and guided, in-app product tours. For smaller initiatives, ‘good’ might look like a well-crafted slack announcement where understanding of key stakeholders is sought and acknowledged.  
- **Entry Criteria**: A clear, written description of what was delivered, who it impacts and the value it provides
- **Exit Criteria**: Key stakeholders of the release have received the most important information about the release. 

## **_Assess & Learn_**

- **Description**: The only other contender for the most important PDLC phase. Mastering this phase is where we separate ourselves, as a team and company. Done well, this phase is marked by rapid testing of the value-hypothesis initially established during the ‘Identify Opportunity’ phase. Gaps in either the opportunity, hypothesized value, user-story relevance or technical implementation are quickly identified and inserted into the top of the PDLC. 
- **Entry Criteria**: Assess and Learn Plan, new hypothesized value to test
- **Exit Criteria**: Production results are measured against established goals. Documented learnings and next steps are identified OR the opportunity is considered closed
